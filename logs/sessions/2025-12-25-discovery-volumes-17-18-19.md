## Volume 18 Discovery Findings

### 1. Orson Pratt - "Prophecies of Joseph Smith and Their Literal Fulfillment"
**Citation:** JoD 18:222-229
**Date:** August 26, 1876
**Categories:** Failed Prophecy, Civil War, Times of Gentiles
**Song Potential:** ★★★★★

#### KEY FINDING: "Times of Gentiles" Failed Prophecy

**The Claim:**
> "How long will it be before the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled? This is a question I cannot fully answer. It will be in the generation that the angel comes. **Forty-six years have already transpired**, since the Lord sent his Gospel message to the Gentile nations" (JoD 18:226)

**Context:**
- Sermon delivered 1876, 46 years after Church organization (1830)
- Pratt explicitly ties "times of Gentiles" to "the generation that the angel comes"
- Claims this generation would see fulfillment before passing away
- References Luke 21: "Jerusalem shall be trodden down of the Gentiles, until the times of the Gentiles be fulfilled"

**Analysis:**
- As of 2024: 194 years have passed since 1830
- The "generation" of 1830 is long dead
- Jerusalem returned to Jewish control (1967), but "times of Gentiles" not "fulfilled" per LDS eschatology
- Pratt's confidence: "it will be in the generation that the angel comes"
- IRONY: Pratt immediately precedes this with: "In no instance has that man uttered a single prophecy that either has not already been fulfilled, to the very letter, or will not have its fulfillment in the due time of the Lord"

**Song Material:**
- Strong "This Generation Shall Not Pass" theme
- Direct quote from apostle about time-specific prophecy
- Can contrast 1876 expectation with 2024 reality
- Built-in apologetic response: "in the due time of the Lord" (how long is "due time"?)

#### SECONDARY FINDING: All JSJ Prophecies "Literally Fulfilled"

**The Claim:**
> "I have often reflected upon the early prophecies that were given through our Prophet, Joseph Smith...and have often marveled at their literal fulfillment...in no instance has that man uttered a single prophecy that either has not already been fulfilled, to the very letter, or will not have its fulfillment" (JoD 18:222)

**Analysis:**
- Blanket claim about "all" JSJ prophecies
- Falsifiable: multiple failed JSJ prophecies documented
- Examples: Kirtland temple generation, New Jerusalem temple, Civil War ending with slaves freed, etc.
- The hubris of "in no instance" makes this quotable

**Song Angle:** "Every Word...To The Very Letter"

### 2. Wilford Woodruff - "Parable of the Ten Virgins"
**Citation:** JoD 18:109-122
**Date:** September 12, 1875
**Categories:** Race (Lamanites), Three Nephites, Failed Prophecy (wheat storage)
**Song Potential:** ★★★★★

#### KEY FINDING: Lamanite Curse of Dark Skin

**The Claim:**
> "They were cursed by the Almighty, a skin of darkness came upon them...because they rejected the Gospel. In the fourth century of the Christian era they apostatized from the religion of their fathers; they were cursed by the Almighty, a skin of darkness came upon them; they were cursed in all that they set their hands to do, and the withering curse of the Almighty has been upon them from generation to generation" (JoD 18:109)

**Context:**
- Refers to American Indians as descendants of Book of Mormon peoples
- Attributes dark skin directly to divine curse for apostasy
- Claims curse ongoing "from generation to generation, until the present day"
- Describes them as "degraded, fallen and sunk into the very depths of darkness"

**Current LDS Position:**
- 2013: "The Church disavows the theories advanced in the past that black skin is a sign of divine disfavor or curse" (Race and the Priesthood essay)
- While statement focuses on Black Africans, principle applies to all "curse of dark skin" doctrines
- 2006: Gordon B. Hinckley removed "white and delightsome" from 2 Nephi 30:6

**Song Material:**
- Direct quote from apostle/future prophet
- Clear causation: apostasy → dark skin curse
- Now officially disavowed doctrine
- Can juxtapose with modern LDS statements

#### SECONDARY FINDING: Three Nephites Active in 1870s

**The Claims:**
> "Who are these men who have been to the Indians and told them to repent of their sins, and be baptized by the 'Mormons'? They are men who obtained the promise of the Lord, upwards of eighteen centuries ago, that they should be instruments in his hands of bringing about the redemption of their descendants." (JoD 18:110)

> "What for? What do they want with the Elders? They want to be baptized. Who told them to come and be baptized? They say that men came to them in their dreams, and spoke to them in their own language, and told them that away yonder was a people who had authority from God to baptize them" (JoD 18:110)

**Context:**
- Woodruff attributes Indian conversions to ministry of Three Nephites
- Claims they appear "in their dreams" to teach Gospel
- Specific claim: Indians traveling "hundreds of miles" based on these dreams
- Ties to Book of Mormon promise (3 Nephi 28) that Three Nephites would minister "among all the scattered tribes of Israel"

**Analysis:**
- Empirical claim about contemporary events (1870s)
- Attribut es specific religious experiences to specific supernatural beings
- Claims can be fact-checked: Were there such Indian conversions? Were they attributed to dreams?
- Similar to claims about angelic visitations, Cain sightings, etc.

**Song Angle:** "Three Men Who Never Die"
- Combine with other Three Nephites claims across JoD
- Contrast supernatural claims with mundane reality
- Tone: wondrous/mysterious building to ironic reveal

#### TERTIARY FINDING: Wheat Storage Prophecy

**The Claim:**
> "I want to say one word to our farmers before I close. I want to ask you if you ever heard brother Kimball tell about laying up wheat? 'Yes,' say some 'we have heard him, but the famine has not come yet.' No, but it will come...the day will come when, if you do not take this counsel, you will want your wheat for bread...Lay up your wheat; and other provisions against a day of need, for the day will come when they will be wanted, and no mistake about it." (JoD 18:122)

**Context:**
- Heber C. Kimball (d. 1868) had prophesied famine requiring stored wheat
- Woodruff in 1875 confirms and extends prophecy
- Specific, material claim: wheat will be needed for bread due to famine
- Instruction to farmers: store wheat, don't sell it

**Analysis:**
- Time-bound: "the day will come"
- 150 years later: no such famine requiring stored wheat
- Part of larger pattern of imminent judgment prophecies
- Practical impact: economic advice based on failed prophecy

### 4. Daniel H. Wells - "The Blessing of Life for Evermore"
**Citation:** JoD 18:95-102
**Date:** October 7, 1875
**Categories:** Failed Prophecy, Slavery, Civil War
**Song Potential:** ★★★★

#### KEY FINDING: God Commanded US Gov to Free Slaves

**The Claim:**
> "It is not a great while since the word of the Lord came through Joseph Smith, the Prophet of the Lord, to this nation, to free their slaves, and for the Government to pay for them out of the treasury of the United States. Would the people receive the word of the Lord through his servant? No, they would not. What was the result? Why a fratricidal civil war in which thousands of millions of dollars were spent, devastation was spread over the land and rivers of blood were shed, and all this might have been avoided and the slaves liberated by peaceful means at not more than one-tenth of the expense, if they would have hearkened to the word of the Lord." (JoD 18:95-96)

**Context:**
- References D&C 87 (Civil War prophecy, December 1832)
- CLAIMS: Revelation commanded US Gov to purchase slaves' freedom from treasury
- Nation rejected this divine command
- Result: Civil War as divine punishment

**Historical Reality:**
- D&C 87 does NOT command US Gov to buy slaves' freedom
- D&C 87 only predicts war "beginning at South Carolina" and slaves "shall rise up against their masters"
- No revelation exists commanding compensated emancipation
- This is Wells ADDING to the prophecy, or conflating separate JSJ statements

**Analysis:**
- Failed prophecy (no compensated emancipation occurred)
- Revisionist interpretation of Civil War (not divine punishment for rejecting revelation)
- Wells likely conflating JSJ's political views with revelation
- Shows how prophetic claims expand in retelling

**Song Potential:** "The Lord Commanded"
- Great dramatic narrative: God speaks → Nation rejects → War as consequence
- Problem: The command doesn't exist in scripture
- Can use Wells' voice but fact-check against D&C 87
- Apologetic response: JSJ may have spoken this in non-canonical setting

### 5. Erastus Snow - "There is a God"
**Citation:** JoD 19:266-279
**Date:** March 3, 1878
**Categories:** Adam-God, Mother God, Cosmology, Gender Theology
**Song Potential:** ★★★★★

#### KEY FINDING: God Consists of Man and Woman United

**The Claims:**
> "What, do you mean we should understand that Deity consists of man and woman? **Most certainly I do.** If I believe anything that God has ever said about himself, and anything pertaining to the creation and organization of man upon the earth, **I must believe that Deity consists of man and woman.**" (JoD 19:269)

> "There is not in all the eternities that exist, nor ever will be, a God in any other way...a man and a woman; the male and the female." (JoD 19:269)

> "The man is not without the woman, nor the woman without the man in the Lord...and there is no Lord, there is no God in which the two principles are not blended, nor can be." (JoD 19:270)

**The Shears Metaphor:**
> "I sometimes illustrate this matter by taking up a pair of shears...they are necessarily parts, one of another...neither one of them is fitted for the accomplishment of their works alone." (JoD 19:269)

**Context:**
- Extended sermon on nature of God
- Explicitly addresses "God consists of man and woman" as essential doctrine
- Uses analogy: shears (two halves), neither complete without the other
- Ties to Genesis: "male and female created he them, and called their name Adam"
- Anticipates objections: "What! God a man and woman?"

**Current LDS Position:**
- Official doctrine: God the Father is male
- Heavenly Mother exists but is not discussed publicly
- No doctrine that "Deity consists of" both male and female as inseparable unit
- Snow's formulation goes beyond even modern Heavenly Mother theology

**Theological Implications:**
- Challenges traditional monotheism
- Suggests God is composite being (male + female = one God)
- Different from "God has a wife" → "God IS male-female unity"
- Raises questions: Does Heavenly Mother have equal divinity? Equal worship?

**Song Material:** "The Shears of Heaven"
- Use Snow's own metaphor
- Contrast bold 1878 assertions with modern silence
- Address: Why was this taught then, hidden now?
- Tone: Reverent/wondering → puzzled → ironic

**Apologetic Responses to Anticipate:**
- "This was Snow's personal speculation, not official doctrine"
- COUNTER: He says "I must believe" based on scripture, presents as doctrinal necessity
- "This supports Heavenly Mother doctrine"
- COUNTER: Modern HM doctrine doesn't claim God "consists of" male-female unity
- "Language of the time, not meant literally"
- COUNTER: Snow explicitly addresses objections, doubles down on literalism

#### SECONDARY FINDINGS: Spirit/Body Dualism

**Claims:**
> "If the vail were drawn aside, and we could see the spirits of those that once have lived here in the flesh...we should find we could talk with them...How does brother Snow's spirit look when it is disembodied? Why, you just look at me now, and you can answer the question." (JoD 19:275)

> "Our tabernacles are formed for our spirits, yes, expressly for our spirits...they are made to fit the spirits." (JoD 19:275)

**Analysis:**
- Material conception of spirit
- Spirits have tangible form matching physical body
- Tabernacle (body) custom-fitted to spirit
- Continues Mormon materialism: everything is matter

### 7. Joseph F. Smith - "Arrival in Salt Lake City"
**Citation:** JoD 19:187-197
**Date:** September 30, 1877
**Categories:** Polygamy Defense
**Song Potential:** ★★★★

#### KEY FINDING: Extensive Polygamy Apologetic

**The Claims:**
> "'You have dodged the main question; it is polygamy that causes all the trouble!' **Nothing can be more fallacious**; those who assert this only expose their ignorance. The fact is that **since the announcement and practice of that principle by this people, their persecutions have been comparatively trivial** and harmless to what they were, before it was even known to themselves." (JoD 19:190)

> "Has plural marriage deprived any woman of a home, of husband or children? Has it promoted immorality or vice? **No, it has not.**" (JoD 19:190)

> "Can the Elders of this Church be accused of going to the Gentiles for their wives and daughters? No, for **we think we have better ones at home, we have not the least occasion to go abroad.**" (JoD 19:190)

**Context:**
- Joseph F. Smith (future prophet, 1901-1918) defends polygamy in 1877
- Delivered just after Brigham Young's death
- Strong apologetic: polygamy NOT cause of persecution
- Claims polygamy prevents immorality

**Historical Irony:**
- 1890: Wilford Woodruff issues Manifesto ending polygamy
- 1904: Joseph F. Smith (as President) testifies before Congress, distances church from polygamy
- 1904: Second Manifesto, stricter enforcement
- Reed Smoot hearings (1904-1907): Church under intense scrutiny for lingering polygamy

**Analysis:**
- JFS in 1877: Polygamy is not the problem, persecution decreased since announcement
- JFS in 1904: Testifying against polygamy to save Church's reputation
- Document shows evolution of prophetic stance based on political pressure

**Song Potential:** "We Have Better Ones at Home"
- Use JFS's defensive tone
- Juxtapose 1877 defense with 1890 Manifesto, 1904 testimony
- Address: What changed? Divine revelation or political expediency?
- Include: "Has it promoted immorality?" vs. modern understanding

### 8. Erastus Snow - "Origin of Man and Attributes of Deity"
**Citation:** JoD 19:322-329
**Date:** January 20, 1878
**Categories:** Evolution, Anti-Darwin, Polygamy, Becoming Gods
**Song Potential:** ★★★★

#### KEY FINDING: Anti-Evolution Polemic

**The Claim:**
> "Mr. Darwin, and a kindred school of modern philosophers, would fain try to impress upon us their theory of evolutions, and would have us believe we are descendants from, and only a little in advance of our ancestor, the monkey...they fail to demonstrate their theories, simply because they are not demonstrateable." (JoD 19:323)

**Context:**
- Darwin's "Origin of Species" published 1859
- "Descent of Man" published 1871
- Snow speaking 1878, evolution actively debated
- Rejects evolution, affirms special creation
- "Man is only God in embryo" (direct creation by God)

**Current LDS Position:**
- No official stance on evolution
- BYU teaches evolution in biology classes
- 1909 "Origin of Man" statement: Ambiguous, allows for theistic evolution
- 1925, 1931 First Presidency statements: Neutral on mechanism
- Modern members: Mix of creationists and theistic evolutionists

**Evolution of Position:**
- 1878: Snow flatly rejects Darwin
- 1909: First Presidency allows for ambiguity
- 2025: Evolution taught at BYU, widely accepted by educated members

**Song Angle:** "Our Ancestor, the Monkey"
- Use Snow's mocking tone toward Darwin
- Contrast with modern LDS acceptance of evolution (or at least agnosticism)
- Raises question: Was Snow speaking as prophet or man?

#### SECONDARY FINDING: Polygamy Defense via Congress Hypocrisy

**The Claim:**
> "O, you Latter-day Saints, we can't endure you! you will not descend to a level with us we congressmen are after you, we will teach you morals! **No matter how many mistresses you may have, we do not enter any complaint against that phase of it, the bills we have introduced are not intended to prevent your having and keeping as many courtesans as you may choose to, but it is to prohibit your marrying them!**" (JoD 19:329)

**Context:**
- Morrill Anti-Bigamy Act (1862)
- Poland Act (1874)
- Edmunds Act pending (1882)
- Snow uses sarcastic voice of congressmen
- Argument: Polygamy more moral than mistresses

**Analysis:**
- Effective rhetorical device: hypocrisy charge
- BUT: Two wrongs don't make a right
- Also: Assumes congressmen have mistresses (ad hominem)
- Reveals LDS siege mentality re: polygamy

## Methodological Notes

### What Makes a ★★★★★ Finding?

1. **Quotability:** Direct, memorable quotes that stand on their own
2. **Clarity:** Unambiguous claims that can't be explained away easily
3. **Authority:** High-ranking speakers (apostles, future prophets)
4. **Falsifiability:** Can be tested against history, science, or later doctrine
5. **Irony:** The greater the distance between claim and reality, the stronger the material
6. **Documentation:** Multiple sources, corroboration, official publication

### Discovery Agent Checklist (Used for Each Sermon)

For each HIGH priority sermon, I:
1. ✅ Read full text (not just keywords)
2. ✅ Identified BIZARRE/STRIKING quotes
3. ✅ Noted exact JoD citation (volume:page)
4. ✅ Captured speaker, date, location
5. ✅ Assessed song potential (★ to ★★★★★)
6. ✅ Noted categories (prophecy, race, polygamy, etc.)
7. ✅ Identified apologetic responses
8. ✅ Cross-referenced with modern LDS positions
9. ✅ Extracted multiple quotable segments per sermon
10. ✅ Documented context and implications
