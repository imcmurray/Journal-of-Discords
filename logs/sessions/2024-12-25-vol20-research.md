## Volume Character

Volume 20 is from a critical period (1878-1879) when federal anti-polygamy persecution was intensifying. The volume contains:
- **Heavy polygamy defense** (responding to Morrill Act and mounting pressure)
- **Prophetic warnings** against the U.S. government
- **Lamanite/racial theology** (Orson Pratt's extensive cosmology sermon)
- **Persecution narratives** (Haun's Mill, Carthage, Mountain Meadows)
- **Defiant rhetoric** about choosing God's law over man's law

This is a **defensive and combative** volume, with church leaders rallying members during the early days of what would become the "Mormon Underground."

## Notable Patterns

### 1. Polygamy Defense at Peak Intensity

Volume 20 contains the most extensive and explicit polygamy defense in the entire JoD corpus:
- **Salvation requirement** explicitly stated
- **Angel with drawn sword** story told by Joseph F. Smith
- **Congregational votes** to defy federal law
- **Daniel in lion's den** comparison by John Taylor
- **Higher law theology** fully developed

This volume captures the church at the HEIGHT of polygamy commitment, just before the collapse that led to the 1890 Manifesto.

### 2. Apocalyptic Rhetoric

Multiple speakers predict divine judgment on the United States:
- **Orson Hyde**: Cyclones as punishment (his last sermon)
- **John Taylor**: Nation will "crumble to pieces"
- **Franklin D. Richards**: War over polygamy like war over slavery

These prophecies FAILED. The U.S. did not perish. The church abandoned polygamy in 1890.

### 3. Persecution Complex

Heavy emphasis on:
- Haun's Mill massacre
- Carthage Jail murders
- Mountain Meadows Massacre (defensive framing)
- Comparison to early Christian martyrs
- Romans casting Christians to lions

This narrative justified civil disobedience and prepared members for underground activity.

### 4. Constitutional Crisis Theology

Leaders frame the conflict as:
- **Government violating First Amendment** (religious freedom)
- **God's law vs. man's law** (higher law doctrine)
- **LDS as defenders of Constitution** (ironic, since defying federal law)
- **Joseph Smith prophecy** about saving Constitution

This theology persists in modern fundamentalist Mormon groups (FLDS, etc.)
