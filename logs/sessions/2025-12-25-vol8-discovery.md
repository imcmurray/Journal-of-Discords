### Sermon 1: Light of the Spirit—Course of Missionaries (JoD 8:176-182)

**Speaker:** Brigham Young
**Date:** September 9, 1860
**Location:** Bowery and Historian's Office, Great Salt Lake City

#### Context
Instructions to missionaries about to depart on foreign missions. Brigham forbids them from engaging in commerce or bringing home luxury goods. The sermon covers multiple themes: missionary conduct, tithing, speculation, and endowments.

#### KEY FINDING: "Making Devils" Doctrine

**Category:** Sons of Perdition / Apostasy Theology
**Song Potential:** ★★★★★ (EXCEPTIONAL)

**The Quote:**
> "Do you query why we give endowments to A., B., and C? It is to make devils of those who will deny the faith, for that is also necessary, as a host of devils will be needed."

**Full Context:**
> "Brother Cannon remarked that people wondered how many wives and children I had. He may inform them that I shall have wives and children by the million, and glory, and riches, and power, and dominion, and kingdom after kingdom, and reign triumphantly. 'What will you do with all those who have sought to kill you?' Make them soap-boilers and kitchen flunkeys. We are not going to send them into hell fire, for it takes a good Latter-day Saint apostatized to get down into that deep (did I say bottomless?) pit. **A person, to become an angel of the Devil, has first to be a good Saint, and then deny the Lord who bought him.**
>
> **Do you query why we give endowments to A., B., and C? It is to make devils of those who will deny the faith, for that is also necessary, as a host of devils will be needed.** We also want Saints, angels, holy ones, and those that are exalted to the highest glory—we want them to inherit kingdoms that number millions on millions."

**Citation:** JoD 8:181-182

**Analysis:**
- This is an EXPLICIT statement that temple endowments are given INTENTIONALLY to create devils from future apostates
- Presents apostasy as a necessary cosmic function ("a host of devils will be needed")
- States that only "good Latter-day Saints apostatized" can become devils/sons of perdition
- Casual, matter-of-fact tone about creating eternal damnation
- No metaphor, no qualification - stated as doctrine

**Apologetic Challenges:**
1. **"It's metaphorical"** - No indication of metaphor; stated as doctrine alongside other concrete teachings
2. **"Out of context"** - Context makes it WORSE: he's talking about cosmic design and organization
3. **"Speaking as a man"** - He's giving official instructions to missionaries in his capacity as President
4. **Modern disavowal** - Church no longer teaches endowments create devils, so this is doctrinal evolution

**Song Angle:** "A Host of Devils Will Be Needed" - dark humor about cosmic planning requiring devils, with endowments as the devil-creation mechanism

**Song Potential:** ★★★

### Sermon 2: Human Intelligence and Freedom (JoD 8:319-326)

**Speaker:** Brigham Young
**Date:** February 10, 1861
**Location:** Bowery, Great Salt Lake City

#### Context
Civil War is beginning. Brigham reflects on US government, political prophecy, and the martyrdom of Joseph Smith. Heavy political commentary mixed with theological claims.

#### KEY FINDING: Joseph Smith Could Have Saved the Nation

**Category:** Failed Prophecy / Political Messianism
**Song Potential:** ★★★★★

**The Quote:**
> "The nation is ruined, and will crumble to pieces. They will destroy themselves. Joseph rose up and said—'I will save them, if they will let me.' He stepped forth like a man and proffered his services to save the nation that is now breaking; and he would have saved it, if they had permitted him."

**Full Context:**
> "Where is there a nation that acknowledges the Supreme God to be their President and their King? The best form of man-made government upon the earth is that of a nation now breaking to pieces. Have they ever acknowledged God? No. They spurned from their presence the man who would acknowledge that God should reign King of nations as well as King of Saints. Have we proof of this before us? We have. When Joseph Smith rose in the majesty of his calling before God, he saw what we are now hearing of through despatches [dispatches] received from week to week. **The nation is ruined, and will crumble to pieces. They will destroy themselves. Joseph rose up and said—'I will save them, if they will let me.' He stepped forth like a man and proffered his services to save the nation that is now breaking; and he would have saved it, if they had permitted him.** What did they bestow upon him in return? They made a martyr of him..."

**Citation:** JoD 8:319-320

**Analysis:**
- Claims Joseph Smith could have saved the United States from Civil War
- Presents Joseph as political messiah, not just religious prophet
- Nation "is ruined, and will crumble to pieces" - absolute language
- Attribution of divine political power to Joseph
- Reality: US recovered from Civil War and became world superpower

**Apologetic Challenges:**
1. **"Spiritual not political"** - Text explicitly discusses political salvation
2. **"Conditional prophecy"** - No conditions stated; absolute claim
3. **Nation didn't crumble** - US is still standing 164 years later
4. **Joseph's presidency was symbolic** - Brigham treats it as literal divine commission

**Song Angle:** "I Will Save Them (If They Let Me)" - political messianism meets reality

**Song Potential:** ★★★★

### Sermon 3: Gathering—Return to Jackson County (JoD 8:348-351)

**Speaker:** Heber C. Kimball
**Date:** February 17, 1861
**Location:** Tabernacle, Great Salt Lake City

#### Context
Heber C. Kimball preaching about gathering the poor, the role of kings and queens, and the ultimate return to Jackson County, Missouri.

#### KEY FINDING: "I Shall Go to Jackson County"

**Category:** Failed Prophecy
**Song Potential:** ★★★★★

**The Quote:**
> "Brethren, I shall go to Jackson County with thousands of this people who will be faithful to their integrity."

**Full Context:**
> "If you wait for him, or for any of the wicked, to take you back to Jackson County, Missouri, you will have to wait some millions of years. And if we should wait for the rotten-hearted kings and queens of the wicked nations to gather us home, we shall have to wait a long time. Possibly some of them may come and look at the place, but they will never come to stay and assist in building up Zion. Many of them will yet drive the Saints from their lands and homes, just as the wicked have driven us from the United States into these mountains of Deseret.
>
> Then who is to gather the people of God? You all say that we are to become a kingdom of kings and priests—of queens and priestesses; and the Bible supports this doctrine. Now, the truth is, you are the very kings and priests that have got to gather the Saints, and your wives have got to school them and nurse them...
>
> **Brethren, I shall go to Jackson County with thousands of this people who will be faithful to their integrity**; but we cannot go back until we have built some good houses."

**Citation:** JoD 8:348-350

**Analysis:**
- First-person prophecy: "I SHALL go"
- Not "we might" or "if the Lord wills" - absolute statement
- Specifies "thousands" will go with him
- Heber died in 1868, never returned to Jackson County
- LDS Church abandoned Jackson County dreams in practice

**Apologetic Challenges:**
1. **"Resurrection fulfillment"** - He said "I SHALL go" in present life context
2. **"Conditional prophecy"** - No conditions stated
3. **"Eventually will happen"** - 163 years later, still waiting
4. **Doctrine & Covenants 57** - Joseph Smith received revelation designating Jackson County as Zion's center lot

**Song Angle:** "I Shall Go to Jackson County" - perfect title, speaks for itself

**Song Potential:** High - combines well with Jackson County prophecy for militant edge

### Sermon 4: Hints to Faultfinders (JoD 8:11-19)

**Speaker:** Brigham Young
**Date:** March 5, 1860
**Location:** Tabernacle, Great Salt Lake City

#### Context
Long sermon defending Brigham's authority against critics. Discusses faith vs. belief, Joseph Smith's character, and why he doesn't care about accusations against Joseph.

#### KEY FINDING: Joseph Could Steal Horses Daily, Doctrine Still True

**Category:** Prophetic Defense / Faith vs. Works
**Song Potential:** ★★★★

**The Quote:**
> "I know that the doctrine he preaches is the power of God to my salvation, if I live it. I did not make him a revelator; I have no business to dictate him. I never called him in question, even in my feelings, for an act of his, except once."

**Full Context:**
> "I recollect that while on my way to Ohio, to see brother Joseph the first time, I took dinner with a Mr. Gillmore—I think a Methodist priest. He began to tell me the character of Joseph Smith, what he had been guilty of, how long he had been a money-digger, how long a horse-jockey, and how many horses he had stolen; and his statement made Joseph to be some seventy or eighty years of age. I said to him, **'Joseph Smith I never saw. He says that he has received revelations from God, and declared that an angel visited him. He has declared that he found plates, and other witnesses have seen and handled them, from which the Book of Mormon was translated. I know nothing about these witnesses, neither do I care. I went to my Father in heaven and asked him with regard to the truth of the doctrines taught by Joseph Smith, and I know they will save all that will hearken to them, and that those who do not will miss salvation in the celestial kingdom of God; and though Joseph Smith should steal horses every day, or gamble every night, or deny his Saviour from the crowing of the cock in the morning until sunset in the evening, I know that the doctrine he preaches is the power of God to my salvation, if I live it.** I did not make him a revelator; I have no business to dictate him. I never called him in question, even in my feelings, for an act of his, except once."

**Citation:** JoD 8:15-16

**Analysis:**
- Extreme hypothetical: daily horse theft, nightly gambling, denying Christ
- Separates doctrine from prophet's behavior
- Presents as a virtue the refusal to question the prophet
- Modern problem: How do you distinguish true from false prophets if behavior doesn't matter?
- Shows blind obedience culture

**Apologetic Challenges:**
1. **"Hypothetical argument"** - Yes, but reveals Brigham's actual epistemology
2. **"Defending Joseph's character"** - Actually doing opposite: saying character irrelevant
3. **"Follow the doctrine not the man"** - But who validates the doctrine if not the man?

**Song Angle:** "Steal Horses Every Day" - exploring the logic of prophetic infallibility

### Sermon 5: Duties of the Saints (JoD 8:338-347)

**Speaker:** Brigham Young
**Date:** January 20, 1861
**Location:** Tabernacle, Great Salt Lake City

#### Context
Sermon on stewardship, taxation, tithing, and property ownership. Brigham defends territorial taxes while explaining theology of stewardship.

#### KEY FINDING: Sell Every House for Taxes

**Category:** Economic Coercion / Taxation
**Song Potential:** ★★★★

**The Quote:**
> "I tell you what I say to tax-gatherers: I would sell every improvement, every ox, cow, mule, horse, sheep, hog, &c., but what I would have the taxes in the kinds prescribed by law."

**Full Context:**
> "But what I dislike most is, that when the officer requests the taxes, some will lie from morning until night to escape paying them... I tell you what I say to tax-gatherers: **I would sell every improvement, every ox, cow, mule, horse, sheep, hog, &c., but what I would have the taxes in the kinds prescribed by law.** You may call that hard; but what would the cash portion be, compared to the money that is paid to these merchants? This is what I do not like. Go to a man, and he will declare that he cannot pay his taxes; then go into his house, and he has taught his family to lie; but begin to sell his cow, &c., and it will be, 'Stop, Sally; go and bring out that old stocking.' I have proved this. That is what I do not like."

**Citation:** JoD 8:344

**Analysis:**
- Authoritarian approach to tax collection
- Threatens total property seizure
- Accuses people of teaching families to lie
- "I have proved this" - suggests he's done it
- Shows economic control over community

**Apologetic Challenges:**
1. **"Legal taxation"** - Yes, but extreme enforcement approach
2. **"Fighting dishonesty"** - By threatening to sell all property?
3. **"Separation of church/state"** - Brigham was both religious and political leader

**Song Potential:** High - shows authoritarian control, economic coercion

#### SECONDARY FINDING: Divorce Fees as Revenue

**Category:** Economic Practice / Temple Ordinances
**Song Potential:** ★★★★★

**The Quote:**
> "I have told them, from the beginning, that sealing men and women for time and all eternity is one of the ordinances of the house of God, and that I never wanted a farthing for sealing them, nor for officiating in any of the ordinances of God's house; but when you ask for a bill of divorce, I intend that you shall pay for it. That keeps me in spending money, besides enabling me to give hundreds of dollars to the poor."

**Full Context:**
> "Are you afraid that I will make a bad use of it? I have plenty of money for my private use. You may wish to know how I get it. I believe I will tell you how I get some of it. A great many of these Elders of Israel, soon after courting these young ladies, and old ladies, and middle-aged ladies, and having them sealed to them, want to have a bill of divorce. **I have told them, from the beginning, that sealing men and women for time and all eternity is one of the ordinances of the house of God, and that I never wanted a farthing for sealing them, nor for officiating in any of the ordinances of God's house; but when you ask for a bill of divorce, I intend that you shall pay for it. That keeps me in spending money, besides enabling me to give hundreds of dollars to the poor**, and buy butter, eggs, and little notions for women and children, and otherwise use it where it does good."

**Citation:** JoD 8:345-346

**Analysis:**
- Temple sealings are free
- Divorces cost money
- Brigham uses divorce fees as personal revenue stream
- Casual admission of monetizing sacred ordinances (negatively)
- Shows economic incentive structure around polygamous marriages
- "Keeps me in spending money" - remarkably candid

**Apologetic Challenges:**
1. **"Uses it for charity"** - Also admits using for personal spending money
2. **"Discourages frivolous divorce"** - Economic barrier to ending bad marriages
3. **"Personal income vs. church income"** - Blurs the line

**Song Potential:** EXCEPTIONAL - incredibly candid about monetizing temple ordinances through divorce fees. Perfect for exploring economic side of polygamy.
