## SUMMARY

Volume 3 (March-June 1856) represents the **REFORMATION CRISIS** period - an extraordinary window into early Mormon extremism during a time of famine and internal purges. This volume contains some of the most violent, authoritarian, and disturbing rhetoric in the entire Journal of Discourses.

**Context**: 1855-56 drought, grasshopper plague, severe winter. Leadership response: intensive

 religious reformation, public shaming, threats of violence, and blood atonement rhetoric.

**Key Themes**:
- Blood atonement and execution rhetoric
- Extreme authoritarianism and obedience demands
- Violent threats against dissenters and "corrupt" members
- Bishops caught stealing tithing
- Courts and lawyers cursed from the pulpit
- Theocratic governance demands
- Anti-intellectual warnings about visions

## CONTEXTUAL PATTERNS

### Reformation Period Characteristics:
1. **Food scarcity used for control** - famine as divine punishment
2. **Violent rhetoric escalates** - blood atonement becomes mainstream
3. **Surveillance increases** - bishops searching homes
4. **Purges threatened** - lawyers, judges, apostates targeted
5. **Obedience obsession** - constant demands for submission
6. **Anti-legalism** - rejection of courts, lawyers, due process

### Speaker Analysis:
- **Brigham Young**: Most violent rhetoric, blood atonement, bishop corruption exposure
- **Jedediah M. Grant**: Direct murder threats, "unsheath the sword"

- **George A. Smith**: Milk strippings story, practical advice mixed with control
- **Amasa Lyman**: Tithing-based salvation, transactional theology
- **Orson Pratt**: Anti-intellectualism despite being most educated apostle
