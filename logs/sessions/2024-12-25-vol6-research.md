## High Priority Sermons (Analysis Ready)

### 1. Character and Being of God, etc. (JoD 6:1-11)
**Speaker:** Joseph Smith
**Date:** April 6, 1844 (Nauvoo)
**Pages:** 1-11
**Category:** Cosmology, Adam-God doctrine
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ HIGHEST
**Song Potential:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ TIER 1

**Context:** King Follett funeral discourse, delivered 2 months before Joseph Smith's death. One of the most significant and controversial sermons in LDS history.

**Key Quotes:**

> "God himself was once as we are now, and is an exalted Man, and sits enthroned in yonder heavens. That is the great secret."

> "I am going to tell you how God came to be God. We have imagined and supposed that God was God from all eternity, I will refute that idea, and will take away and do away the vail, so that you may see."

> "God himself the Father of us all, dwelt on an earth, the same as Jesus Christ himself did"

> "you have got to learn how to be Gods yourselves, and to be kings and priests to God, the same as all Gods have done before you"

> "In the beginning, the head of the Gods called a council of the Gods; and they came together and concocted a plan to create the world and people it."

> "The intelligence of spirits had no beginning, neither will it have an end...There never was a time when there were not spirits; for they are co-equal with our Father in heaven."

**Problematic Claims:**
1. God was once a mortal man on another earth
2. Multiple Gods exist in a council hierarchy
3. Humans can become Gods through progression
4. Intelligence/spirits are co-eternal with God (not created)
5. Matter is eternal, not created ex nihilo

**Apologetic Challenges:**
- Modern LDS Church has distanced from these teachings
- Conflicts with traditional Christian monotheism
- "As man is, God once was; as God is, man may become" became controversial slogan
- Church essay on "Becoming Like God" softens but doesn't repudiate

**Song Development Notes:**
- Rich source material with direct, quotable statements
- Historical significance (funeral discourse, final major sermon)
- Clear doctrinal shift from later LDS positions
- Multiple angles: polytheism, eternal progression, pre-mortal existence

### 2. Polygamy (JoD 6:349-364)
**Speaker:** Orson Pratt
**Date:** July 24, 1859 (Great Salt Lake City)
**Pages:** 349-364 (15 pages)
**Category:** Polygamy
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ HIGH
**Song Potential:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ TIER 1

**Context:** Major defensive sermon on polygamy during period of federal opposition. Comprehensive Biblical, theological, and constitutional argument for plural marriage.

**Key Quotes:**

> "If polygamy be a crime—if it can be proved to be a crime by the law of God, then the inhabitants of this Territory, so far as this one institution is concerned, are in an awful condition"

> "Let us inquire whether, indeed, plurality of wives ever was sanctioned by the God of heaven—whether he himself is the Author of it, or whether he barely permitted it as a crime"

> "The Lord approbated polygamy, and gave laws regulating the descent of property in the polygamic families"

> "Jacob and Leah were one flesh, Leah being his first wife. Jacob and Rachel were one flesh. Jacob and Bilhah were one flesh. Jacob and Zilpah were one flesh; and if he had had a thousand more, it would have been the same"

> "Every individual among the Latter-day Saints...have practically embraced this doctrine, believing it to be a Divine institution, approbated of God and the Bible"

> "Point out in the Bible where polygamy is a crime, and then you may say we have no right to embrace it as a part of our religious creed"

**On Marriage for Eternity:**

> "The marriage covenant is indissoluble; it is everlasting; it is not limited to time; but it is a covenant to exist while eternity exists"

> "Adam and Eve...were married as immortal beings...If there had been no sin, there would have been no death. If Adam and Eve never had sinned, they would have been alive on the earth at this time"

**On Constitutional Rights:**

> "We ask no rights that are not guaranteed unto us by the American Constitution...certain rights belonging to every religious sect...every sect has a right to claim them, if they should have to do it at the point of the sword"

**Problematic Claims:**
1. Polygamy is divinely commanded, not merely permitted
2. Biblical patriarchs were blessed for polygamy
3. Marriage for eternity requires plural wives for exaltation
4. Women without opportunity to marry should become plural wives
5. Constitutional right to practice polygamy as religious freedom

**Apologetic Challenges:**
- Official Declaration 1890 ended polygamy
- Church now teaches monogamy as divine standard
- Fundamentalist groups still practice based on these teachings
- Creates cognitive dissonance: was it commanded or sinful?

**Song Development Notes:**
- Extensive material (15 pages) to mine
- Constitutional arguments now abandoned
- "Awful condition" quote powerful for contrast
- Marriage for eternity doctrine still taught, but not polygamy
- Clear before/after doctrinal shift

### 3. Extensive Character of the Gospel, etc. (JoD 6:283-298)
**Speaker:** Brigham Young
**Date:** August 15, 1852 (Great Salt Lake City)
**Pages:** 283-298 (15 pages)
**Category:** Salvation, Degrees of Glory, Temple Work
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ HIGH
**Song Potential:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ TIER 1

**Context:** Major theological discourse on salvation, the nature of God's names, degrees of glory, and temple work for the dead. Contains unique doctrinal claims.

**Key Quotes on God's Names:**

> "for Endless is my name. Wherefore, eternal punishment is God's punishment. Endless punishment is God's punishment."

> "it is not written that there shall be no end to this torment; but it is written, Endless torment...Eternal damnation...for it is meet unto you to know, even as mine Apostles"

**On Universal Salvation:**

> "every man shall be judged according to his works, and every man will receive according to the extent of his capacity"

> "Every faithful Methodist that has lived up to and faithfully fulfilled the requirements of his religion...will have as great a heaven as he ever anticipated in the flesh, and far greater"

> "Every Presbyterian, and every Quaker, and every Baptist, and every Roman Catholic member...that lives according to the best light they have...will have and enjoy all they live for"

**On Degrees of Glory:**

> "The glory of the telestial world no man knows, except he partakes of it...it is so great and so exquisite that it is altogether beyond mortal perception"

> "The terrestrial glory is greater still, and the celestial is the greatest of all; that is the glory of God the Father, where our Lord Jesus Christ reigns"

**On Temple Work and Spirits:**

> "About the time that the Temples of the Lord will be built and Zion is established...there will be strangers in your midst, walking with you, talking with you: they will enter into your houses and eat and drink with you"

> "brothers Joseph and Hyrum Smith, for instance; for they will be perfectly capable of coming and staying over night with you, and you not know who they are"

> "They will expound the Scriptures to you, and open your minds, and teach you of the resurrection of the just and the unjust, of the doctrine of salvation"

**On Sons of Perdition:**

> "he will bring them forth, when they have suffered his wrath according to the deeds done in the body...except the sons of perdition...they are all in the hands of the Saviour, and he has redeemed them all"

> "Every person has the privilege of retaining it there [Lamb's Book of Life] for ever and ever. If they neglect that privilege, then their names will be erased, and not till then"

**Problematic Claims:**
1. "Endless" and "Eternal" are names of God, not descriptions of duration
2. Nearly universal salvation (all except sons of perdition)
3. People of all faiths receive glory based on their faithfulness
4. Spirits of dead prophets will physically visit Saints in temples
5. Temple ordinances can save the dead retroactively

**Apologetic Challenges:**
- "Endless/Eternal as names" teaching not emphasized in modern church
- Universal salvation conflicts with traditional evangelical Christianity
- Spirits physically visiting and eating/drinking is not current teaching
- Creates questions about necessity of Mormonism if all faithful are saved

**Song Development Notes:**
- Long sermon with multiple angles
- "Endless is my name" is unique and quotable
- Universal salvation theme interesting contrast to evangelical views
- Spirit visitation claims are concrete and falsifiable
- Mix of still-taught doctrines and abandoned ones

### 4. Shedding Blood—God's Provision for His Saints (JoD 6:50-52)
**Speaker:** Heber C. Kimball
**Date:** November 15, 1857 (Great Salt Lake City)
**Pages:** 50-52
**Category:** Blood Atonement (mild)
**Priority:** ⭐⭐⭐ MEDIUM
**Song Potential:** ⭐⭐ LOW-MEDIUM

**Context:** Utah War period (1857-58), federal troops approaching Utah. Sermon ostensibly about blood but actually focuses on self-reliance and provision.

**Key Quotes:**

> "If this people will live up to their profession...we shall never be under the necessity of shedding much of the blood of our enemies. You have heard me say often that I do not believe God designs that we should delight in shedding blood."

> "When the time comes that we have need to shed blood, then it will be necessary we should do it, and it will be just as innocent as to go and kill an ox when we are hungry or in the time of famine."

> "Will our enemies come here? No, except we let them. God gives us that privilege. We have the right to let them in here or keep them out; and we choose to keep them out"

**Analysis:**
Despite the provocative title, this sermon contains very little explicit blood atonement doctrine. The "shedding blood" references are:
1. Defense against enemies (Utah War context)
2. Killing for food (not sinful if necessary)
3. General defensive warfare language

The bulk of the sermon is about:
- Self-reliance and cultivating gardens
- Not waiting for God to miraculously provide
- Sending militia to "hedge up" enemy's way
- Working to save handcart pioneers

**Song Development Notes:**
- Title is more provocative than content
- Could be used for context/comparison with more explicit blood atonement sermons in other volumes
- Historical context (Utah War) makes it defensive rather than doctrinal
- **Recommendation:** Low priority unless paired with stronger blood atonement material from Vol 4
