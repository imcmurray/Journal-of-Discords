## HIGH PRIORITY Findings

### 1. "The Kingdom of God" - Brigham Young (JoD 2:, July 8, 1855) ★★★★★
**Category:** Violence/Threats, Political Defiance
**Song Potential:** VERY HIGH

**Key Quotes:**
- "If men come here and do not behave themselves, they will not only find the Danites, whom they talk so much about, biting the horses' heels, but **the scoundrels will find something biting their heels**."
- "In my plain remarks, I merely call things by their right names. Brother Kimball is noted for doing that, **I will do the same on this occasion. I shall call criminals by their own names, when I see proper**, to do so."
- "**I want to say to you reporters, that write for the papers, that when miserable scoundrels come among this people, and do the things that they are doing, we have men here that will lay judgment to the line and righteousness to the plummet**."
- "**If you say it is right, raise your hands. [All hands up.] Let us call upon the Lord to assist us in this, and every good work**."

**Wait, What?**
Direct violent threats with congregational approval. Brigham Young explicitly threatens "scoundrels" with violence ("biting their heels"), calls for "judgment to the line" (execution), and gets unanimous vote of support. This is theocratic vigilantism endorsed by prophet and congregation.

**Apologetic Challenge:**
- Context: Speaking about soldiers seducing women
- Modern disavowal: Church now teaches separation of church/state
- Violence: Explicit threats incompatible with "peacemaker" narrative

### 2. "Utah Delegate to Washington, etc." - Brigham Young (JoD 2:, June 17, 1855) ★★★★★
**Category:** Violence/Threats, Women/Sexual Politics
**Song Potential:** VERY HIGH

**Key Quotes:**
- "I do not know what I shall say next winter, if such men make their appearance here, as were some last winter. I know what I think I shall say, if they play the same game again, **let the women be ever so bad, so help me God, we will slay them**."
- "If any wish to go to California to whore it, we will send a company of them off; that is my mind, and perhaps some few ought to go, for they are indeed bad enough."
- "**If they want women to go to California with them, we will send a company of the same stripe, if they can be found, and then both parties will be suited to and for each other. I would rather follow her to the grave, and send her home pure, than suffer my daughter to be prostituted**."
- "**Write this to the States, if you please. If there are any Gentiles or hickory 'Mormons' here, and so disposed, write it down and send it to Washington, that if they send their officers and soldiers here, to conduct themselves as they did last winter, they shall meet upon the spot the due reward of their crimes**."

**Wait, What?**
Brigham Young threatens to KILL men who seduce Mormon women, threatens to kill "bad" women who want to leave, and explicitly tells reporters to publish his threats to Washington. This is pre-Mountain Meadows Massacre (1857) evidence of violent rhetoric becoming policy.

**Apologetic Challenge:**
- Violence against women: "I would rather follow her to the grave" = murder threat
- Vigilante justice: "meet upon the spot the due reward" = lynch mob
- Publicity: "Write this to the States" = intentional threat, not heat-of-moment

### 3. "Consecration" - Brigham Young (JoD 2:, June 3, 1855) ★★★★
**Category:** Coercion/Control, Economic Manipulation
**Song Potential:** HIGH

**Key Quotes:**
- "They would say, 'I have a cow that will kick a person's hat off, we will consecrate her,' or, '**I have a horse that is foundered, and I will consecrate him, or the horse with the pole evil, or fistula, and he is not worth his keeping, and if we consecrate him, we will get rid of him**.' And as for old sore-backed horses, old wagons, and every other old consumptive trash, that would be consecrated."
- "**Says one, 'I have got a cow I wish you to buy.' 'What do you want for it?' 'I want thirty-five dollars.' 'Well, that cow has just kicked my little child, and she has just hooked a little girl, and she is mischievous in the dairy; but I would not take thirty-five dollars for her, and now I will consecrate her'**."
- "There would be persons, when they paid their Tithing, give in a piece of property worth one hundred dollars, and it takes the Tithing Office six months to get the thing valued before a person can get his money for it."

**Wait, What?**
Brigham Young admits that people tried to dump worthless property as "consecration" rather than real sacrifice. This contradicts the idealized narrative of voluntary consecration. Shows economic coercion and manipulation in early Utah economy.

**Apologetic Challenge:**
- Admission of fraud: People gaming the consecration system
- Economic pressure: Tithing office delays and property valuations
- Modern relevance: Echoes modern tithing pressure tactics

### 4. "The Constitution and Government of the United States, etc." - Brigham Young (JoD 2:, Feb 18, 1855) ★★★★
**Category:** Political/Theocracy, Anti-Democracy
**Song Potential:** HIGH

**Key Quotes:**
- "The signers of the Declaration of Independence and the framers of the Constitution were inspired from on high to do that work. But **was that which was given to them perfect, not susceptible of any addition whatever? No; for if men know anything, they must know that the Almighty has never yet found a man in mortality that was capable, at the first intimation, at the first impulse, to receive anything in a state of entire perfection**."
- "**How much more intelligence, goodness, and purity the Government of the United States has manifested over the Government of any other nation, just so much more it is cursed**."
- "I know their design is to trample upon us in every respect they can; to do away the last vestige of every right, every privilege, and every franchise upon which a human being can stand."

**Wait, What?**
Brigham Young claims the Constitution is inspired but imperfect, and that the MORE righteous the US government is, the MORE cursed it is. This is anti-American rhetoric cloaked in praise. Sets up theocratic alternative.
