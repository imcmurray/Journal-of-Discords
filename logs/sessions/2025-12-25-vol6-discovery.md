### Sermon #2: "The Work of Reformation" - Orson Hyde (JoD 6:11-18)
**Date:** 1857
**Status:** COMPLETED
**Song Potential:** ★★★★ HIGH

**Category:** Failed Prophecy, Theocracy, Violence

**Key Findings:**

1. **Prophetic Boast (Immediately Falsified)**
   - Quote: "And the prayers of all the Saints throughout the world should be to that effect; for they wage war against the kingdom of God, and have fallen upon that stone with an army; and let them be broken, even according to the words of Jesus."
   - Quote: "I do believe that God is about to come out of his hiding-place and to vex the nation according to his word through the martyred Joseph."
   - Quote: "Do I believe that the United States will be divided? Yes, I do"
   - Context: Utah War (1857-58) - predicting divine destruction of US Army
   - **Actual Outcome:** Utah War ended peacefully in 1858 with pardon, no divine intervention, US remained united until Civil War for completely different reasons

2. **Supernatural Army Defeat Claims**
   - Quote: "If the army now invading Utah should be overthrown, are you not fearful that a much larger one will be sent to chastise you? No, sir; I am not. If we honour our God by keeping his law, no army of this world can ever prevail against us"
   - Quote: "If the Red Sea be not the trap in which the enemy will be caught, there will be a snow or hailstorm, a whirlwind, an earthquake, fire from above or from beneath, or the sword of the Lord and of Brigham"
   - Context: Expecting miraculous deliverance like biblical accounts
   - **Irony:** Army simply camped nearby, no miracles occurred, peaceful settlement reached

3. **Violent Prayer Language**
   - Quote: "We do not desire to shed the blood of our enemies...But our prayers should be, that the power and strength arrayed against us may be broken by the providence of God...that they may be wasted away like the early frosts, and be scattered about like chaff before the wind, until...there shall not be a potsherd of them left"
   - Quote: "A mighty host may gather against us; but if God be with us, we may have a seven months' job in burying their dead; and their weapons of war may furnish us fuel for years"
   - **Tone:** Gleeful anticipation of mass slaughter

4. **Missionaries Recalled as Sign of Judgment**
   - Quote: "These missionaries and labourers are now called home. The Lord says, 'It is enough.' 'Come out of her, my people,' is now the voice of God to his servants in every land and nation...After your testimony cometh wrath and indignation upon the people"
   - Quote: "I will take the fulness of my Gospel from among the Gentiles"
   - Context: Interpreting missionary recall as sign of imminent divine judgment on world
   - **Irony:** Missionaries resumed shortly after, no apocalypse occurred

5. **Theocratic Absolutism**
   - Quote: "Govern Utah who will, Brigham Young or his duly chosen successor can alone govern the 'Mormons.'"
   - Quote: "The army sent by the President...Has that army a chaplain Prophet that can obtain the word of the Lord for them, or one whose prayers the Lord hears? We say no"
   - **Implications:** Rejection of federal authority in favor of prophetic rule

**Song Potential Notes:**
- Failed prophecy is clear and documented
- Seven months burying enemy dead = specific, falsifiable prediction
- Weapons for fuel = vivid, specific imagery
- Could contrast prophetic confidence with mundane historical outcome
- Title idea: "Seven Months to Bury Them" or "The Army That Never Came"

### Sermon #3: "The Kingdom of God or Nothing" - John Taylor (JoD 6:18-27)
**Date:** November 1, 1857
**Status:** COMPLETED
**Song Potential:** ★★★★★ HIGHEST

**Category:** Theocracy, Violence, Inflammatory Language

**Key Findings:**

1. **Text from "Gospel of Brigham"**
   - Quote: "In the Second Epistle and last verse of the Gospel according to St. Brigham to Colonel Alexander, will be found the following words:—'WE SAY IT IS THE KINGDOM OF GOD OR NOTHING.'"
   - **Bizarre:** Treating Brigham's letter as scripture ("Gospel according to St. Brigham")

2. **Kingdom = Church AND State**
   - Quote: "Was the kingdom that the Prophets talked about, that should be set up in the latter times, going to be a Church? Yes. And a State? Yes, it was going to be both Church and State, to rule both temporarily and spiritually."
   - Quote: "When the millennium...is introduced, all potentates, powers, and authorities—every man, woman, and child will be in subjection to the kingdom of God; they will be under the power and dominion of the Priesthood of God"
   - **Implications:** Explicit theocratic vision

3. **God Owns Everything**
   - Quote: "Who made this earth? The Lord...Who feeds and clothes the millions of the human family...Who sets up the kings, emperors, and potentates that rule and govern the universe? The Lord."
   - Quote: "And who is there that acknowledges his hand? Where is the nation, the people, the church even, or other power that does it? You may wander east, west, north, and south, and you cannot find it in any church or government on the earth, except the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints."
   - **Tone:** Arrogant exclusivism

4. **Violent, Inflammatory Rhetoric**
   - Quote: "Shall we lie down and let those scoundrels cut our throats?...Shall we untie our neckcloths and tell them to come on and cut and carve away as they please...to prostitute our women and lay low our best men?"
   - Quote: "Having been forced from the United States...after all this, and more, I say, shall we suffer those poor, miserable, damned, infernal scoundrels to come here and infringe upon our sacred rights?" [Congregation: "NO!" making walls tremble]
   - Quote: "We are the Saints of God; we have the kingdom of God, and the devils in hell and all the wicked men on the earth cannot take it from us"
   - **Tone:** Violent, apocalyptic, defiant

5. **Mocking US Soldiers**
   - Quote: "Who would be afraid of a poor, miserable soldier—a man that gets eight dollars a month for killing people, and a miserable butcher at that...Mean as the Americans are, they will not, many of them, hire for soldiers. But the Government must hire foreigners for eight dollars a month to come out here to kill us! Who is afraid of them?"
   - **Tone:** Contemptuous

6. **Death is Nothing**
   - Quote: "Are you not afraid of being killed? No. Great conscience! who cares about being killed? They cannot kill you...Who would be afraid?...Let them come on or stay and wiggle, it is all right."
   - **Implications:** Martyrdom rhetoric

**Song Potential Notes:**
- "Gospel according to St. Brigham" is inherently satirical
- Kingdom = Church + State is theocratic smoking gun
- Walls trembling with "NO!" = dramatic imagery
- "Eight dollar soldiers" = memorable contempt
- Title idea: "The Gospel According to Brigham" or "Kingdom or Nothing"

2. **WOMEN WHORING = BLOOD ATONEMENT** (★★★★★)

   - **Category:** Blood Atonement for sexual sin
   - **Tone:** Matter-of-fact about killing

3. **VIOLENCE & CLEANSING RHETORIC** (★★★★★)
   - Quote: "We shall clean the platter of all such scoundrels; and if men and women will not live their religion, but take a course to pervert the hearts of the righteous, we will 'lay judgment to the line and righteousness to the plummet,' and we will let you know that the earth can swallow you up, as it did Korah with his host; and as brother Taylor says, you may dig your graves, and we will slay you, and you may crawl into them."
   - Quote: "If you grow as you ought, five years will not pass away before your lips will be five times as thick as they are now...and his [Joseph's] countenance was as white as the whitest thing you ever saw."
   - **Implications:** Violent purification; racial undertones re: thick lips

4. **CURSING THE PRESIDENT** (★★★★)
   - Quote: "I feel, in the name and by the authority of Jesus Christ and my calling, to curse that man that lifts his heel against my God and his cause and kingdom; and the curse of God shall be upon him: the angels of God shall chase him, and he shall have no peace. The President of the United States and his coadjutors that have caused this thing shall never rest again, for they shall go to hell."
   - **Category:** Prophetic curse

5. **BIZARRE: EARTH IS ALIVE & PRODUCES LIKE WOMAN** (★★★)
   - Quote: "Do you believe that a dead woman can conceive from a live man and bring forth a live child? Do you believe it, any of you mothers?...Well, if a woman will not produce when she is dead, then the earth cannot produce living things if it was dead."
   - Quote: "Does the earth conceive? It does, and it brings forth...Where did the earth come from? From its parent earths."
   - **Category:** Cosmology - bizarre folk theology

6. **DOUGLAS INSULT** (★★★★)
   - Quote: "They have been sitting on Ham's Fork so long, it has begun to ulcerate, as that nasty fop, Douglas, uses the term—that little nasty snot-nose: you cannot call him anything half so mean as he is—the nastiest of all nasties that God could suffer on the earth."
   - Context: Stephen Douglas, Senator, presidential candidate
   - **Tone:** Shockingly crude from church leader

7. **DEVIL IN SMART BODIES** (★★★)
   - Quote: "You will not catch a mean, low, inferior, stupid devil in a smart man. I will tell you the Devil has his smart men. Says he, 'You get into a smart body.' Smart spirits do not get into inferior bodies. Would you? No."
   - Quote: "Was not Lucifer a pretty smart lad? Just look at it—son of the morning—when all heaven wept when he fell."

**Song Potential Notes:**
- "None of your business whether it is right or wrong" = STUNNING quote
- "We will slay both men and women" for sexual sin = blood atonement clarity
- "Dig your graves and we will slay you" = chilling specificity
- "Snot-nose Douglas" = crude vulgarity from apostle
- "Dead woman can't conceive" earth theology = bizarre
- Multiple song possibilities from single sermon
- Titles: "None of Your Business," "We Will Slay Them," "Dig Your Graves"

### Sermon #5: "Source of True Happiness" - Brigham Young (JoD 6:39-47)
**Date:** November 15, 1857
**Status:** COMPLETED
**Song Potential:** ★★★★ HIGH

**Category:** Prayer/Unity, Failed Prophecy, Authority

**Key Findings:**

1. **PRAYER MUST BE UNIFIED** (★★★★)
   - Quote: "When a man opens or closes a meeting with prayer, every man, woman, and child in the congregation who professes to be a Saint should have no desire or words in their hearts and mouths but what are being offered by the man who is mouth for all the congregation"
   - Quote: "How many times have I attended prayer-meetings among the Methodists, in my youthful days, when perhaps one hundred men and women would all be praying aloud at once? I did not then know but that it was all right"
   - Quote: "Take ten men whose hearts, when they pray, are upon one sentence and upon one idea at a time...do you think that the powers of hell can hinder what they ask for? No"
   - Context: Criticism of independent prayer
   - **Irony:** Controlling even private thoughts during prayer

2. **BRIGHAM CRITICIZES HIS OWN PEOPLE'S PRAYERS** (★★★★)
   - Quote: "How many times have I said here, within the last three months, I pray that God would so lead us and our enemies that there will be no blood shed? And how many have come to meeting and prayed in their hearts that 'our enemies would come on, for we want to slay them'"
   - Quote: "One of the brethren prayed in camp that the snow might fall 40 feet deep on our enemies. I am satisfied if it falls only four or five feet deep."
   - Quote: "Go into meetings, and you may hear thirty different prayers, if there are so many offered up, for everything but what I tell them to pray for"
   - **Tone:** Frustrated with bloodthirsty Saints

3. **GIDEONITES & ARMY** (★★★)
   - Quote: "How many times have I said that I would, rather have one hundred true Saints in the mountains than five millions that are not Saints, if I had to contend against the whole world? What, with the sword? Yes. Let me have the Gideonites that can kneel down and lap the water, and one will chase a thousand, and two put ten thousand to flight"
   - Quote: "I believe that if the faith of this people were united, all hell cannot get armies in here to disturb our settlements"
   - **Failed Prophecy:** Army DID come to Utah

4. **SELFISH PRAYER CRITICISM** (★★★)
   - Quote: "Perhaps I am wrong, but I think that he will be sure not to pray for the things he ought to. He will pray that himself and family may have plenty to eat and live in peace, and probably stop at that. His prayer will be something like a certain old man's blessing at his meals: 'O Lord, bless me and my wife, my son John and his wife, us four, and no more: Amen.'"
   - Quote: "I have known a tolerably good man to pray. He was so ignorant that he would cheat a widow woman out of her last cow, and then go down on his knees and thank God for his peculiar blessings to him!"

5. **COLONEL ALEXANDER QUOTE** (★★★)
   - Quote: "Colonel Alexander—probably one of the best men in the army now near Bridger ruins, told one of our messengers...that he was compelled to remain in the army; for, if he resigned, he knew not how to manage to sustain his family...'I cannot throw up my commission, for then I should have no means to support my wife and children.'"
   - Quote: "As an American, shame and confusion would overwhelm me, were I to even think of trying to sustain my family by siding with tyranny and oppression"
   - **Tone:** Moral superiority

**Song Potential:**
- Unified prayer = thought control
- "Us four and no more" prayer = memorable satire
- Criticizing bloodthirsty prayers while preparing for war = ironic
- Title: "Pray As You're Told" or "Us Four and No More"

   - Quote: "There are Elders who have laboured from sea to sea, from island to island, from country to country, and have spent the vigour and strength of their days in the work of proclaiming the Gospel. May we not say, upon the same principle, that their garments are clean from the blood of this generation?"

And there is another thing implied in this: **If this generation shall rise against you to slay you for your religion, and because you are righteous, your garments being clear of their blood, and you slay them, their blood is upon their own heads.**"

## DISCOVERY THEMES

### 1857-58 Utah War Context
ALL sermons from November 1857 - height of Utah War crisis. Themes:
- Failed prophecies about army destruction
- Violent rhetoric ("slay them," "bury their dead")
- Theocratic defiance
- Missionary recall as apocalyptic sign
- Blood atonement threats

### Obedience/Authority
- Blind obedience doctrine (Kimball)
- "None of your business whether right or wrong"
- Unified prayer = thought control (Brigham)
- "We'll mind what Brigham says" (Lyman)
- Bishop as "gas pipe" for Holy Ghost

### Blood Atonement
- Sexual sin = death ("We will slay both men and women")
- "Garments clean" = license to kill
- Blood shedding "just as innocent as killing an ox"
- Matter-of-fact tone about killing

### Theocracy
- Kingdom = Church AND State (Taylor)
- "Gospel according to St. Brigham" (treating letters as scripture)
- Rejection of federal authority
- Allegiance only when protection given

### Failed Prophecy
- Army would be miraculously destroyed (multiple sermons)
- "Never could have seen this side of South Pass" - but they're here
- Seven months burying enemy dead
- Weapons for fuel for years
