## Research Notes

### Historical Context

Volume 23 covers 1882, the year the Edmunds Act was passed (March 22, 1882). This law:
- Made polygamy a felony (up to 5 years prison, $500 fine)
- Made "unlawful cohabitation" a misdemeanor (6 months, $300)
- Disenfranchised polygamists
- Created test oath excluding polygamists from voting/office

This explains the EXTREME defensiveness and combative tone throughout the volume. Nearly 25% of sermons directly address polygamy persecution.

### Notable Speakers

**Erastus Snow** — 4 sermons in this volume, including 2 HIGH priority:
- Most extensive polygamy defender in Vol. 23
- Historical arguments (Roman monogamy origins)
- Political arguments (Joseph Smith's slavery proposal)
- Social arguments (New England infanticide accusations)

**Wilford Woodruff** — Apostle, later 4th President:
- Direct eyewitness to Joseph Smith's polygamy
- Contradicts Emma Smith's later denials
- Theological justification (eternal families)

**John Taylor** — 3rd President of the Church:
- Multiple sermons on political/legal issues
- Defiant tone toward federal government

### Apologetic Anticipation

**Emma Smith Denial:**
- Modern LDS scholars note Emma later denied Joseph practiced polygamy
- Woodruff's testimony (JoD 23:124-133) directly contradicts this
- Claims Emma "gave her husband in marriage several women"
- Claims witnesses present in congregation

**Joseph Smith's Slavery Proposal:**
- Historically documented (1844 pamphlet: "General Smith's Views")
- Proposed compensated emancipation via federal sinking fund
- Not widely adopted by any faction
- Snow portrays this as prophetic counsel rejected by nation

**Monogamy Origins:**
- Snow's claim: Roman state, not Christianity
- Historically complex: Roman law did limit to one legal wife
- Early Christian practice varied by region/era
- Catholic prohibition on clerical marriage came later (12th century)
