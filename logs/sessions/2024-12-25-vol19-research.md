## HIGH Priority Sermons (Detailed)

### 1. "There is a God" - Erastus Snow (JoD 19:266-279) ★★★★★

**Date:** March 3, 1878, Beaver City, Utah
**Category:** Adam-God, Mother God, Cosmology
**Song Potential:** MAXIMUM

**Key Doctrines:**
- God consists of man and woman united
- Male and female both necessary for Godhood
- "There is not in all the eternities that exist, nor ever will be, a God in any other way"
- Extended shears metaphor for male/female unity

**Exact Quotes:**

> "What, do you mean we should understand that Deity consists of man and woman? Most certainly I do. If I believe anything that God has ever said about himself, and anything pertaining to the creation and organization of man upon the earth, I must believe that Deity consists of man and woman."

> "I sometimes illustrate this matter by taking up a pair of shears, if I have one, but then you all know they are composed of two halves, but they are necessarily parts, one of another, and to perform their work for each other, as designed, they belong together, and neither one of them is fitted for the accomplishment of their works alone."

> "In other words, there can be no God except he is composed of the man and woman united, and there is not in all the eternities that exist, nor ever will be, a God in any other way."

> "We have another description: There never was a God, and there never will be in all eternities, except they are made of these two component parts; a man and a woman; the male and the female."

**Apologetic Vulnerabilities:**
- No way to reinterpret "man and woman" language
- Explicit and repeated
- Uses analogy (shears) that makes it memorable
- Directly contradicts modern LDS theology which emphasizes singular "Heavenly Father"

**Song Development Notes:**
- The shears metaphor is PERFECT for a song
- Multiple verse opportunities with direct quotes
- Refrain possibility: "Two parts, one God, the shears that cut through time"
- High contrast with modern disavowal

### 2. "Arrival in Salt Lake City" - Joseph F. Smith (JoD 19:187-197) ★★★★

**Date:** September 30, 1877, Salt Lake City
**Category:** Polygamy
**Song Potential:** VERY HIGH
**Speaker:** Joseph F. Smith (future church president, 1901-1918)

**Key Doctrines:**
- Extensive defense of polygamy
- Claims persecution not actually about polygamy
- Asserts polygamy has not harmed anyone
- Argues polygamy promotes health and virtue

**Exact Quotes:**

> "But says one, 'You have dodged the main question; it is polygamy that causes all the trouble!' This is the mind of our enemies generally, yet nothing can be more fallacious; those who assert this only expose their ignorance."

> "Did they ever stop to reflect as to what harm this principle and practice has done? Let me ask the ladies in this vast audience, Have any of you, or do you know of any woman who has been compelled to practice polygamy among this people? Or who has been compelled even to marry at all? I think not."

> "Has plural marriage deprived any woman of a home, of husband or children? Has it promoted immorality or vice? No, it has not. Has it sown the seeds of corruption and death among the people? On the contrary it has promoted healthy, robust and vigorous increase, and the laws of life and health."

> "Can the Elders of this Church be accused of going to the Gentiles for their wives and daughters? No, for we think we have better ones at home, we have not the least occasion to go abroad."

**Apologetic Vulnerabilities:**
- By future prophet (Joseph F. Smith)
- Delivered AFTER 1862 Morrill Anti-Bigamy Act
- Just months before his cousin Joseph Smith III's anti-polygamy campaign
- Claims polygamy never harmed anyone (demonstrably false)
- Church now disavows polygamy entirely

**Song Development Notes:**
- Could contrast 1877 defense with modern disavowal
- JFS became prophet 24 years later
- Multiple quotable defenses
- Irony: "We have better ones at home"

### 3. "Temples in Ancient America" - Orson Pratt (JoD 19:311-321) ★★★★★

**Date:** October 7, 1867, Salt Lake City
**Category:** Plurality of Gods, Cosmology
**Song Potential:** MAXIMUM

**Key Doctrines:**
- Explicit teaching of millions of individual gods
- 144,000 with "God" on foreheads are literal gods
- Man will become "a personal god, as much so as the God of this creation"
- Pre-existence detailed

**Exact Quotes:**

> "We... believe that in the eternity of eternities, in the heaven of heavens there will be innumerable millions of persons who will occupy that exalted station—each one being a personal god, as much so as the God of this creation—the Father of our spirits is."

> "What would you think if you were to have the future opened to you as John had, and could see these men with the word God, inscribed in bright and shining characters upon each of their foreheads? Would you think that God was making fun of them by putting such an inscription there? Would you suppose the inscription was a mere form without any meaning? No: every man permitted to see these things would at once say, 'they are gods having been redeemed, and made like their Father.'"

> "We not only believe in our personal Father, in His Son Jesus Christ, and in the Holy Ghost, as personages, but we also believe that in the eternity of eternities, in the heaven of heavens there will be innumerable millions of persons who will occupy that exalted station—each one being a personal god."

**Apologetic Vulnerabilities:**
- Direct contradiction of modern LDS monotheism emphasis
- "Innumerable millions" of individual gods
- Clear, unambiguous language
- No metaphorical escape

**Song Development Notes:**
- "Millions of Gods" as title/refrain
- Vision of 144,000 with "God" on foreheads
- Contrast with modern "we worship only one God" emphasis
- Quote mining impossible to defend

### 4. "God is Light" - Orson Pratt (JoD 19:281-294) ★★★

**Date:** October 7, 1867, Salt Lake City
**Category:** Cosmology, Pre-existence
**Song Potential:** MEDIUM-HIGH

**Key Doctrines:**
- God as material personage with body
- All humans existed as spirits before birth
- Spirits material, not immaterial
- 1/3 of heaven fell and became devils

**Exact Quotes:**

> "God is a being, then, who has a tabernacle of flesh and bones in which his spirit dwells; and this flesh, bones and spirit are material."

> "We, who compose this congregation, are all one family, and only a very small portion of the family of our Father and God. But when did he beget us? I answer before this world was made; not our flesh and bones, but that being called man that was created in the image and likeness of God and who dwells in his mortal tabernacle."

**Apologetic Vulnerabilities:**
- Material nature of spirits (now downplayed)
- Pre-existence details (still taught but less emphasized)
- Detailed cosmology now simplified

**Song Development Notes:**
- Could work as part of cosmology medley
- Pre-existence still taught, so less controversial
- Material spirits doctrine interesting
