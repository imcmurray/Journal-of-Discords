# TOPIC: Racial Teachings
## Teachings on Race, Curse of Cain, and Interracial Marriage

---

## Overview

Early LDS prophets taught that Black people bore the "curse of Cain," were less valiant in the pre-existence, and were denied priesthood and temple blessings as a result. Interracial marriage was condemned with severe language, including death penalty rhetoric.

**Why This Matters:**
- Taught as doctrine explaining priesthood/temple ban
- Used theological justifications (pre-existence, curse of Cain)
- Interracial marriage = "death on the spot" language
- 1978 revelation reversed the ban
- 2013 church essay disavowed the teachings as theories

---

## Key Findings

### Interracial Marriage

#### Finding 1: "Death on the Spot"
**Source:** JoD 10:110 (March 8, 1863)

> "Shall I tell you the law of God in regard to the African race? If the white man who belongs to the chosen seed mixes his blood with the seed of Cain, the penalty, under the law of God, is death on the spot. This will always be so."
> — Brigham Young

**Category:** DISAVOWED, MORAL-PROBLEM

**Context:** General discourse on race and lineage. Brigham presents this as "the law of God" — not opinion, not cultural preference, divine law.

**Why It Matters:**
- "The law of God" — claimed divine authority
- "Death on the spot" — capital punishment
- "This will always be so" — eternal, unchangeable
- Now completely rejected

## The Doctrinal Problem

### What Was Taught:
1. Black skin = mark/curse of Cain
2. Less valiant in pre-existence
3. Interracial marriage = death penalty under God's law
4. "This will always be so"
5. Priesthood/temple denial was God's will

### What the Church Now Says (2013 Essay):
1. The curse of Cain teaching is disavowed
2. Pre-existence valiance theory is disavowed
3. These were "theories" not doctrine
4. The ban was policy, causes unknown
5. Church "disavows the theories advanced in the past"

### The Contradiction:
- "The law of God" → now "theories advanced in the past"
- "This will always be so" → changed in 1978
- Prophetic teaching → now disavowed

## Apologetic Responses

### Defense 1: "Product of His Time"

**What They Say:** These views reflected 19th-century American racism.

**Why It Fails:**
1. This defense proves the point — prophets reflected culture, not revelation
2. "The law of God" claim elevates it beyond cultural opinion
3. Other Christians opposed slavery and racism in this period
4. A prophet should transcend cultural prejudice

---

### Defense 2: "Speaking as a Man"

**What They Say:** Not everything a prophet says is doctrine.

**Why It Fails:**
1. "Shall I tell you the law of God" is explicit doctrine claim
2. It was institutional policy for 130+ years
3. Temple and priesthood were denied — real consequences
4. If this was "as a man," what ISN'T?

---

### Defense 3: "We Don't Know Why the Ban Existed"

**What They Say:** The 2013 essay admits we don't know the origin of the ban.

**Why It Fails:**
1. We DO know what justifications were taught
2. Those justifications are documented in JoD
3. "We don't know" isn't honest when the record is clear
4. Leaders taught reasons; those reasons are now disavowed

---

### Defense 4: "The Church Changed"

**What They Say:** 1978 revelation shows continuing revelation.

**Why It Fails:**
1. "This will always be so" was wrong
2. If eternal law changes, is it eternal?
3. Why did God wait until 1978?
4. Real people were harmed for 130 years
