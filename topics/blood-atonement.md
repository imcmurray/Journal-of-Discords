# TOPIC: Blood Atonement
## The Doctrine That Some Sins Require the Shedding of the Sinner's Blood

---

## Overview

Blood atonement is the teaching that certain sins are so serious that the blood of Christ cannot atone for them—only the shedding of the sinner's own blood can provide salvation. This was taught by Brigham Young and other leaders, particularly during the "Mormon Reformation" of 1856-57.

**Why This Matters:**
- Taught as doctrine necessary for salvation
- Implies (or explicitly states) killing sinners is an act of love
- Now completely disavowed by the church
- Associated with historical violence (Mountain Meadows, etc.)
- Creates serious moral problems regardless of historicity

## Key Findings

### The Core Teaching

#### Finding 1: Some Sins Beyond Christ's Atonement
**Source:** JoD 4:53-54 (September 21, 1856)

> "There are sins that men commit for which they cannot receive forgiveness in this world, or in that which is to come, and if they had their eyes open to see their true condition, they would be perfectly willing to have their blood spilt upon the ground, that the smoke thereof might ascend to heaven as an offering for their sins; and the smoking incense would atone for their sins, whereas, if such is not the case, they will stick to them and remain upon them in the spirit world."
> — Brigham Young

**Category:** DISAVOWED, MORAL-PROBLEM

**Context:** Delivered during the Mormon Reformation, a period of intense religious revival in Utah. This was not an isolated statement.

#### Finding 2: Love Thy Neighbor = Kill Them
**Source:** JoD 4:219-220 (February 8, 1857)

> "I could refer you to plenty of instances where men have been righteously slain, in order to atone for their sins. I have seen scores and hundreds of people for whom there would have been a chance... if their lives had been taken and their blood spilled on the ground as a smoking incense to the Almighty... This is loving our neighbor as ourselves; if he needs help, help him; and if he wants salvation and it is necessary to spill his blood on the earth in order that he may be saved, spill it."
> — Brigham Young

**Category:** DISAVOWED, MORAL-PROBLEM

**Context:** Explicit statement that killing sinners is an act of love and necessary for their salvation.

**Why It Matters:**

- "Righteously slain" — killing framed as righteous
- "Scores and hundreds of people" — implies this happened

## The Doctrinal Problem

### What Was Taught:
1. Some sins are beyond Christ's atonement
2. Only the sinner's own blood can atone
3. Killing such sinners is an act of love
4. This is necessary for their salvation
5. This has been done ("scores and hundreds")

### What the Church Now Says:
1. Christ's atonement is sufficient for all sins (except blasphemy against Holy Ghost)
2. Human sacrifice is not part of LDS doctrine
3. Blood atonement was "theoretical" not "practical"
4. No evidence it was ever practiced (disputed by historians)

### The Problems:
1. If it was doctrinal then, why isn't it now?
2. If "theoretical," why say "I have seen scores and hundreds"?
3. How does "love thy neighbor = kill them" get walked back?
4. Mountain Meadows and other incidents suggest it WAS practiced

## Apologetic Responses

### Defense 1: "It Was Theoretical, Never Practiced"

**What They Say:** Blood atonement was discussed theologically but never implemented.

**Why It Fails:**
1. "I have seen scores and hundreds of people" — past tense, observed
2. "Men have been righteously slain" — past tense, happened
3. Mountain Meadows Massacre (1857) occurred during this period
4. Other documented killings during the Reformation
5. If "theoretical," why preach it as action?

**Source Rebuttal:**
> "I could refer you to plenty of instances where men have been righteously slain, in order to atone for their sins."

"Plenty of instances" is not theoretical.

---

### Defense 2: "Product of His Time"

**What They Say:** 19th-century frontier justice was harsh. This reflects the era.

**Why It Fails:**
1. This wasn't presented as frontier justice but as theological necessity
2. "Smoking incense to the Almighty" is religious, not pragmatic
3. A true prophet should transcend cultural violence, not baptize it
4. Other Christians of the era did not teach this

---

### Defense 3: "Taken Out of Context"

**What They Say:** These quotes don't represent the full teaching.

**Why It Fails:**
1. The full sermons are available — context doesn't help
2. The teaching was consistent across multiple sermons
3. Multiple leaders taught it
4. The Mormon Reformation was a sustained campaign

---

### Defense 4: "We Don't Practice It Now"

**What They Say:** Whatever was taught then, we don't believe it now.

**Why It Fails:**
1. Admits prophets taught something now rejected
2. If they were wrong about this, what else?
3. Doesn't address the moral horror of the teaching
4. Doesn't explain why it was taught at all
