# TOPIC: Bizarre Theology
## Unusual, Disturbing, and Unexplainable Doctrines in the Journal of Discourses

---

## Overview

Beyond the major doctrinal categories (Adam-God, blood atonement, racial teachings, polygamy), the Journal of Discourses contains numerous theological claims that are simply bizarre—teachings that:

- Contradict modern church doctrine without formal repudiation
- Present disturbing views of God's plan and purposes
- Create logical contradictions and theological problems
- Are so unusual they defy categorization
- Have been quietly abandoned without explanation

These teachings are particularly valuable because they:
1. Cannot be dismissed as "products of their time" (they were strange even then)
2. Were stated with prophetic confidence and certainty
3. Reveal fundamental confusion or evolution in core doctrines
4. Create apologetic nightmares (no clean defense)
5. Demonstrate that prophets taught bizarre ideas with full conviction

**Why This Matters:**
- These aren't just failed prophecies or outdated policies
- They reveal fundamentally different views of God, salvation, and reality
- Modern members are unaware these teachings ever existed
- The church's silence about them raises questions about transparency
- If prophets were this wrong about fundamental theology, what else is unreliable?

## Key Findings

### TEMPLE AND SALVATION

#### Finding 1: Endowments Given to Create Devils
**Source:** JoD 8:181-182 (September 9, 1860)
**Speaker:** Brigham Young

> "Do you query why we give endowments to A., B., and C? It is to make devils of those who will deny the faith, for that is also necessary, as a host of devils will be needed."
> — Brigham Young, JoD 8:181-182

**Context:** Brigham is instructing missionaries. After discussing the future kingdom where enemies become servants and apostates go to hell, he reveals that temple endowments serve a dual purpose: making saints AND making devils.

**Full Context Quote:**
> "A person, to become an angel of the Devil, has first to be a good Saint, and then deny the Lord who bought him."
> — Brigham Young, JoD 8:181

**Additional Context:**
> "What will you do with all those who have sought to kill you? Make them soap-boilers and kitchen flunkeys. We are not going to send them into hell fire, for it takes a good Latter-day Saint apostatized to get down into that deep (did I say bottomless?) pit."
> — Brigham Young, JoD 8:181

**Category:** BIZARRE (primary), DISAVOWED (secondary), SELF-CONTRADICTORY

**Why It's Bizarre:**
1. **Dual-purpose sacred ordinances** — Temple endowments serve BOTH to exalt saints AND to create devils
2. **Cosmic necessity of devils** — "A host of devils will be needed" for God's plan to function
3. **Predestination implications** — Endowments given to people who "will deny the faith" suggests foreknowledge
4. **Instrumental use of sacred rites** — Most sacred ordinances are tools for cosmic opposition, not just salvation
5. **Contradicts modern teaching** — Modern church NEVER mentions creating devils as a purpose of endowments

**Self-Contradiction:** In the same sermon, Brigham says:
- "A host of devils will be needed" (many damned)
- "All who lived according to the best light they had will have better kingdoms than ever entered into their hearts to conceive" (universal positive outcome)

These cannot both be true.

**Modern Church Position:**
- Temple endowments are for making covenants that lead to exaltation
- Never mentions creating devils as a purpose
- De-emphasizes sons of perdition doctrine
- Focuses exclusively on positive aspects of temple worship
- Would be horrified by this characterization

**Analysis:** `/home/ianm/Development/jod-project/volumes/vol-08/findings/1860-09-09-host-of-devils.md`

**Song Potential:** ★★★★ (HIGH)

**Apologetic Problems:**
- "Hyperbole" → Too specific and theological
- "Out of context" → Context makes it worse
- "Possibility not intent" → "To make devils" is purpose language
- "Opposition theology" → Opposition doesn't require instrumentally creating devils through sacred ordinances
- "Foreknowledge" → If God knows they'll become devils, why increase their damnation with sacred knowledge?
- "Speculation" → Too serious a topic, no hedging language, official setting
- "Growing in understanding" → Complete reversal, not refinement

#### Finding 2: Only Good Saints Can Become Angels of the Devil
**Source:** JoD 8:181 (September 9, 1860)
**Speaker:** Brigham Young

> "A person, to become an angel of the Devil, has first to be a good Saint, and then deny the Lord who bought him."
> — Brigham Young, JoD 8:181

**Context:** Brigham is explaining the hierarchy of damnation. External enemies will be servants in the kingdom; only apostate endowed saints can reach the deepest hell.

**Category:** BIZARRE, DISAVOWED

**Why It's Bizarre:**
1. **Higher damnation for faithful** — Being a "good Saint" creates possibility for worse outcome than never joining
2. **Temple ordinances increase risk** — Sacred knowledge creates potential for "angel of the Devil" status
3. **Inverted reward structure** — More light/knowledge = greater potential damnation
4. **Contradicts missionary message** — "Join us and you might become a devil's angel"

**Modern Church Position:** Sons of perdition doctrine is rarely taught and extremely de-emphasized. Modern teaching focuses on salvation, not damnation tiers.

#### Finding 3: It Takes a Good Saint to Reach the Bottomless Pit
**Source:** JoD 8:181 (September 9, 1860)
**Speaker:** Brigham Young

> "What will you do with all those who have sought to kill you? Make them soap-boilers and kitchen flunkeys. We are not going to send them into hell fire, for it takes a good Latter-day Saint apostatized to get down into that deep (did I say bottomless?) pit."
> — Brigham Young, JoD 8:181

**Context:** Discussing fate of enemies vs. apostates in the future kingdom.

**Category:** BIZARRE, CONTRA-MODERN

**Why It's Bizarre:**
1. **Parenthetical uncertainty** — "(did I say bottomless?)" shows Brigham questioning his own cosmology mid-sentence
2. **Enemies get better outcome** — Those who "sought to kill you" become servants, not devils
3. **Membership increases damnation potential** — External persecutors fare better than internal apostates
4. **Cosmic hierarchy** — Never-believers < enemies < apostate saints (inverted from expected)

**Modern Church Position:** Modern church emphasizes God's mercy and multiple kingdoms of glory (D&C 76). Outer darkness is mentioned but not emphasized.

### EXALTATION AND KINGDOMS

#### Finding 4: Wives and Children by the Million
**Source:** JoD 8:181 (September 9, 1860)
**Speaker:** Brigham Young

> "Brother Cannon remarked that people wondered how many wives and children I had. He may inform them that I shall have wives and children by the million, and glory, and riches, and power, and dominion, and kingdom after kingdom, and reign triumphantly."
> — Brigham Young, JoD 8:181

**Context:** Responding to George Q. Cannon's comment about public curiosity regarding Brigham's plural families. Brigham expands to describe his future exalted state.

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN (eternal polygamy de-emphasized), BIZARRE (scale of claims)

**Why It's Bizarre:**
1. **Celestial polygamy on cosmic scale** — "By the million" is staggering
2. **Eternal increase doctrine** — Having spirit children eternally
3. **Kingdom after kingdom** — Becoming a god over multiple worlds
4. **Personal deification** — Extremely bold claim about personal future
5. **Context for devils** — Large kingdoms need opposition ("host of devils")

**Modern Church Position:**
- De-emphasizes eternal polygamy
- Rarely discusses mechanics of eternal increase
- Focuses on "eternal families" without specifics about celestial marriage structure
- Official essay on polygamy doesn't address eternal continuation

#### Finding 5: Universal Good Kingdoms (Contradiction)
**Source:** JoD 8:182 (September 9, 1860)
**Speaker:** Brigham Young

> "My charity is that God has provided for his children, the sons and daughters of Adam and Eve, where all who have lived according to the best light they had will have better kingdoms than ever entered into their hearts to conceive."
> — Brigham Young, JoD 8:182

**Context:** Responding to Heber C. Kimball's comment about charity.

**Category:** SELF-CONTRADICTORY (with "devils needed" statement), BIZARRE

**Why It's Bizarre:**
1. **Universal salvation tone** — Everyone gets "better kingdoms than they conceived"
2. **Contradicts devils statement** — If all get good kingdoms, who populates hell?
3. **Within same sermon** — Direct contradiction in adjacent passages
4. **Tension unresolved** — Brigham never addresses the conflict

**Self-Contradiction:**
- "A host of devils will be needed" (many damned) vs.
- "All who lived according to best light will have better kingdoms" (universal good outcome)

If everyone living according to their light gets good kingdoms, where do the devils come from? Only from those who received MORE than their light (endowed saints with full knowledge who apostatize). This creates the bizarre theology that sacred ordinances primarily serve to create potential devils.

### NATURE OF GOD AND CREATION

#### Finding 6: Children Remain Children Eternally
**Source:** JoD 6:9 (April 7, 1844)
**Speaker:** Joseph Smith

> "But as the child dies, so shall it rise from the dead, and be for ever living in the learning of God. It will never grow: it will still be the child, in the same precise form as it appeared before it died out of its mother's arms, but possessing all the intelligence of a God. Children dwell in the mansions of glory and exercise power, but appear in the same form as when on earth. Eternity is full of thrones, upon which dwell thousands of children reigning on thrones of glory, with not one cubit added to their stature."
> — Joseph Smith, JoD 6:9

**Context:** King Follett Discourse. Joseph offers comfort to grieving parents by teaching that children who die young remain children forever in form while possessing divine intelligence.

**Category:** BIZARRE, CONTRA-MODERN (not emphasized)

**Why It's Bizarre:**
1. **Eternal child-form with God-intelligence** — Bizarre combination
2. **No growth in stature** — "Not one cubit added" seems to contradict eternal progression
3. **Thousands of child-gods** — "Eternity is full of thrones" populated by children
4. **Exercising power as children** — Child appearance with divine authority
5. **Raises questions** — What about eternal families? Spousal relationships? Progression?

**Modern Church Position:**
- Not emphasized in modern teaching
- Creates too many theological problems
- Contradicts emphasis on eternal progression
- Modern teaching focuses on eternal families without this specificity

### COSMIC MECHANICS

#### Finding 7: God's Ongoing Progression Through Children's Exaltation
**Source:** JoD 6:4 (April 7, 1844)
**Speaker:** Joseph Smith

> "What did Jesus do? Why, I do the things I saw my Father do when worlds come rolling into existence. My Father worked out his kingdom with fear and trembling, and I must do the same; and when I get my kingdom, I shall present it to my Father, so that he may obtain kingdom upon kingdom, and it will exalt him in glory. He will then take a higher exaltation, and I will take his place, and thereby become exalted myself."
> — Joseph Smith, JoD 6:4

**Context:** King Follett Discourse. Joseph explains the mechanism of divine progression—Gods continue to advance through the exaltation of their spirit children.

**Category:** DISAVOWED, CONTRA-MODERN, BIZARRE

**Why It's Bizarre:**
1. **God continues to progress** — Not eternally perfect, still advancing
2. **Generational succession** — Jesus takes Father's place, Father moves higher
3. **Kingdom upon kingdom** — Endless progression of divine advancement
4. **"Fear and trembling"** — God worked out salvation with anxiety/uncertainty
5. **Never-ending hierarchy** — Implies infinite regress of Gods above God

**Modern Church Position:**
- Rarely taught
- Presents problems for God's omnipotence and perfection
- Creates theological questions about ultimate divinity
- Modern teaching emphasizes God's perfection, not His progression

## Modern Church Position

The modern church has taken a position of **strategic silence** on these teachings:

**What They Do:**
- Don't formally repudiate the teachings
- Don't mention them in manuals or lessons
- Keep JoD published but not emphasized
- De-emphasize sons of perdition doctrine
- Focus on positive aspects of temple worship
- Avoid specific claims about exaltation mechanics

**What They Don't Do:**
- Don't say these teachings were false
- Don't explain the contradictions
- Don't address members who discover these teachings
- Don't provide alternative explanations
- Don't acknowledge the shift in teaching

**The Problem:**
This creates an epistemological crisis:
1. If these teachings were true, why abandon them?
2. If they were false, why not say so?
3. If prophets taught false doctrine confidently, how do we trust current prophets?
4. If the church evolved in understanding, why not acknowledge it openly?

## Apologetic Responses

### Defense 1: "These Were Speculations, Not Doctrine"

**What they'll say:** Brigham and Joseph sometimes speculated. Not everything they said was official doctrine.

**Why it fails:**
1. Delivered in official settings (General Conference, missionary instructions)
2. Recorded by official scribes and published
3. No hedging language ("I think," "perhaps," "it seems")
4. Topics too sacred (temple ordinances) for casual speculation
5. If these can be dismissed, what CAN'T be dismissed?

---

### Defense 2: "Out of Context"

**What they'll say:** These quotes are cherry-picked and taken out of context.

**Why it fails:**
1. Full context often makes it worse (e.g., endowments quote comes amid other bizarre claims)
2. Context confirms the bizarre nature, doesn't explain it away
3. Providing full sermons available for verification
4. The issue isn't context; it's content

---

### Defense 3: "The Church Has Grown in Understanding"

**What they'll say:** Line upon line, continuing revelation has clarified these teachings.

**Why it fails:**
1. These aren't refinements; they're complete reversals
2. No official revelation has corrected them
3. Church doesn't say "we used to be wrong about this"
4. If prophets misunderstood fundamental sacred ordinances, what else is wrong?
5. Members living under these teachings had fundamentally different gospel

---

### Defense 4: "Prophets Are Human"

**What they'll say:** Prophets aren't perfect; they make mistakes.

**Why it fails:**
1. The issue is reliability, not perfection
2. These are fundamental doctrines (temple purposes, God's nature), not minor details
3. Certainty language ("no question of it," "it is to make devils") claims divine knowledge
4. If prophets are this unreliable on core doctrines, why follow them?
5. Creates impossible standard: trust them completely, but they might be completely wrong

---

### Defense 5: "Opposition Theology"

**What they'll say:** (For devils teaching) LDS theology teaches opposition is necessary; this fits that framework.

**Why it fails:**
1. Opposition theology doesn't require CREATING devils through sacred ordinances
2. Satan and his hosts already provide opposition (pre-mortal rebellion)
3. This defense validates the bizarre claim rather than explaining it away
4. If accepted, means every endowment session includes future devils by divine design
5. Raises more questions than it answers

---

### Defense 6: "You're Trying to Make Mormonism Look Weird"

**What they'll say:** Critics cherry-pick unusual statements to make the church look bad.

**Why it fails:**
1. These ARE weird—that's the point
2. Not cherry-picking; providing full context and documentation
3. Modern church's silence about these teachings suggests they agree they're problematic
4. If these teachings were sound, church would still teach them
5. "It sounds weird" is not a counter-argument to "they taught weird things"
