# TOPIC: Failed Prophecies
## When Prophetic Declarations Don't Come to Pass

---

## Overview

Early LDS prophets made specific, testable predictions about the future. Some involved timelines ("not far distant"), absolute statements ("shall never"), or conditional prophecies with measurable outcomes. When these prophecies demonstrably failed to materialize—or were directly contradicted by subsequent church actions—they reveal the limits of prophetic authority.

**Why This Matters:**
- Tests the claim that LDS prophets speak for God
- Prophets used absolute language ("never," "cannot," "as sure as He predicted it")
- Failures often involve central doctrines (polygamy, priesthood, constitution)
- Creates apologetic crisis: Were they speaking for God or not?
- Undermines trust in current prophetic declarations

---

## What Constitutes a Failed Prophecy?

### Clear Criteria:
1. **Specific prediction** made by church authority
2. **Testable parameters** (timeline, outcome, conditions)
3. **Presented as revelation** or divine certainty
4. **Demonstrable non-occurrence** or direct contradiction
5. **No reasonable reinterpretation** that preserves accuracy

### Not Included:
- Vague spiritual promises
- Conditional blessings dependent on individual faith
- General warnings without specific timelines
- Poetic/metaphorical language
- Statements explicitly labeled as speculation

#### Finding 1: "We Cannot Tone It Down"
**Source:** JoD 25:309 (October 6-7, 1884)
**Speaker:** John Taylor (Church President)
**Analysis:** `/home/ianm/Development/jod-project/volumes/vol-25/findings/1884-10-06-cannot-tone-down.md`

**Primary Quote:**
> "God has given us a revelation in regard to celestial marriage. I did not make it. He has told us certain things pertaining to this matter, and they would like us to tone that principle down and change it and make it applicable to the views of the day. This we cannot do; nor can we interfere with any of the commands of God to meet the persuasions or behests of men. I cannot do it, and will not do it."
> — John Taylor, JoD 25:309

**Additional Key Quotes:**
- "We will stand by the principles of eternal truth; living we will proclaim them, and dying we will be true to them, and after death will live again in their enjoyment in the eternal worlds... and all who sanction it say Amen—[the vast congregation responded with a loud 'Amen.']" (JoD 25:309)
- "We have also been told that 'it is not mete that men who will not abide my law shall preside over my Priesthood,' and yet some people would like very much to do it. Well, they cannot do it." (JoD 25:309)

**Category:** PRIMARY: FAILED-PROPH / SECONDARY: IRONIC, SELF-CONTRA, DISAVOWED, MORAL-PROBLEM

**Context:** General Conference, October 6-7, 1884 — exactly **5 years, 11 months, 18 days** before the 1890 Manifesto. Church President speaking from Tabernacle pulpit with formal congregational ratification.

**The Prophecy:**
- **What was promised:** "We CANNOT... interfere with any of the commands of God to meet the persuasions or behests of men"
- **Timeline:** Presented as absolute impossibility (not "won't" but "CANNOT")
- **Authority claim:** Church President at General Conference, congregational "Amen" vote
- **What happened:** Six years later, church issued Manifesto "interfering" with polygamy to meet government demands

**Why It Failed:**
- **Six years later:** 1890 Manifesto abandoned polygamy practice
- **"Persuasions or behests of men":** Federal government pressure (statehood, property confiscation) was exactly what Taylor said they wouldn't accommodate
- **"Cannot" became "did":** Theological impossibility became historical fact
- **Congregational vote reversed:** Same congregation (or descendants) voted "Amen" to opposite position in 1890

**Song Status:** ★★★★★ PLATINUM TIER

#### Finding 2: "Never Given to Another People"
**Source:** JoD 25:271 (August 31, 1884)
**Speaker:** George Q. Cannon (First Presidency)
**Analysis:** `/home/ianm/Development/jod-project/volumes/vol-25/findings/1884-08-31-never-another-people.md`

**Primary Quote:**
> "God has given unto us the assurance that this Church, this work that He has established, shall never be given into the hands of another people, but that it shall stand forever, and it shall go forward accomplishing His designs, until it shall fill the whole earth."
> — George Q. Cannon, JoD 25:271

**Additional Key Quotes:**
- "Men may be slain, as they have been; people may be driven, as they have been; efforts of the most herculean character may be made to extirpate this work from the earth, but we have the promise of our God that it shall stand and that it shall not be overthrown." (JoD 25:271)
- "Looking at affairs naturally, however, it would seem as though it was presumptuous in a people like us to entertain such hopes. How often have we been told that in a very little while the opposition to this work would be of such a character that it would completely overwhelm it." (JoD 25:271)

**Category:** PRIMARY: FAILED-PROPH / SECONDARY: IRONIC, CONTRA-MODERN

**Context:** August 31, 1884 — six years before 1890 Manifesto. First Presidency member during intense federal persecution (Edmunds Act 1882, pending Edmunds-Tucker Act 1887). "The work" being defended was centrally polygamy.

**The Prophecy:**
- **What was promised:** "This Church, this work... shall NEVER be given into the hands of another people"
- **Timeline:** Eternal ("shall stand forever," "fill the whole earth")
- **Authority claim:** "We have the promise of our God" (divine assurance)
- **What happened:** Either mainstream LDS became "another people" (changed doctrine) or fundamentalists did (separated from mainstream)

**Why It Failed:**
- **Six years later:** Fundamental doctrine (polygamy) abandoned
- **"The work" in 1884:** Polygamy was the defining practice under attack
- **Two interpretations, both failures:**
  1. **Mainstream perspective:** Fundamentalists (FLDS, AUB) are "another people" who received "the work" (1884 doctrine) when mainstream abandoned it
  2. **Fundamentalist perspective:** Mainstream became "another people" by abandoning "the work"
- **Either way:** The 1884 "work" was not preserved by the same people

**Song Status:** ★★★★★ CRITICAL TIER — Perfect ironic question: "Who are the 'other people'?"

#### Finding 3: "Until the Last Ones" (Priesthood Ban Timeline)
**Source:** JoD 7:290-291 (October 9, 1859)
**Speaker:** Brigham Young
**Analysis:** `/home/ianm/Development/jod-project/volumes/vol-07/findings/1859-10-09-flat-nose-black-skin.md`

**Primary Quote:**
> "Trace mankind down to after the flood, and then another curse is pronounced upon the same race—that they should be the 'servant of servants;' and they will be, until that curse is removed; and the Abolitionists cannot help it, nor in the least alter that decree. How long is that race to endure the dreadful curse that is upon them? That curse will remain upon them, and they never can hold the Priesthood or share in it until all the other descendants of Adam have received the promises and enjoyed the blessings of the Priesthood and the keys thereof. Until the last ones of the residue of Adam's children are brought up to that favourable position, the children of Cain cannot receive the first ordinances of the Priesthood. They were the first that were cursed, and they will be the last from whom the curse will be removed."
> — Brigham Young, JoD 7:290-291

**Category:** PRIMARY: FAILED-PROPH / SECONDARY: DISAVOWED, MORAL-PROBLEM, FALSIFIED

**Context:** October 9, 1859 — two years before Civil War. Priesthood ban against Black members in effect but not yet formally codified. Young establishing theological justification for racial policies.

**The Prophecy:**
- **What was promised:** Black people will be "the LAST" to receive priesthood — only after "ALL the other descendants of Adam" receive it first
- **Timeline:** "Until the last ones of the residue of Adam's children are brought up to that favourable position"
- **Authority claim:** "Abolitionists cannot help it, nor in the least alter that decree" (divine decree, unchangeable)
- **What happened:** 1978 revelation extended priesthood to Black members while millions of non-Black people still haven't received priesthood ordinances

**Why It Failed:**
- **1978 Official Declaration 2:** Extended priesthood to all worthy males regardless of race
- **Timeline contradiction:** Black members received priesthood while millions of non-Black people ("other descendants of Adam") still haven't received it
- **"Last ones" proven false:** They were not the last; they received it while many others haven't
- **Sequential order violated:** "First cursed, last freed" order not followed

**Apologetic Problems:**
- Can't claim "all other descendants" means "all worthy/faithful others" — that changes the prophecy entirely
- Young explicitly rejected human intervention ("Abolitionists cannot help it")
- The 1978 change came from leadership decision, not after all non-Black people received priesthood
- 2013 "Race and the Priesthood" essay disavows the entire Curse of Cain framework

**Song Status:** ★★★★★

## Cross-Reference Connections

### Timeline Pattern: 1884 Prophecies to 1890 Failures

Both major polygamy prophecies (Cannon and Taylor) were delivered at **1884 General Conference** — mere months apart:

| Date | Speaker | Prophecy | Failure Date | Gap |
|------|---------|----------|--------------|-----|
| **Aug 31, 1884** | George Q. Cannon | "Never given to another people" | Sep 24, 1890 | 6 years |
| **Oct 6-7, 1884** | John Taylor | "We cannot tone it down" | Sep 24, 1890 | 5y 11m 18d |

**Pattern:** Both used absolute language ("never," "cannot"), both claimed divine authority, both failed within six years.

### Connection Type: Prophecy-Failure Chain

1. **Cannon (1884):** "This work shall never be given to another people"
   - **FAILED:** Either mainstream or fundamentalists became "another people"

2. **Taylor (1884):** "We cannot interfere with any of the commands of God to meet the persuasions or behests of men"
   - **FAILED:** Manifesto interfered with polygamy to meet government pressure

3. **Brigham (1859):** Black people will be "the last ones" to receive priesthood
   - **FAILED:** 1978 revelation gave priesthood while millions of non-Black people still haven't received it

4. **Cannon (1884):** Constitutional collapse "fast approaching"
   - **FAILED:** 140+ years later, still waiting

5. **Cannon (1884):** Civil strife "not far distant," will be "fulfilled to the letter"
   - **FAILED:** 140+ years later, no mass migration to Saints for peace

### Authority Connection: Highest Leadership, Complete Failures

- **John Taylor:** Church President at General Conference
- **George Q. Cannon:** First Counselor in First Presidency
- **Brigham Young:** Church President

**Pattern:** Highest possible prophetic authority → demonstrable prophetic failure

**Problem:** If prophets at peak authority can make categorically false predictions, what prophecies can be trusted?

### The Congregational Problem: Conflicting "Amen" Votes

**October 6-7, 1884 (General Conference):**
- John Taylor: "We cannot tone it down... and all who sanction it say Amen"
- **Congregation:** Loud "AMEN"
- **Result:** Formally ratified commitment to never compromise polygamy

**October 6, 1890 (General Conference):**
- Wilford Woodruff: Manifesto presented for sustaining vote
- **Congregation:** "AMEN"
- **Result:** Formally ratified abandonment of polygamy

**The Dilemma:**
- Both votes claimed divine inspiration
- Both votes involved solemn covenant
- Both votes contradicted each other
- **Question:** Which "Amen" was from God?

### Fundamentalist Connection: Who Kept the Prophecy?

**FLDS/Fundamentalist Claim:**
- We refused to "tone it down" (kept Taylor's commitment)
- We didn't give "the work" to "another people" (kept Cannon's prophecy)
- We practice what prophets taught as "commands of God"
- **Mainstream broke the prophecy, we kept it**

**Mainstream LDS Response:**
- Fundamentalists are apostates who rejected living prophets
- Manifesto was revelation superseding previous teaching
- We follow priesthood authority, not old sermons
- **Fundamentalists are the "other people"**

**The Irony:**
- By 1884 standards, fundamentalists kept the prophecy
- By modern standards, mainstream followed living prophets
- **Cannon's prophecy guarantees one of them failed**

## The Doctrinal Problem

### What Was Promised vs. What Happened

| Promise | Reality | Gap |
|---------|---------|-----|
| "We CANNOT tone it down" (Taylor, 1884) | Toned it down (Manifesto, 1890) | 6 years |
| "NEVER given to another people" (Cannon, 1884) | Either mainstream or FLDS is "another people" | 6 years |
| "Until the LAST ONES" (Brigham, 1859) | Not last; millions of non-Black still unreceived | 119 years |
| "Fast approaching" constitutional collapse (Cannon, 1884) | Still waiting | 140+ years |
| "Not far distant" civil strife (Cannon, 1884) | Still waiting | 140+ years |

### Common Elements in All Failures:

1. **Absolute language:** "Cannot," "never," "last ones," "fast approaching," "to the letter"
2. **Divine authority claims:** "Promise of our God," "commands of God," "God revealed this"
3. **Highest church authority:** Church Presidents, First Presidency
4. **Testable parameters:** Timelines, sequences, specific outcomes
5. **Demonstrable failure:** Historical record contradicts prophecies
6. **No reasonable reinterpretation:** Plain language, specific claims

## Common Apologetic Defenses (And Why They Fail)

### Defense #1: "Speaking as a Man, Not Prophet"

**Why It Fails:**
- All delivered from church pulpits in official settings
- All published in official church records (Journal of Discourses)
- All claimed divine revelation/assurance as source
- If prophets can be catastrophically wrong in official settings, what can be trusted?
- The "speaking as man" defense undermines the entire concept of prophetic authority

**Source Rebuttal:**
- Taylor: "God has given us a revelation... He has told us certain things" (JoD 25:309)
- Cannon: "We have the promise of our God" (JoD 25:271)
- Brigham: "Abolitionists cannot help it, nor in the least alter that decree" (JoD 7:290-291)

---

### Defense #2: "Circumstances Changed; Continuing Revelation"

**Why It Fails:**
- Taylor explicitly rejected this: "They would like us to... make it applicable to the views of the day. This we cannot do" (JoD 25:309)
- Cannon framed it as divine decree unchangeable by humans
- If "eternal principles" change based on government pressure, they're not eternal
- Creates theological problem: What other "commands of God" might change?
- Undermines reliability of current prophetic declarations

**Source Rebuttal:**
- "Nor can we interfere with any of the commands of God to meet the persuasions or behests of men" (Taylor, JoD 25:309)
- The Manifesto was literally interfering with commands to meet government persuasion

---

### Defense #3: "Timeline Prophecies Could Still Come True"

**Why It Fails:**
- "Fast approaching" in 1884 ≠ 140+ years later
- "Not far distant" in 1884 ≠ still waiting in 2025
- Language matters: "Fast," "not far distant," and "to the letter" set expectations
- Moving goalposts indefinitely makes prophecy unfalsifiable (therefore meaningless)
- Failed timeline predictions are still failed prophecies

**Source Rebuttal:**
- "It will, just as sure as He predicted it, be fulfilled to the letter" (Cannon, JoD 25:276)
- "To the letter" means precise fulfillment, not "eventually maybe"

---

### Defense #4: "They Were Right About the Principle, Wrong About Details"

**Why It Fails:**
- The "details" ARE the prophecy (timelines, absolutes, conditions)
- "Cannot" vs. "did" is not a detail—it's the substance
- "Never" vs. "six years later" is not a detail—it's complete reversal
- "Last ones" vs. "not last" is not a detail—it's factual error
- Prophecies require accuracy, not just vague directional correctness

**Source Rebuttal:**
- "We cannot do; nor can we interfere" (Taylor) — "Cannot" is categorical
- "Shall never be given" (Cannon) — "Never" is absolute
- "They will be the last" (Brigham) — "Last" is sequential and testable

---

### Defense #5: "We Don't Understand the Full Context"

**Why It Fails:**
- Full context makes prophecies WORSE, not better
- 1884 context: Height of polygamy persecution, leaders promising God would preserve them
- Six years later: God didn't preserve them; they surrendered
- Context shows these were CENTRAL promises, not peripheral comments
- Understanding context reveals how catastrophic the failures were

**Source Rebuttal:**
- Full sermon context shows Taylor was addressing those who wanted to compromise, promising God would never allow it (JoD 25:309)
- Full sermon context shows Cannon offering divine consolation that church would never change (JoD 25:271)
- Context reinforces the prophecies, doesn't excuse them

---

### Defense #6: "Prophets Are Human and Can Make Mistakes"

**Why It Fails:**
- This defense concedes the entire point: prophets made false prophecies
- If "prophets make mistakes" about core doctrines from pulpit using divine authority claims, prophetic office is meaningless
- Creates impossible situation: How do members know which teachings are mistakes?
- These weren't minor errors—they were fundamental promises about church's future
- Brigham, Taylor, Cannon all claimed divine certainty, not human speculation

**Source Rebuttal:**
- "I cannot do it, and will not do it" (Taylor, JoD 25:309)
- "We have the promise of our God" (Cannon, JoD 25:271)
- "Abolitionists cannot help it, nor in the least alter that decree" (Brigham, JoD 7:290-291)
