# TOPIC: Cosmology
## Astronomical and Scientific Claims in the Journal of Discourses

---

## Overview

Early LDS prophets made specific claims about the sun, moon, and other celestial bodies that have been scientifically falsified. These claims were delivered with prophetic certainty, making them particularly significant for examining prophetic reliability.

**Why This Matters:**
- Claims were stated with absolute certainty ("No question of it")
- Scientific verification is objective and unambiguous
- Apologetic defenses ("personal speculation") are refuted by the certainty language
- Self-contradiction potential (e.g., defining "fanatics" as those who adopt false ideas, then exemplifying it)

## Key Findings

#### Finding 1: Brigham Young — "No Question Of It"
**Source:** JoD 13:271 (July 24, 1870)

> "So it is with regard to the inhabitants of the sun. Do you think it is inhabited? I rather think it is. Do you think there is any life there? No question of it; it was not made in vain."
> — Brigham Young

**Category:** FALSIFIED

**Context:** General discourse on God's creative purposes. Brigham was responding to rhetorical questions about celestial bodies, delivering answers with prophetic confidence.

**Why It Matters:**
- "No question of it" is certainty language, not speculation
- The sun is definitively not inhabited
- This was stated from the pulpit by the prophet

## Apologetic Responses

### Defense 1: "Personal Speculation, Not Doctrine"

**What They Say:** These were just Brigham's personal opinions, not prophetic pronouncements.

**Why It Fails:**
1. "No question of it" is certainty language, not speculation
2. Delivered from the pulpit during general discourse
3. JoD 13:95 claims sermons are Scripture
4. If prophets' confident public teachings can be dismissed as speculation, what CAN'T be?

**Source Rebuttal:**
> "No question of it; it was not made in vain."

The phrase "no question" eliminates speculation framing.

---

### Defense 2: "Product of His Time"

**What They Say:** 19th-century people commonly believed in moon/sun inhabitants. Brigham was just reflecting his culture.

**Why It Fails:**
1. Proves the point: teachings reflected culture, not revelation
2. A true prophet should transcend cultural errors
3. "No question of it" claims divine certainty, not cultural assumption
4. If prophets just echo cultural assumptions, what value is prophecy?

---

### Defense 3: "Prophets Are Human"

**What They Say:** Prophets make mistakes. This doesn't invalidate their calling.

**Why It Fails:**
1. The question is about reliability, not perfection
2. If "No question of it" can be wrong, how do we trust ANY confident prophetic claim?
3. The church teaches to "follow the prophet" without qualification
4. Creates impossible epistemology: trust them absolutely, but they might be absolutely wrong
