# TOPIC: Cosmology
## Nature of God, Creation, and the Universe in the Journal of Discourses

---

## Overview

Early LDS prophets made extensive claims about cosmology at two levels:

1. **Theological Cosmology** — The nature of God, creation, human potential for godhood, and the structure of reality
2. **Physical Cosmology** — Claims about celestial bodies (sun, moon, planets) that have been scientifically falsified

These teachings ranged from radical reimaginings of Christian theology to specific astronomical claims. Many have been scientifically falsified, doctrinally disavowed, or quietly abandoned by the modern church.

**Why This Matters:**
- Claims were stated with prophetic certainty ("No question of it")
- Theological claims represent fundamental departures from traditional Christianity
- Physical claims have been definitively falsified by science
- Modern church has distanced itself from most radical theological claims
- Apologetic defenses ("personal speculation") are refuted by the certainty language
- Self-contradiction potential (e.g., defining "fanatics" as those who adopt false ideas, then exemplifying it)

## Key Findings

### THEOLOGICAL COSMOLOGY

#### Finding 1: God Was Once a Mortal Man (King Follett Discourse)
**Source:** JoD 6:3 (April 7, 1844)
**Speaker:** Joseph Smith

> "God himself was once as we are now, and is an exalted Man, and sits enthroned in yonder heavens. That is the great secret. If the vail was rent to-day, and the great God who holds this world in its orbit, and who upholds all worlds and all things by his power, was to make himself visible—I say, if you were to see him to-day, you would see him like a man in form—like yourselves, in all the person, image, and very form as a man."
> — Joseph Smith, JoD 6:3

**Extended Teaching:**
> "In order to understand the subject of the dead, for the consolation of those who mourn for the loss of their friends, it is necessary that we should understand the character and being of God, and how he came to be so; for I am going to tell you how God came to be God. We have imagined and supposed that God was God from all eternity, I will refute that idea, and will take away and do away the vail, so that you may see."
> — Joseph Smith, JoD 6:3

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN / DISAVOWED (de-emphasized)

**Context:** Joseph Smith's funeral sermon for King Follett, delivered April 7, 1844, just two months before Joseph's death. This represents his most mature theological exposition and most radical departure from traditional Christianity.

**Modern Church Position:**
- Still teaches humans can become like God (exaltation)
- De-emphasizes that God was once mortal
- President Gordon B. Hinckley (1997): "I don't know that we teach it. I don't know that we emphasize it."
- Official statements distance church from "get your own planet" characterizations

#### Finding 2: God the Father Died and Was Resurrected
**Source:** JoD 6:3-4 (April 7, 1844)
**Speaker:** Joseph Smith

> "It is the first principle of the Gospel to know for a certainty the character of God and to know that we may converse with him as one man converses with another, and that he was once a man like us; yea, that God himself the Father of us all, dwelt on an earth, the same as Jesus Christ himself did; and I will show it from the Bible... Jesus, what are you going to do? To lay down my life, as my Father did, and take it up again."
> — Joseph Smith, JoD 6:3-4

**Category:** DISAVOWED / CONTRA-MODERN

**Context:** King Follett Discourse. Joseph argues that just as Jesus died and was resurrected, so too did God the Father.

**Modern Church Position:** This specific teaching is not emphasized and is often denied or minimized in modern church materials.

#### Finding 3: You Have Got to Learn to Be Gods Yourselves
**Source:** JoD 6:4 (April 7, 1844)
**Speaker:** Joseph Smith

> "Here, then, is eternal life—to know the only wise and true God; and you have got to learn how to be Gods yourselves, and to be kings and priests to God, the same as all Gods have done before you—namely, by going from one small degree to another, and from a small capacity to a great one—from grace to grace, from exaltation to exaltation, until you attain to the resurrection of the dead and are able to dwell in everlasting burnings and to sit in glory, as do those who sit enthroned in everlasting power."
> — Joseph Smith, JoD 6:4

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN (still taught but de-emphasized)

**Context:** The practical application of the doctrine. Note "all Gods have done before you" (plural), suggesting multiple Gods who have undergone this process.

**Modern Church Position:** Still teaches exaltation and becoming "like God," but avoids explicit claims about becoming separate gods or having spirit children on other worlds.

#### Finding 4: The Council of the Gods
**Source:** JoD 6:5 (April 7, 1844)
**Speaker:** Joseph Smith

> "In the beginning, the head of the Gods called a council of the Gods; and they came together and concocted a plan to create the world and people it."
> — Joseph Smith, JoD 6:5

**Category:** DISAVOWED / CONTRA-MODERN

**Context:** Joseph reinterprets Genesis 1 to argue for multiple Gods in council, with a "head God" presiding. Claims this is the correct translation of Hebrew "Elohim."

**Modern Church Position:** Modern church emphasizes monotheism in public teaching, though retains concept of divine council in more esoteric teachings.

---

#### Finding 5: God Never Had Power to Create Human Intelligence
**Source:** JoD 6:6 (April 7, 1844)
**Speaker:** Joseph Smith

> "I want to reason more on the spirit of man... As the Lord liveth, if it had a beginning it will have an end. All the fools and learned and wise men from the beginning of creation, who say that the spirit of man had a beginning, prove that it must have an end; and if that doctrine is true, then the doctrine of annihilation would be true. But if I am right, I might with boldness proclaim from the housetops that God never had the power to create the spirit of man at all. God himself could not create himself."
> — Joseph Smith, JoD 6:6

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN

**Context:** Joseph argues that human intelligence is co-eternal with God and uncreated. This limits God's power—he is an organizer of eternal intelligences, not their Creator.

**Modern Church Position:** Still teaches that intelligence is eternal (D&C 93:29), but rarely emphasizes the limitation on God's creative power.

---

#### Finding 6: God Instituted Laws for Other Intelligences to Progress
**Source:** JoD 6:6 (April 7, 1844)
**Speaker:** Joseph Smith

> "Intelligence is eternal and exists upon a self-existent principle. It is a spirit from age to age, and there is no creation about it. All the minds and spirits that God ever sent into the world are susceptible of enlargement. The first principles of man are self-existent with God. God himself, finding he was in the midst of spirits and glory, because he was more intelligent, saw proper to institute laws whereby the rest could have a privilege to advance like himself."
> — Joseph Smith, JoD 6:6

**Category:** DISAVOWED / CONTRA-MODERN

**Context:** This presents God not as an omnipotent Creator but as a more advanced intelligence who provides laws for others to follow his path. The phrase "finding he was in the midst of spirits" suggests God did not create these spirits but discovered them.

**Modern Church Position:** Rarely taught; presents theological problems about God's nature and omnipotence.

#### Finding 7: Children Remain Children Eternally
**Source:** JoD 6:9 (April 7, 1844)
**Speaker:** Joseph Smith

> "A question may be asked—'Will mothers have their children in eternity?' Yes! yes! Mothers, you shall have your children; for they shall have eternal life... But as the child dies, so shall it rise from the dead, and be for ever living in the learning of God. It will never grow: it will still be the child, in the same precise form as it appeared before it died out of its mother's arms, but possessing all the intelligence of a God. Children dwell in the mansions of glory and exercise power, but appear in the same form as when on earth. Eternity is full of thrones, upon which dwell thousands of children reigning on thrones of glory, with not one cubit added to their stature."
> — Joseph Smith, JoD 6:9

**Category:** BIZARRE / CONTRA-MODERN (not emphasized)

**Context:** Joseph offers comfort to grieving parents by teaching that children who die young remain children forever in form while possessing divine intelligence.

**Modern Church Position:** Not emphasized in modern teaching; raises troubling questions about eternal progression and embodiment.

> "So it is with regard to the inhabitants of the sun. Do you think it is inhabited? I rather think it is. Do you think there is any life there? No question of it; it was not made in vain."

**Category:** FALSIFIED

**Context:** General discourse on God's creative purposes. Brigham was responding to rhetorical questions about celestial bodies, delivering answers with prophetic confidence.

**Why It Matters:**
- "No question of it" is certainty language, not speculation
- The sun is definitively not inhabited

## Apologetic Responses

### Defense 1: "Personal Speculation, Not Doctrine"

**What They Say:** These were just Brigham's personal opinions, not prophetic pronouncements.

**Why It Fails:**
1. "No question of it" is certainty language, not speculation
2. Delivered from the pulpit during general discourse
3. JoD 13:95 claims sermons are Scripture
4. If prophets' confident public teachings can be dismissed as speculation, what CAN'T be?

**Source Rebuttal:**
> "No question of it; it was not made in vain."

The phrase "no question" eliminates speculation framing.

---

### Defense 2: "Product of His Time"

**What They Say:** 19th-century people commonly believed in moon/sun inhabitants. Brigham was just reflecting his culture.

**Why It Fails:**
1. Proves the point: teachings reflected culture, not revelation
2. A true prophet should transcend cultural errors
3. "No question of it" claims divine certainty, not cultural assumption
4. If prophets just echo cultural assumptions, what value is prophecy?

---

### Defense 3: "Prophets Are Human"

**What They Say:** Prophets make mistakes. This doesn't invalidate their calling.

**Why It Fails:**
1. The question is about reliability, not perfection
2. If "No question of it" can be wrong, how do we trust ANY confident prophetic claim?
3. The church teaches to "follow the prophet" without qualification
4. Creates impossible epistemology: trust them absolutely, but they might be absolutely wrong
