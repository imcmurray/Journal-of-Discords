## About This Project

### What Is the Journal of Discourses?

The Journal of Discourses is a 26-volume collection of sermons and speeches delivered by early leaders of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints between 1854 and 1886. The collection includes over 1,500 addresses by Brigham Young, John Taylor, Orson Pratt, Heber C. Kimball, George Q. Cannon, and other prominent leaders.

**Distribution:** The Journal was published in Liverpool, England and sold to members worldwide as a **subscription publication**—a semi-monthly periodical that members purchased to receive successive volumes. This made prophetic sermons available to international members who couldn't access the Deseret News.

**Official Endorsement:** The First Presidency (Brigham Young, Heber C. Kimball, and Willard Richards) issued a letter dated June 1, 1853 encouraging all Saints to purchase the publication. In 1861, apostle George Q. Cannon wrote in the preface to Volume 8:

> "The Journal of Discourses deservedly ranks as one of the standard works of the Church, and every rightminded Saint will certainly welcome with joy every Number as it comes forth from the press as an additional reflector of 'the light that shines from Zion's hill.'"

**Then and Now:** George Q. Cannon called the Journal "an additional reflector of 'the light that shines from Zion's hill.'" Today, the LDS Church states it "is not an authoritative source of Church doctrine." This raises an important question: if these teachings were authoritative enough to be sold as prophetic light to members worldwide for 35 years, shaping beliefs about race, marriage, and salvation across generations, what responsibility exists for the real-world impact of those teachings? This project documents what was taught—in the prophets' own words—so that history is not quietly revised away.

### Why This Project Exists

This project systematically documents statements from early LDS prophets that have been:
- **Scientifically falsified** (e.g., inhabited sun and moon)
- **Doctrinally disavowed** (e.g., Adam-God doctrine, racial curse theories)
- **Failed as prophecy** (e.g., "the church will never abandon polygamy")
- **Self-contradicting** (e.g., polygamy required for godhood vs. current temple practice)

The goal is historical accuracy: letting the prophets speak for themselves through carefully sourced, contextually faithful documentation.

### Methodology

Each song in this project follows a rigorous process:

1. **Research Phase**: Survey all 26 volumes using the Research Agent to identify potentially significant sermons
2. **Discovery Phase**: Full-text reading of flagged sermons to find unusual, striking, or doctrinally problematic content
3. **Analysis Phase**: Deep-dive documentation of the most significant findings ("crown jewels"), including exact quotes, context, apologetic responses, and cross-references
4. **Song Development**: Transform analyzed findings into historically accurate songs with complete lyric-to-source mapping

Every claim traces to a specific JoD volume, page, date, and speaker. Original spelling and punctuation are preserved. Context is documented. Apologetic defenses are anticipated and addressed.

### What We Found

After surveying all 26 volumes (approximately 1,500 sermons), we identified 9 "crown jewel" findings—statements so significant they warranted full song development:

- **3 on race**: Death penalty for interracial marriage, blacks as "devil's representation," physical curse of Cain
- **4 on polygamy**: Only polygamists become Gods, monogamy as "robbery," church will "never" abandon it
- **2 on theology**: King Follett Discourse (God was once a man), endowments creating "devils"

All 9 songs are complete with full source documentation.
