## SOURCE MATERIAL

### Primary Quote 1: The Central Claim

> "God himself was once as we are now, and is an exalted Man, and sits enthroned in yonder heavens. That is the great secret."
> — Joseph Smith Jr., JoD 6:3 (April 7, 1844)

### Primary Quote 2: The Promise to Reveal God's Origin

> "In order to understand the subject of the dead, for the consolation of those who mourn for the loss of their friends, it is necessary that we should understand the character and being of God, and how he came to be so; for I am going to tell you how God came to be God."
> — Joseph Smith Jr., JoD 6:3 (April 7, 1844)

### Primary Quote 3: God Died and Rose

> "That God himself the Father of us all, dwelt on an earth, the same as Jesus Christ himself did... Jesus, what are you going to do? To lay down my life, as my Father did, and take it up again."
> — Joseph Smith Jr., JoD 6:3-4 (April 7, 1844)

### Primary Quote 4: Learning to Be Gods

> "Here, then, is eternal life—to know the only wise and true God; and you have got to learn how to be Gods yourselves, and to be kings and priests to God, the same as all Gods have done before you."
> — Joseph Smith Jr., JoD 6:4 (April 7, 1844)

### Primary Quote 5: Council of the Gods

> "In the beginning, the head of the Gods called a council of the Gods; and they came together and concocted a plan to create the world and people it."
> — Joseph Smith Jr., JoD 6:5 (April 7, 1844)

### Primary Quote 6: God's Limitations

> "I might with boldness proclaim from the housetops that God never had the power to create the spirit of man at all. God himself could not create himself."
> — Joseph Smith Jr., JoD 6:6 (April 7, 1844)

### Primary Quote 7: Eternal Intelligence

> "Intelligence is eternal and exists upon a self-existent principle... God himself, finding he was in the midst of spirits and glory, because he was more intelligent, saw proper to institute laws whereby the rest could have a privilege to advance like himself."
> — Joseph Smith Jr., JoD 6:6 (April 7, 1844)

### Primary Quote 8: Joseph's Authority Claim

> "I have an old edition of the New Testament in the Hebrew, Latin, German, and Greek languages... I am learned, and know more than all the world put together."
> — Joseph Smith Jr., JoD 6:4-5 (April 7, 1844)

### Primary Quote 9: Staking His Prophetic Call

> "I shall comment on the very first Hebrew word in the Bible; I will make a comment on the very first sentence of the history of creation in the Bible... if I do not do it, I should like to know who can; and if I fail to do it, it becomes my duty to renounce all further pretensions to revelations, inspirations, or to be a Prophet."
> — Joseph Smith Jr., JoD 6:3 (April 7, 1844)

### Primary Quote 10: Modern Church Response

> "I don't know that we teach it. I don't know that we emphasize it... I understand the philosophical background behind it, but I don't know a lot about it, and I don't think others know a lot about it."
> — Gordon B. Hinckley, President of LDS Church, Time Magazine interview (1997)

## HISTORICAL CONTEXT

### The Setting

April 7, 1844. An outdoor conference near the nearly-completed Nauvoo Temple. Joseph Smith stood before an estimated 8,000-20,000 people—the largest audience of his prophetic career. The occasion was ostensibly a funeral sermon for King Follett, a church member who had died in a well accident. But Joseph used the opportunity to deliver his most radical and comprehensive theological statement.

### The Speaker

Joseph Smith Jr., founder of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. At this point, he was at the height of his power—prophet, mayor of Nauvoo, candidate for President of the United States, and commander of the Nauvoo Legion. He had introduced plural marriage secretly and was preparing his most ambitious theological innovations. This sermon came exactly 81 days before his death at Carthage Jail on June 27, 1844.

### The Broader Context

Spring 1844 was a moment of crisis and expansion for the church. Joseph faced growing opposition from former followers (the Nauvoo Expositor would be published in June), political enemies, and continued persecution. His presidential campaign was underway. The temple was nearly complete. Secret practices (plural marriage, the Council of Fifty) were creating internal tensions. Against this backdrop, Joseph chose to reveal what he called "the great secret"—his most radical reimagining of God and human destiny.

### Why It Matters

The King Follett Discourse is the single most important sermon in Mormon theological history because it:
1. Redefined God from eternal Creator to exalted man who once lived on another planet
2. Moved Mormonism from functional monotheism to explicit polytheism ("head of the Gods called a council of the Gods")
3. Made human apotheosis (becoming gods) the central purpose of existence
4. Limited God's power (cannot create spirits, cannot create ex nihilo)
5. Directly contradicted the Book of Mormon's monotheism
6. Separated Mormonism definitively from mainstream Christianity
7. Was given with Joseph explicitly staking his prophetic calling on its truth

### The Aftermath

The doctrine was celebrated and taught throughout 19th-century Mormonism:
- Four official scribes recorded it (Willard Richards, Wilford Woodruff, Thomas Bullock, William Clayton)
- Published in church periodicals and the Journal of Discourses
- Codified by Lorenzo Snow in his famous couplet: "As man now is, God once was; as God now is, man may be"
- Explicitly taught by Brigham Young and subsequent church presidents

The 20th century brought quiet de-emphasis:
- Less prominent in missionary materials
- Omitted from investigator-focused publications
- 1997: President Gordon B. Hinckley, when asked directly by Time Magazine about the doctrine, replied: "I don't know that we teach it. I don't know that we emphasize it... I don't know a lot about it."

The modern church cannot fully repudiate the sermon without undermining Joseph's prophetic authority (he staked his calling on it), but cannot fully embrace it without appearing bizarre and polytheistic to modern investigators.

## ADDRESSING APOLOGETIC RESPONSES

### Defense #1: "This was metaphorical, not literal"

**What They Say:**
Joseph was speaking poetically about humans becoming like God in character and moral development, similar to Christian concepts of sanctification. He didn't literally mean God was once a mortal man or that we become separate gods.

**Why It Fails:**
The specificity and explicitness of Joseph's language defeats the metaphorical reading. He claimed to explain "how God came to be God"—not how we become more godly. He stated God "dwelt on an earth, the same as Jesus Christ himself did" and that God "laid down his body... and took it up again." These are specific historical claims about God's biography, not metaphors for spiritual growth.

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "I am going to tell you how God came to be God... God himself the Father of us all, dwelt on an earth, the same as Jesus Christ himself did."
> — JoD 6:3

> "The head of the Gods called a council of the Gods; and they came together and concocted a plan to create the world and people it."
> — JoD 6:5

---

### Defense #2: "This was speculation, not official doctrine"

**What They Say:**
Joseph was theorizing at a funeral, not delivering binding revelation. Prophets can have personal opinions that aren't official church doctrine.

**Why It Fails:**
Joseph explicitly staked his prophetic calling on this teaching. He declared that if he failed to prove it from scripture, "it becomes my duty to renounce all further pretensions to revelations, inspirations, or to be a Prophet." This wasn't speculation—it was a test of his prophetic authority by his own declaration. Furthermore:
- Delivered at General Conference, not a private setting
- Four official scribes recorded it as important doctrine
- Published in official church literature
- Taught as doctrine by subsequent church presidents for over a century

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "If I fail to do it, it becomes my duty to renounce all further pretensions to revelations, inspirations, or to be a Prophet."
> — JoD 6:3

---

### Defense #3: "Modern revelation has superseded this"

**What They Say:**
The church receives continuing revelation. The prophets may have received new light that clarifies or updates this teaching.

**Why It Fails:**
No official revelation has contradicted or superseded the King Follett Discourse. The church still publishes the sermon in its official manuals (Teachings of the Presidents of the Church: Joseph Smith). President Hinckley's "I don't know that we teach it" is an evasion, not a revelatory correction. If the teaching is false, the church should say so explicitly—and explain why Joseph was wrong about something he staked his prophetic calling on. Strategic silence is not doctrinal correction.

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "I don't know that we teach it. I don't know that we emphasize it... I don't know a lot about it."
> — Gordon B. Hinckley, Time Magazine, 1997

If it's been superseded, why not say so clearly? The evasion reveals embarrassment, not correction.

---

### Defense #4: "You're taking this out of context"

**What They Say:**
Critics cherry-pick quotes without understanding the broader context of the sermon or Joseph's other teachings.

**Why It Fails:**
The context strengthens the case, not weakens it. The sermon was:
- Delivered at General Conference to 8,000-20,000 people
- Recorded by four official scribes
- Published and celebrated for over a century
- Explicitly framed by Joseph as a revelation of "the great secret"
- Given as comfort to grieving parents (they could become gods like their children would)

Reading the full sermon reveals even more radical claims, not fewer.

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "In order to understand the subject of the dead, for the consolation of those who mourn for the loss of their friends, it is necessary that we should understand the character and being of God, and how he came to be so."
> — JoD 6:3

---

### Defense #5: "This is just like Christian theosis/deification"

**What They Say:**
Eastern Orthodox Christianity teaches theosis (deification), so human beings becoming divine is not unique to Mormonism or incompatible with Christianity.

**Why It Fails:**
Orthodox theosis and Joseph's teaching are fundamentally different:
- Orthodox: Humans participate in divine nature while remaining creatures; God is eternally uncreated
- Joseph: Humans become separate gods with their own worlds; God was once a mortal creature who achieved godhood

No Christian denomination teaches:
- That God was once a mortal man on another planet
- That multiple gods exist in council
- That God cannot create ex nihilo
- That intelligence is co-eternal with God and was not created by Him

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "The head of the Gods called a council of the Gods"
> — JoD 6:5

> "God never had the power to create the spirit of man at all"
> — JoD 6:6

---

### Defense #6: "President Hinckley was just being cautious with outsiders"

**What They Say:**
Hinckley knew the doctrine could be misunderstood by non-members, so he downplayed it to avoid confusion. He wasn't denying it.

**Why It Fails:**
This defense admits the problem: the church's founding prophet's central teaching is now considered too embarrassing to acknowledge publicly. Whether Hinckley was lying, evading, or genuinely ignorant, the effect is the same—the "great secret" Joseph proclaimed with such confidence is now treated as something to hide from investigators. If Joseph was right, why be ashamed? If he was wrong about something he staked his prophetic authority on, what does that mean for his other claims?

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
Joseph, 1844: "That is the great secret... you have got to learn how to be Gods yourselves"
Hinckley, 1997: "I don't know that we teach it"

The juxtaposition speaks for itself.

## THE LOGICAL STRUCTURE

1. **Joseph's Claim:** "God himself was once as we are now" is THE central truth of existence—"the great secret"
2. **Joseph's Stakes:** He explicitly bet his prophetic calling on this teaching ("if I fail... I renounce all pretensions to be a Prophet")
3. **Historical Reality:** The teaching was published, celebrated, and taught as official doctrine for over a century
4. **Modern Reality:** The church's president in 1997 claimed not to know much about it and denied it's taught or emphasized
5. **The Contradiction:** Either Joseph was right (and Hinckley was lying/ignorant about the church's core teaching) or Joseph was wrong (and failed his own test of prophetic authority)

**The Self-Refuting Element:**
Joseph set the terms himself: "If I fail to do it, it becomes my duty to renounce all further pretensions to revelations, inspirations, or to be a Prophet." The modern church's embarrassed retreat from this teaching implicitly admits Joseph failed—yet they cannot say so without undermining the entire prophetic foundation of the church.

## PRODUCER NOTES

### What This Song Does

This song presents Joseph Smith's King Follett Discourse—the most important and radical sermon in Mormon history—through his own words, then contrasts his confident proclamations with the modern church's embarrassed evasion. The irony is not manufactured; it emerges from the historical record itself.

### Key Production Decisions

1. **Extended version prioritized:** The 4+ minute version allows full development of the theological claims, Joseph's authority staking, the historical timeline, and the modern contrast. The material demands the space.

2. **Epic/theatrical style chosen:** The subject matter is cosmic in scope—gods, councils, human apotheosis, the nature of ultimate reality. The musical treatment matches the grandiosity of Joseph's claims, making the contrast with modern sheepishness more stark.

3. **Direct quotes emphasized:** By using Joseph's actual words ("God himself was once as we are now," "you have got to learn how to be Gods yourselves"), the song is defensible against claims of distortion. We let Joseph speak for himself.

4. **Bridge as tonal pivot:** The stripped-down bridge (Hinckley quotes) creates dramatic contrast with the epic verses and choruses. The music reflects the institutional shrinking back.

5. **Ironic parentheticals in final chorus:** Rather than tell the audience what to think, the parenthetical asides let the contradiction speak for itself.

### Potential Challenges

- Length may limit radio/social play (mitigated by alternate shorter versions available in candidate file)
- Theological complexity may require visual/textual support for non-Mormon audiences
- Maintaining respect for the subject matter while presenting the irony
- Ensuring Hinckley quotes are delivered with appropriate weight (spoken-word quality)

### Comparable Works

- Queen's "Bohemian Rhapsody" (theatrical shifts, emotional range, unconventional structure)
- Muse's "Uprising" / "Knights of Cydonia" (epic scope, cosmic themes)
- Ghost's catalog (religious themes with ironic edge, theatrical presentation)
- Dream Theater's conceptual works (progressive structure, dramatic storytelling)
