## SOURCE MATERIAL

### Primary Quote 1: The Only Men Who Become Gods

> "The only men who become Gods, even the Sons of God, are those who enter into polygamy. Others attain unto a glory and may even be permitted to come into the presence of the Father and the Son; but they cannot reign as kings in glory, because they had blessings offered unto them, and they refused to accept them."
> -- Brigham Young, JoD 11:269 (August 19, 1866)

### Primary Quote 2: Polygamists at Least in Your Faith

> "I wish to say to you, and all the world, that if you desire with all your hearts to obtain the blessings which Abraham obtained, you will be polygamists at least in your faith, or you will come short of enjoying the salvation and the glory which Abraham has obtained. This is as true as that God lives."
> -- Brigham Young, JoD 11:269 (August 19, 1866)

### Primary Quote 3: I Know It Is From God

> "I heard the revelation on polygamy, and I believed it with all my heart, and I know it is from God--I know that he revealed it from heaven; I know that it is true, and understand the bearings of it and why it is."
> -- Brigham Young, JoD 11:270 (August 19, 1866)

### Primary Quote 4: The Failed Statehood Prophecy

> "Do you think that we shall ever be admitted as a State into the Union without denying the principle of polygamy? If we are not admitted until then, we shall never be admitted."
> -- Brigham Young, JoD 11:270 (August 19, 1866)

### Primary Quote 5: The Lord Will Conquer

> "The Government of the United States do not intend that that prophecy shall be fulfilled, and the Lord Almighty means that it shall. Do you not think that the Lord will conquer?"
> -- Brigham Young, JoD 11:271 (August 19, 1866)

### Primary Quote 6: For the Resurrection

> "What is this order for? It is for the resurrection; it is not for this world. I would not go across this bowery for polygamy, if it only pertained to this world. It is for the resurrection."
> -- Brigham Young, JoD 11:271 (August 19, 1866)

### Primary Quote 7: Those Who Wish It Didn't Exist

> "You who wish that there were no such thing in existence, if you have in your hearts to say: 'We will pass along in the Church without obeying or submitting to it in our faith or believing this order, because, for aught that we know, this community may be broken up yet, and we may have lucrative offices offered to us; we will not, therefore, be polygamists lest we should fail in obtaining some earthly honor, character and office, etc,'--the man that has that in his heart, and will continue to persist in pursuing that policy, will come short of dwelling in the presence of the Father and the Son, in celestial glory."
> -- Brigham Young, JoD 11:269 (August 19, 1866)

## HISTORICAL CONTEXT

### The Setting

August 19, 1866 -- The Bowery, Great Salt Lake City. An outdoor tabernacle where Brigham Young delivers a Sunday sermon to the general membership. The setting is formal, the record official. G.D. Watt reports the sermon for publication in the Journal of Discourses.

The sermon begins with praise for Delegate Hooper's service in Washington, then transitions into an extended defense and doctrinal exposition of polygamy--the bulk of the sermon (60%+).

### The Speaker

Brigham Young -- Second President of the LDS Church (1847-1877), Prophet, Seer, and Revelator. The most prolific speaker in the Journal of Discourses. His words were published as official church record and treated as authoritative teaching by the membership.

In this sermon, Brigham speaks with maximum prophetic authority:
- "I KNOW it is from God"
- "I KNOW that he revealed it from heaven"
- "I understand the bearings of it and why it is"
- "This is as true as that God lives"

### The Broader Context

**1866** -- Six years after the Republican platform called polygamy and slavery the "twin relics of barbarism." With slavery abolished (1865), polygamy was the next federal target. The Morrill Anti-Bigamy Act (1862) had been passed but not yet enforced. Brigham was preparing the Saints for sustained federal pressure.

Brigham acknowledges the internal struggle: "it is in the hearts of many of them to wish that the doctrine of polygamy was not taught and practiced by us." Some Saints hoped to "pass along in the Church" without polygamy to gain "lucrative offices" and respectability. This describes exactly what the modern church would do.

### Why It Matters

This is the definitive statement on polygamy as salvation requirement:

1. **Categorical absolute:** "The ONLY men who become Gods are those who enter into polygamy"
2. **Ultimate authority claim:** "This is as true as that God lives"
3. **Complete prophetic certainty:** "I KNOW it is from God"
4. **Failed prophecy attached:** "We shall never be admitted" without denying polygamy
5. **Modern contradiction:** Monogamous couples now receive temple sealings promising exaltation

Every modern temple-married monogamous LDS couple exists in direct contradiction to this teaching. Either Brigham was catastrophically wrong about salvation requirements, or the modern church is making false promises.

### The Aftermath

**1890 (24 years later):** Wilford Woodruff issues the Manifesto suspending polygamy. The church does exactly what Brigham condemned--"passes along without polygamy" to gain respectability and avoid federal prosecution.

**1896 (30 years later):** Utah granted statehood. Brigham's absolute "never" becomes the literal path to statehood. The church denied polygamy and was admitted.

**1904:** Second Manifesto. Church excommunicates new polygamists.

**Present Day:**
- Monogamous temple sealings promise exaltation
- D&C 132 (commanding polygamy) remains canonized scripture
- Polygamists are excommunicated
- Modern members are not required to believe in polygamy as eternal law

The transformation:
- "ONLY polygamists become Gods" -> monogamists promised exaltation
- "We shall never deny it" -> they denied it
- "The Lord will conquer" -> the government won
- "As true as God lives" -> completely reversed

## ADDRESSING APOLOGETIC RESPONSES

### Defense #1: "He was speaking as a man, not as a prophet"

**What They Say:**
Brigham was expressing personal opinion, not prophetic revelation. Prophets can have personal views that aren't binding doctrine.

**Why It Fails:**
- He explicitly claims divine knowledge: "I KNOW it is from God--I know that he revealed it from heaven"
- He invokes God's existence as authority: "This is as true as that God lives"
- Formal sermon from the pulpit, published in official Journal of Discourses
- He claims complete understanding: "I understand the bearings of it and why it is"
- He addresses "all the world" with prophetic authority

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "I heard the revelation on polygamy, and I believed it with all my heart, and I know it is from God--I know that he revealed it from heaven; I know that it is true, and understand the bearings of it and why it is."
> -- JoD 11:270

If this isn't prophetic teaching, then NOTHING Brigham said can be trusted as prophetic teaching.

---

### Defense #2: "Polygamy is still doctrine, just not practiced"

**What They Say:**
Eternal polygamy is still LDS doctrine--men can be sealed to multiple wives after a spouse's death. It's just not practiced on earth right now.

**Why It Fails:**
- Brigham requires EARTHLY entry: "Those who ENTER INTO polygamy" (present tense, mortal action)
- Modern monogamists are promised godhood: Current teaching is that monogamous temple marriage leads to exaltation
- The contradiction remains: If ONLY polygamists become Gods, monogamists cannot
- "At least in your faith" requirement: Brigham requires belief in polygamy as eternal law; modern church doesn't
- The church actively excommunicates polygamists: Contradicts the "still doctrine" claim

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "The only men who become Gods, even the Sons of God, are those who enter into polygamy. Others attain unto a glory and may even be permitted to come into the presence of the Father and the Son; but they cannot reign as kings in glory."
> -- JoD 11:269

"ONLY" is categorical. Monogamous men "cannot reign as kings in glory."

---

### Defense #3: "Continuing revelation superseded it"

**What They Say:**
The 1890 Manifesto was revelation that superseded the earlier requirement. God can change his requirements.

**Why It Fails:**
- The Manifesto never claims to be revelation: It's a public policy statement, not "thus saith the Lord"
- D&C 132 is still canon: The revelation commanding polygamy remains scripture
- Undermines prophetic trust: If "as true as God lives" can be reversed, nothing is trustworthy
- Brigham said "I know": Not conditional but absolute knowledge claim

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "This is as true as that God lives."
> -- JoD 11:269

Either:
- Brigham was wrong about what he "knew"
- God changed an "eternal principle" (see Quote 6: "for the resurrection")
- The modern church is wrong

All three options are problems for prophetic authority.

---

### Defense #4: "It was conditional based on circumstances"

**What They Say:**
Polygamy was required under certain historical circumstances but not as a universal, eternal law.

**Why It Fails:**
- Brigham makes it absolute: "ONLY men who enter into polygamy"--no conditions, no exceptions
- He ties it to Abraham: "The blessings which Abraham obtained"--presented as eternal pattern
- "For the resurrection, not for this world": Brigham explicitly says it's eternal, not temporal
- "As true as God lives": No hedging, no conditional language

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "What is this order for? It is for the resurrection; it is not for this world. I would not go across this bowery for polygamy, if it only pertained to this world. It is for the resurrection."
> -- JoD 11:271

Brigham explicitly teaches this is ETERNAL, not circumstantial.

---

### Defense #5: "You're taking it out of context"

**What They Say:**
The quote is cherry-picked without understanding the full sermon context.

**Why It Fails:**
- The full context makes it worse: The entire middle section is about polygamy as eternal requirement
- Multiple reinforcing quotes throughout the sermon, not one cherry-picked line
- The audience understood it as requirement: Brigham acknowledges Saints struggle with this teaching
- Published in official record as authoritative teaching
- Consistent with D&C 132: "if ye abide not that covenant, then are ye damned"

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
Read the full sermon. The context includes:
- Defense against government pressure
- Teaching that polygamy is required for exaltation
- Condemning those who hope it will go away
- Prophesying the church will never abandon it
- Invoking God's existence as guarantee of truth

The full context makes the problem worse, not better.

---

### Defense #6: "The government forced them to stop"

**What They Say:**
The church had to abandon polygamy due to federal prosecution--it wasn't a theological change.

**Why It Fails:**
- Brigham prophesied they'd never yield: "If we are not admitted until then, we shall never be admitted"
- This admits defeat by secular power: God's "eternal law" overridden by Congress?
- Contradicts "The Lord will conquer": Brigham asked "Do you not think that the Lord will conquer?"--the Lord didn't
- Makes it pragmatic, not revelatory: If it was just political surrender, it's not "continuing revelation"

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "The Government of the United States do not intend that that prophecy shall be fulfilled, and the Lord Almighty means that it shall. Do you not think that the Lord will conquer?"
> -- JoD 11:271

The Lord did not conquer. The government won. The church abandoned polygamy exactly as Brigham prophesied they "never" would.

---

### Defense #7: "We don't understand the mysteries fully"

**What They Say:**
The nature of exaltation and eternal marriage are mysteries we don't fully comprehend. We have to trust that God knows best.

**Why It Fails:**
- Brigham claimed to understand fully: "I understand the bearings of it and why it is"
- The prophets understood it well enough to require it: Can't claim mystery when they were categorical
- "ONLY" is not mysterious: The word is exclusionary and clear
- "As true as God lives" leaves no room for mystery
- This defense is an admission: "We can't explain it"

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "I heard the revelation on polygamy, and I believed it with all my heart, and I know it is from God--I know that he revealed it from heaven; I know that it is true, and understand the bearings of it and why it is."
> -- JoD 11:270

The mystery defense contradicts Brigham's claim to complete understanding.

## THE LOGICAL STRUCTURE

1. **Premise 1:** Brigham Young teaches with absolute prophetic authority that "the ONLY men who become Gods are those who enter into polygamy"
2. **Premise 2:** He anchors this to God's existence: "This is as true as that God lives"
3. **Premise 3:** He prophesies the church will never abandon polygamy: "We shall never be admitted" without denying it
4. **Premise 4:** In 1890, the church abandoned polygamy under government pressure
5. **Premise 5:** In 1896, Utah was admitted to statehood--exactly what Brigham said would "never" happen
6. **Premise 6:** Today, monogamous temple-married couples are promised exaltation
7. **Conclusion:** Either Brigham was catastrophically wrong about what he "knew" was from God, or the modern church is making false promises about exaltation

**The Self-Refuting Element:**

Brigham condemns those who "wish that there were no such thing in existence" and hope to "pass along in the Church without obeying or submitting to it." He declares they "will come short of dwelling in the presence of the Father and the Son."

This exactly describes the modern LDS church--they wished polygamy didn't exist, abandoned it for respectability, and by Brigham's own standard "come short."

The institution condemns itself by its founding prophet's own words.

## PRODUCER NOTES

### What This Song Does

This song presents Brigham Young's teaching on polygamy requirement with his full prophetic authority claims, then juxtaposes it against the modern church's complete reversal. By letting Brigham's words carry the weight--"I KNOW it is from God," "as true as God lives," "we shall never be admitted"--and then showing the historical outcome (1890 Manifesto, 1896 statehood, modern monogamous sealings), the song lets the institution condemn itself.

The hook--"The only men who become Gods"--is devastating because it's categorical. Not "polygamy is better" but "ONLY." Every monogamous LDS couple exists in direct contradiction.

### Key Production Decisions

1. **Play Brigham's lines straight, with authority:** Let the prophetic certainty come through. No mockery in the delivery--the words are devastating on their own.

2. **The shift to modern confusion:** Verse 2's temple couple section should feel different--quieter, questioning. They're promised what Brigham said they cannot have.

3. **The bridge is the pivot:** The spoken-word prophecy ("We shall never be admitted") followed by the historical reversal ("Eighteen ninety, gave it up") is the structural heart.

4. **End with questions, not mockery:** "One of them was wrong" lets the listener reach the conclusion. The haunting "as true as God lives" refrain invites them to feel the weight.

5. **Dark but not cruel:** This is about institutional self-contradiction, not attacking individual believers. The song exposes the problem; it doesn't mock the people affected.

### Potential Challenges

- **Subject sensitivity:** Modern monogamous LDS couples are faithful people who were taught they can attain exaltation. The song tells them their church's founding prophet said otherwise. Must balance truth-telling with awareness.

- **Complexity of reversal:** Need to communicate 1890 Manifesto and 1896 statehood efficiently without losing momentum. "Eighteen ninety, gave it up / Eighteen ninety-six, statehood won" is minimal but clear.

- **D&C 132 canon issue:** Complex point (scripture commanding polygamy is still canon while practice is prohibited). "The scripture stays, the practice goes" captures it in one couplet.

- **Multiple strong hooks:** "Only men who become Gods," "as true as God lives," "we shall never be admitted," "the Lord will conquer"--picking the primary hook (recommend "Only men who become Gods" as chorus) while using others as supporting lines.

- Hozier, "Take Me to Church" -- Dark spiritual critique with anthemic build
- The Killers, "Human" -- Questioning faith with stadium rock energy
- Johnny Cash, "God's Gonna Cut You Down" -- Weighty, prophetic, heavy
