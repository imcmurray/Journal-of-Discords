> "What will you do with all those who have sought to kill you? Make them soap-boilers and kitchen flunkeys. We are not going to send them into hell fire, for it takes a good Latter-day Saint apostatized to get down into that deep (did I say bottomless?) pit."

## HISTORICAL CONTEXT

### The Setting

September 9, 1860 — The Bowery and Historian's Office, Great Salt Lake City. Brigham Young is delivering instructions to missionaries about to depart on their missions. The sermon, titled "Light of the Spirit—Course of Missionaries," primarily addresses missionary conduct, the importance of avoiding materialism, and bringing home converts rather than silk dresses.

### The Speaker

Brigham Young — Second President of the LDS Church (1847-1877), Prophet, Seer, and Revelator. He supervised the administration of temple endowments in the Nauvoo Temple (1840s) and established the Endowment House in Salt Lake City (opened 1855). As the leader who oversaw temple ordinances for decades, his understanding of their purpose carries significant weight.

### The Broader Context

**Political Context:**
- Buchanan's Utah War (1857-58) had ended just two years prior
- Federal tensions remained high over polygamy and theocracy
- Missionaries were being sent into a hostile national environment

**Temple Context:**
- Temple endowments had been administered in Nauvoo Temple (1840s)
- Endowment House in Salt Lake City had been operating since 1855
- Temple doctrine was mature, not new or speculative
- Sons of perdition doctrine established in D&C 76

**The Passage:**
The sermon starts practically, instructing missionaries about conduct. Near the end (pages 181-182), Brigham ventures into speculative cosmic theology about his future glory (wives by the million, kingdom after kingdom), the fate of enemies (soap-boilers), and finally the bizarre revelation about temple endowments creating devils.

### Why It Matters

This statement is extraordinary for several reasons:

1. **Sacred Subject:** Temple endowments are the LDS church's most sacred ordinances
2. **Dual Purpose Claim:** Brigham says endowments serve to create both saints AND devils
3. **Cosmic Necessity:** God's plan requires "a host of devils" — this is functional, not accidental
4. **No Modern Equivalent:** The church never teaches this purpose today
5. **Personal Impact:** Every member knows people who received endowments and left

### The Aftermath

**Modern Teaching:**
Temple preparation materials emphasize:
- Making sacred covenants with God
- Receiving instruction for exaltation
- Drawing closer to God
- Preparation for eternal families

**Never Mentioned:**
- Creating devils
- Cosmic necessity of apostasy
- Dual purpose of ordinances

**The Contrast:**
| Brigham Young (1860) | Modern LDS Church |
|---------------------|------------------|
| Endowments create devils | Endowments prepare for exaltation |
| Devils are necessary to God's plan | Opposition exists but not emphasized as divine creation |
| "Host of devils will be needed" | Sons of perdition extremely rare |
| Endowments given knowing recipients will apostatize | Endowments given with hope of faithfulness |

## ADDRESSING APOLOGETIC RESPONSES

### Defense #1: "He Was Speaking Hyperbolically"

**What They Say:**
Brigham was using exaggerated, colorful language to make a point. He didn't literally mean God gives endowments to create devils. He was emphasizing that people have agency and some will unfortunately choose to reject their covenants.

**Why It Fails:**
The statement is too specific and theological to be hyperbole:
- "It is to make devils of those who will deny the faith" — active purpose statement
- "For that is also necessary" — theological claim about cosmic necessity
- "As a host of devils will be needed" — functional requirement in God's plan
- Embedded in larger cosmological discussion (kingdoms, opposition, glory)
- No markers of hyperbole (no "as it were," "figuratively speaking," etc.)
- Question-answer format suggests doctrinal explanation, not rhetorical flourish

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "Do you query why we give endowments to A., B., and C? **It is to make devils of those who will deny the faith, for that is also necessary**, as a host of devils will be needed."
> — JoD 8:181-182

---

### Defense #2: "Out of Context"

**What They Say:**
This is one sentence pulled out of a long sermon about missionary work. The main topic was missionaries avoiding materialism. This brief cosmological aside doesn't represent the sermon's purpose or Brigham's considered doctrine.

**Why It Fails:**
1. The context actually makes it worse, not better
2. The statement comes in a section about future glory and kingdoms
3. It's connected to established doctrine (sons of perdition, outer darkness)
4. The broader passage includes other bizarre claims (wives by the million, enemies as servants)
5. Being a "brief aside" doesn't make false doctrine acceptable
6. Prophets should be especially careful about teaching on sacred ordinances

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
The full context shows this is part of Brigham's cosmic theology, not a slip of the tongue. He's discussing future kingdoms, the fate of different groups, and making theological claims about necessity of opposition.

---

### Defense #3: "He Meant Possibility, Not Intent"

**What They Say:**
Brigham wasn't saying God INTENDS to make devils through endowments. He was saying that endowments, by elevating people to higher knowledge and covenants, create the possibility for higher rebellion. Like saying 'we give freedom to let people become criminals if they choose.'

**Why It Fails:**
1. The text says "It is TO MAKE devils" (purpose), not "It creates the possibility"
2. "That is also necessary" — suggests intentional design, not mere side effect
3. "A host of devils will be needed" — functional requirement, not just possibility
4. The parallel to freedom/criminality fails because we don't say "we give freedom TO MAKE criminals"
5. If Brigham meant "possibility," he would have said so

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "It is **to make** devils of those who will deny the faith, for **that is also necessary**."
> — JoD 8:181-182

"To make" = purpose. "Also necessary" = dual function. This is intentional design, not accidental byproduct.

---

### Defense #4: "Opposition in All Things"

**What They Say:**
This fits LDS doctrine that opposition is necessary. 2 Nephi 2:11 says 'it must needs be, that there is an opposition in all things.' Brigham was teaching that God's plan requires opposition, and devils provide that opposition. This isn't bizarre; it's standard LDS theology.

**Why It Fails:**
1. "Opposition in all things" doesn't require CREATING devils through sacred ordinances
2. Opposition can exist without God instrumentally making devils
3. The Book of Mormon teaches Satan and his hosts provide opposition (pre-mortal rebellion)
4. No scripture teaches that temple ordinances serve to create devils
5. This defense validates the bizarre claim instead of explaining it away
6. If accepted, every endowment session includes people who will become devils by design

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
The issue isn't opposition theology; it's the MECHANISM. Brigham claims temple endowments are the tool for creating devils. That's not in 2 Nephi 2, D&C 76, or any modern teaching.

---

### Defense #5: "Foreknowledge, Not Predestination"

**What They Say:**
God knows who will deny the faith, but He doesn't cause it. Brigham was saying that God, knowing some endowment recipients will apostatize, still gives them endowments because agency requires the possibility of apostasy.

**Why It Fails:**
1. This defense still admits God gives ordinances knowing they'll create devils
2. "It is to make devils" is active causation, not passive foreknowledge
3. If God knows someone will become a devil, why give them higher knowledge/damnation?
4. The text says endowments are given "TO MAKE devils," not "despite knowing some will become devils"
5. This defense raises more problems: why not withhold endowments from future apostates?

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "Do you query why we give endowments to A., B., and C? It is **to make devils** of those who **will** deny the faith."
> — JoD 8:181

"Will deny" suggests foreknowledge, but "to make" suggests purpose. Both together create theological nightmare.

---

### Defense #6: "Brigham Was Speculating, Not Teaching Doctrine"

**What They Say:**
Brigham often speculated about theology. Not everything he said was official doctrine. This was likely personal speculation about the mechanics of opposition and apostasy, not binding teaching about endowments.

**Why It Fails:**
1. He's instructing departing missionaries in official capacity
2. The sermon was recorded and published in Journal of Discourses
3. No disclaimer or hedge ("I think," "perhaps," "it seems to me")
4. Temple endowments are too sacred to speculate about casually
5. If prophets can't be trusted when teaching in official settings, what's the point?
6. How do members distinguish "speculation" from "doctrine"?
7. This defense destroys prophetic authority

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
This was an official address, recorded by scribes, published as prophetic teaching. The question-answer format ("Do you query why...?") suggests this was addressing actual questions about endowments.

---

### Defense #7: "The Church Has Grown in Understanding"

**What They Say:**
Line upon line, precept upon precept. The early church's understanding of temple ordinances was incomplete. Modern prophets have clarified that endowments are for exaltation, not creating devils.

**Why It Fails:**
1. This is 1860, not 1830 — temple doctrine was mature by then
2. Nauvoo temple had been operating 1840s, Endowment House since 1855
3. "Growing in understanding" shouldn't go from "creating devils" to "never mind"
4. This isn't refinement; it's complete reversal
5. Modern church doesn't say "we used to think endowments created devils, but now we know better"
6. If fundamental purpose of most sacred ordinance was misunderstood, what else is wrong?

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
Brigham wasn't proposing new doctrine; he was explaining established practice. If the prophet who supervised temple ordinances for decades fundamentally misunderstood their purpose, the epistemological crisis is massive.

---

### Defense #8: "He Meant Apostate Leaders, Not All Apostates"

**What They Say:**
Brigham was talking about apostate leaders who become 'angels of the devil' by leading others astray. He wasn't saying all apostates become devils.

**Why It Fails:**
1. The text doesn't limit this to leaders: "A., B., and C" sounds like regular members
2. "A host of devils will be needed" suggests many, not just a few leaders
3. If only leaders become devils, it's not much of a "host"
4. This defense still admits endowments create SOME devils, which contradicts modern teaching

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "Do you query why we give endowments to A., B., and C?"
> — JoD 8:181

A, B, and C are generic placeholders for individual people, not titles for leadership positions.

## THE LOGICAL STRUCTURE

1. **Premise 1:** Brigham Young explains the purpose of temple endowments
2. **Premise 2:** He states endowments are given "to make devils of those who will deny the faith"
3. **Premise 3:** This is "necessary, as a host of devils will be needed"
4. **Premise 4:** In the same sermon, he says all will receive "better kingdoms than entered their hearts to conceive"
5. **Conclusion:** Either God's plan instrumentally creates devils through sacred ordinances, OR Brigham contradicts himself, OR modern temple teaching hides a dark purpose

**The Self-Refuting Element:**

Brigham creates a devastating internal contradiction:
- "A host of devils will be needed" (many will be damned through temple ordinances)
- "All who lived according to the best light they had will have better kingdoms than ever entered into their hearts to conceive" (universal salvation)

These cannot both be true. If everyone gets good kingdoms, who populates the "host of devils"? Only those who received the temple's "best light" and rejected it — meaning the more sacred knowledge you receive, the greater your potential for damnation. This creates a theological catch-22: endowments are required for exaltation but increase the stakes for damnation.

**The Devastating Question:**

If temple endowments serve the dual purpose of creating gods AND devils, what happens in an endowment session? According to Brigham, "future saints and future devils side by side." God gives sacred ordinances to some people specifically "to make devils" of them. The question is devastating: Did God know? Did He intend it? Were YOUR endowments meant for exaltation or damnation?

## PRODUCER NOTES

### What This Song Does

This song exposes bizarre 19th-century temple theology that contradicts everything modern members hear about temple ordinances. It uses Brigham's own words to create cognitive dissonance: the temple preparation class promises exaltation, but the prophet who supervised those ordinances revealed a darker purpose. The song doesn't mock the temple itself; it reveals what was historically taught about its purpose.

### Key Production Decisions

1. **Dark cabaret tone, not satirical:** The subject is strange but serious. The theatrical, sardonic tone captures the bizarreness without reducing it to pure comedy. Think carnival barker revealing cosmic secrets.

2. **Waltz time (3/4):** Creates an unsettling, carousel-like feel appropriate for disturbing revelations dressed in religious language.

3. **Let quotes carry the weight:** "Do you query...?", "It is to make devils," "A host of devils will be needed" — deliver these with theatrical impact. They need no embellishment.

4. **The bridge highlights the contradiction:** The internal self-contradiction (devils needed vs. universal salvation) is the intellectual heart of the song. Slow down and let it land.

5. **Personal application:** "A., B., and C." becomes "everyone you know who left" — makes it personal to every temple-attending member.

### Potential Challenges

- **Temple sensitivity:** Temple ordinances are deeply meaningful to many good people. Target the contradiction between historical and modern teaching, not the practice itself.
- **Explaining context:** Non-LDS listeners may not understand what endowments are or why this matters. The lyrics should provide enough context.
- **Bizarre subject:** This is strange theology. The theatrical style embraces rather than fights that strangeness.
- **Quote length:** The full quote is long. Breaking it across chorus lines maintains accuracy while fitting musical structure.

### Comparable Works

- **Tom Waits** — Theatrical darkness with serious undertones
- **They Might Be Giants** — Absurdist music with intellectual content
- **Weimar cabaret** — Dark humor about serious subjects
- **Gothic folk** — Haunting atmosphere, religious imagery
