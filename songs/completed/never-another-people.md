## SOURCE MATERIAL

### Primary Quote 1: The Central Prophecy

> "God has given unto us the assurance that this Church, this work that He has established, shall never be given into the hands of another people, but that it shall stand forever, and it shall go forward accomplishing His designs, until it shall fill the whole earth."
> — George Q. Cannon, JoD 25:271 (August 31, 1884)

### Primary Quote 2: The Honest Admission

> "Looking at affairs naturally, however, it would seem as though it was presumptuous in a people like us to entertain such hopes. How often have we been told that in a very little while the opposition to this work would be of such a character that it would completely overwhelm it."
> — George Q. Cannon, JoD 25:271 (August 31, 1884)

### Primary Quote 3: Divine Protection Promise

> "Men may be slain, as they have been; people may be driven, as they have been; efforts of the most herculean character may be made to extirpate this work from the earth, but we have the promise of our God that it shall stand and that it shall not be overthrown."
> — George Q. Cannon, JoD 25:271 (August 31, 1884)

### Primary Quote 4: The Stakes of Persecution

> "The espousal and propagation of the truth has cost precious blood, and it is not being established in the earth now any more than at any previous time, without great sacrifices on the part of those who are its devotees. God will test us as a people."
> — George Q. Cannon, JoD 25:270 (August 31, 1884)

### Primary Quote 5: Failed Timeline - Constitutional Government

> "We have been taught to believe that the time will come when constitutional government will be overthrown upon this land, and that it will be the province of the Latter-day Saints to uphold those principles which God inspired the founders of this government to embody in the Constitution; and it seems to be fast approaching."
> — George Q. Cannon, JoD 25:276 (August 31, 1884)

### Primary Quote 6: Failed Timeline - Civil Strife

> "For the day will come, and it is not far distant, when in our own nation, there will be civil strife, there will be domestic broils, there will be a withdrawal of peace, and men will yet have to come to the Latter-day Saints for that peace and that freedom from civil strife that cannot be found elsewhere."
> — George Q. Cannon, JoD 25:276 (August 31, 1884)

### Secondary Source: The Manifesto

> "The Lord will never permit me or any other man who stands as President of this Church to lead you astray."
> — Wilford Woodruff, Official Declaration 1 (October 1890)

## HISTORICAL CONTEXT

### The Setting

George Q. Cannon delivered this sermon on Sunday, August 31, 1884, from the pulpit of the Salt Lake Tabernacle. As First Counselor in the First Presidency under President John Taylor, Cannon spoke with the highest ecclesiastical authority in the LDS Church. The Tabernacle setting on a Sunday represented the most official context possible for prophetic pronouncements.

### The Speaker

George Q. Cannon (1827-1901) was one of the most influential figures in 19th-century Mormonism. He served as a member of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, as Utah's territorial delegate to Congress, and as First Counselor to four consecutive church presidents (Brigham Young, John Taylor, Wilford Woodruff, and Lorenzo Snow). In 1884, Cannon was himself a polygamist with multiple wives and would soon go "underground" to avoid federal marshals enforcing the Edmunds Act.

### The Broader Context

The 1880s were the crisis point of the federal anti-polygamy crusade:
- **Edmunds Act (1882):** Made "unlawful cohabitation" a crime; polygamists lost voting rights and ability to hold office
- **Federal marshals:** Actively hunting polygamists; church leaders in hiding
- **Property seizures:** Pending Edmunds-Tucker Act (1887) would disincorporate the church and confiscate its property
- **Utah statehood:** Denied repeatedly due to polygamy
- **Underground church:** Many leaders, including Cannon, would soon flee to avoid arrest

This sermon was delivered as divine consolation to a persecuted people. Cannon's promise that "this work shall never be given to another people" was meant to assure members that their suffering had meaning and that God would protect them.

### Why It Matters

1. **Highest authority setting:** First Presidency member, Tabernacle, Sunday sermon, published in Journal of Discourses
2. **Absolute language:** "Never," "forever," "shall stand," "shall not be overthrown"
3. **Divine claim:** "God has given unto us the assurance" - not personal opinion
4. **Central issue:** Polygamy was THE defining issue; "the work" in context meant polygamy defense
5. **Admitted vulnerability:** Cannon's honest admission that "looking at affairs naturally, it would seem presumptuous" stakes everything on supernatural intervention

### The Aftermath

**October 1890:** President Wilford Woodruff issued Official Declaration 1 (the Manifesto), officially discontinuing the practice of plural marriage. This came six years after Cannon's prophecy and under intense federal pressure (Edmunds-Tucker Act had confiscated church property; statehood remained denied).

**1904:** Second Manifesto issued to stop secret plural marriages that had continued after 1890.

**Early 1900s:** Fundamentalist groups formed to continue polygamy, claiming they preserved the original doctrine while the mainstream church had apostasized.

**Modern day:**
- Fundamentalist Mormon groups (FLDS, AUB, etc.) practice polygamy and are excommunicated by the mainstream LDS Church
- Mainstream LDS teaches that monogamy is sufficient for exaltation, contradicting Brigham Young's teaching in JoD 11:269
- The "work" was, by any definition, "given to another people" - either those who kept 1884 doctrine (fundamentalists) or those who changed it (mainstream)

## ADDRESSING APOLOGETIC RESPONSES

### Defense #1: "The church didn't stop; it just stopped practicing polygamy"

**What They Say:**
The church as an institution continued and grew; only one practice was discontinued. The "work" refers to the overall mission of the church, not any specific practice.

**Why It Fails:**
- In the 1884 context, "this work" clearly meant polygamy defense - the entire sermon was about persecution for that practice
- Brigham Young had taught: "The only men who become Gods are those who enter into polygamy" (JoD 11:269)
- D&C 132 presents plural marriage as "the new and everlasting covenant" with damnation for those who reject it
- Dropping a salvific doctrine constitutes a fundamental change to "the work"
- If polygamy wasn't central to "the work," why did Cannon spend the entire sermon defending it?

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "The espousal and propagation of the truth has cost precious blood, and it is not being established in the earth now any more than at any previous time, without great sacrifices on the part of those who are its devotees."
> — JoD 25:270

The "truth" requiring "great sacrifices" in 1884 was polygamy. That was "the work."

---

### Defense #2: "The Manifesto was revelation; God changed the work"

**What They Say:**
The Manifesto was a divine revelation from God, so what appears to be a failed prophecy was actually God directing a course change. The work evolved under divine guidance.

**Why It Fails:**
- Creates theological crisis: Did God prophesy "never change" through Cannon, then reverse through Woodruff?
- Cannon said "it shall stand forever" - implies stability, not evolution
- Cannon specifically said federal "efforts to extirpate this work" would fail - Manifesto happened precisely because of those efforts
- If Manifesto was divine, why did it take multiple manifestos (1890, 1904) and secret marriages until ~1910?
- Cannon's words: "Completely overwhelm it" - which is what the federal government achieved

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "Efforts of the most herculean character may be made to extirpate this work from the earth, but we have the promise of our God that it shall stand and that it shall not be overthrown."
> — JoD 25:271

Federal efforts to extirpate polygamy DID succeed. The work WAS "overthrown" (if polygamy was the work) or fundamentally changed.

---

### Defense #3: "Mainstream LDS is still the same church; fundamentalists are apostates"

**What They Say:**
The mainstream LDS Church maintains organizational continuity from Joseph Smith through an unbroken priesthood line. Fundamentalists broke from the church and therefore lost authority. The work wasn't "given to another people" - it continued with the legitimate church.

**Why It Fails:**
By what measure "same"?
- Doctrine: Polygamy went from required for exaltation to forbidden
- Temple ordinances: Plural sealings to monogamous only
- Leadership: Polygamist First Presidency to monogamous
- Practice: Defying federal law to complying with it

Fundamentalists claim doctrinal continuity with 1884; mainstream claims organizational continuity but doctrinal evolution. The question is: which continuity matters for Cannon's "this work"?

If "the work" is organizational structure rather than doctrine, what makes it "God's work"?

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
The song's dual narrative structure (Version B) gives both sides a voice. Either interpretation confirms the prophecy failed: if fundamentalists are right, mainstream "gave it away"; if mainstream is right, the "work" fundamentally changed from 1884.

---

### Defense #4: "Cannon was speaking as a man, not as prophet"

**What They Say:**
Church leaders sometimes express personal opinions rather than revealed truth. Cannon's words may have been his personal conviction rather than divine prophecy.

**Why It Fails:**
- First Counselor in First Presidency (highest leadership position)
- Sunday discourse from Tabernacle pulpit (most official setting)
- Published in Journal of Discourses (official church record)
- Explicit divine authority claim: "God has given unto us the assurance"
- Referenced specific revelations and prophecies throughout sermon
- If First Presidency can be catastrophically wrong from the pulpit about the church's future, what CAN members trust?

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "God has given unto us the assurance that this Church, this work that He has established, shall never be given into the hands of another people."
> — JoD 25:271

"God has given unto us" is not "I personally think." This was presented as divine revelation.

---

### Defense #5: "The timeline prophecies could still come true"

**What They Say:**
Cannon's prophecies about constitutional government being overthrown and civil strife coming could still be fulfilled. Prophetic timelines often extend beyond human expectations.

**Why It Fails:**
- "Fast approaching" (1884) + 140 years = failed timeline
- "Not far distant" (1884) + 140 years = failed timeline
- Moving goalposts indefinitely isn't fulfillment
- Cannon himself said: "Fulfilled to the letter" - demanding precision, not open-ended waiting

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
> "It will, just as sure as He predicted it, be fulfilled to the letter."
> — JoD 25:276

"Fulfilled to the letter" sets the standard. "Maybe someday" doesn't meet it.

## THE LOGICAL STRUCTURE

1. **First Premise:** George Q. Cannon, First Presidency member, prophesies from the Tabernacle that "this work shall never be given into the hands of another people" and "shall stand forever."

2. **Second Premise:** The "work" in 1884 context was defined by and centered on polygamy - the very practice causing persecution.

3. **Third Premise:** Six years later (1890), the Manifesto discontinued polygamy under federal pressure.

4. **Fourth Premise:** Today, two groups exist: fundamentalists who practice 1884 doctrine but are excommunicated, and mainstream LDS who abandoned 1884 doctrine but claim institutional continuity.

5. **Conclusion:** The work WAS "given to another people" - either fundamentalists (if doctrine defines "the work") or modern mainstream members (if doctrine has fundamentally changed from 1884).

**The Self-Refuting Element:**

Cannon admitted his own defeat condition: "Looking at affairs naturally, however, it would seem as though it was presumptuous in a people like us to entertain such hopes."

He acknowledged that without supernatural intervention, they would lose. He staked everything on God's promise that they wouldn't. Six years later, the "natural" outcome prevailed. The presumption Cannon admitted was validated by history.

His own words became his indictment.

## PRODUCER NOTES

### What This Song Does

This song presents a philosophical trap with no escape: Cannon's prophecy that "this work shall never be given to another people" failed regardless of which interpretation one accepts. The song lets both sides speak (especially in the Extended Version B) and asks the devastating question without answering it: "Who are the other people?"

The song is NOT primarily about mocking persecuted people. It's about the tragedy of absolute certainty meeting inevitable failure, about leaders promising divine protection that doesn't arrive, and about the cruelty of false assurance.

### Key Production Decisions

1. **Same melody for both choruses:** The first chorus presents Cannon's promise triumphantly; the second chorus uses the SAME MELODY with opposite emotion (heartbreak instead of triumph). Same notes, opposite meaning. This is the key musical device.

2. **Cannon's admission as the hook:** "Looking at affairs naturally, it would seem presumptuous" is used repeatedly. His honest admission becomes the frame - he knew the natural outcome, bet on the supernatural, and lost.

3. **Dual narrative in Extended Version:** Rather than picking a side (fundamentalist vs. mainstream), Version B gives both voices. The listener must decide who's right - but either way, the prophecy failed.

4. **"Six years" as punctuation:** The timeline is devastating and precise. Using it as a spoken/whispered element at key moments (end of bridge, outro) lets the fact speak for itself.

5. **Post-rock anthemic style:** The cinematic build-and-release structure mirrors the prophetic certainty leading to collapse arc.

### Potential Challenges

- The phrase "never given to another people" is archaic phrasing. Solution: Repeat it extensively to make it the song's identity rather than a barrier.
- Balancing historical accuracy with singability. Solution: Use direct quotes for key phrases, paraphrase for narrative flow.
- Avoiding cruelty toward genuinely persecuted people. Solution: Focus on prophetic failure and institutional decisions rather than personal suffering.
- Extended Version B dual narrative may be challenging vocally. Solution: Could use two distinct singers or one singer with clearly differentiated vocal deliveries.
