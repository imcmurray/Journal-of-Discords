## Core Concept

Brigham Young reveals that "eternal punishment" and "endless torment" don't actually mean forever — they're just God's names for his punishment, since "Endless" is one of God's names. This semantic loophole allows for universal salvation (except for sons of perdition). The song explores the irony of divine revelation operating through word games, while also acknowledging the genuine comfort this doctrine provided to those terrified of traditional Christianity's hell-fire.

This is a more nuanced song — not purely critical, but exploring the strangeness of revelation-through-redefinition.

## Structural Ideas

### Option A: The Revelation Structure
- Verse 1: Traditional hell-fire Christianity (what people feared)
- Verse 2: The revelation — "Endless is my name"
- Chorus: The redefinition — eternal doesn't mean eternal
- Verse 3: The implications — almost everyone is saved
- Bridge: The mystery unveiled
- Final Chorus: Wonder/irony at word-games-as-theology

### Option B: Comfort/Irony Dual Structure
- Verse 1: The terrified believer facing eternal damnation
- Verse 2: The revelation brings comfort
- Chorus: "Endless is my name"
- Verse 3: But wait — revelation through definitions?
- Bridge: What does it mean for God to play with words?
- Final Chorus: Ambiguous — comfort and irony together
