## Core Concept

Apostle Orson Hyde declared that Jesus was the bridegroom at the wedding at Cana, and that he had multiple wives including Mary, Martha, and Mary Magdalene. He argued that Jesus, as a righteous man, would have obeyed the commandment to multiply—and that Jesus "saw his seed" (had children) before the crucifixion. The modern church maintains an awkward silence on Jesus's marital status.

---

## Key Quotes

### The Hook

> "Jesus was the bridegroom at the marriage of Cana of Galilee... If Jesus was not the bridegroom on that occasion, please tell who was."
> — JoD 2:82

### Supporting Quote 1

> "We say it was Jesus Christ who was married, to be brought into the relation whereby he could see his seed, before he was crucified."
> — JoD 2:82

### Supporting Quote 2

> "I do not despise to be called a son of Abraham, if he had a dozen wives; or to be called a brother, a son, a child of the Savior, if he had Mary, and Martha, and several others, as wives."
> — JoD 2:82

### Supporting Quote 3

> "He shall see his seed... Before the Savior died, he looked upon his own natural children, as we look upon ours; he saw his seed, and immediately afterwards he was cut off from the earth."
> — JoD 2:82

### Supporting Quote 4

> "If any man can show this, and prove that it was not the Savior of the world, then I will acknowledge I am in error."
> — JoD 2:82

## Challenges

1. The biblical wedding at Cana is familiar—need to twist it without losing the narrative
2. The doctrine is strange enough that it almost sounds like parody—need to play it straight
3. The modern church's silence is the key irony—need to highlight it
4. Keeping the Isaiah connection clear without being preachy

## Apologetic Responses to Address

1. "Just Orson Hyde's personal opinion" — Apostle at General Conference
2. "Never official doctrine" — Why was it being taught at all?
3. "He was speculating" — He issued a challenge to prove him wrong
4. "Not essential to salvation" — Then why preach it?
