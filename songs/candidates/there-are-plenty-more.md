## Core Concept

A song about the commodification of women in early Mormon polygamy. Jedediah M. Grant, speaking as Second Counselor in the First Presidency, teaches that a faithful man should surrender his wife to the Prophet on request with the same willingness as he'd give money — because "there are plenty more." The song explores the dehumanizing implications of this teaching, letting Grant's own words reveal the disposability of women under priesthood authority.

---

## Key Quotes

### The Hook

> "Or if he came and said, 'I want your wife?' 'O yes,' he would say, 'here she is; there are plenty more.'"
> — JoD 2:14

### Supporting Quote 1

> "What would a man of God say, who felt aright, when Joseph asked him for his money? He would say, 'Yes, and I wish I had more to help to build up the kingdom of God.'"
> — JoD 2:14

### Supporting Quote 2

> "If Joseph had a right to dictate me in relation to salvation, in relation to a hereafter, he had a right to dictate me in relation to all my earthly affairs, in relation to the treasures of the earth, and in relation to the earth itself."
> — JoD 2:14

### Supporting Quote 3

> "Says one brother to another, 'Joseph says all covenants are done away, and none are binding but the new covenants; now suppose Joseph should come and say he wanted your wife, what would you say to that?' 'I would tell him to go to hell.' This was the spirit of many in the early days of this Church."
> — JoD 2:13-14

### Supporting Quote 4

> "Did the Prophet Joseph want every man's wife he asked for? He did not, but in that thing was the grand thread of the Priesthood developed. The grand object in view was to try the people of God, to see what was in them."
> — JoD 2:14

## Structural Ideas

### Option A: The Test Structure
- Verse 1: The Prophet's authority over all things
- Verse 2: The question posed — "Suppose he wanted your wife?"
- Chorus: "There are plenty more"
- Verse 3: The resistance of some ("I'd tell him to go to hell")
- Bridge: The "test" — did he really want them?
- Final Chorus: The answer doesn't matter — women were property either way

### Option B: Woman's Perspective
- Verse 1: A woman hears the sermon
- Verse 2: She learns her value — same as gold and silver
- Chorus: "There are plenty more"
- Verse 3: The men discuss her like livestock
- Bridge: She was a test, not a person
- Final Chorus: Bitter irony

## Challenges
