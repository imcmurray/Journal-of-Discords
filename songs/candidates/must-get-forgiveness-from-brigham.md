## Core Concept

Heber C. Kimball, First Counselor in the First Presidency, taught a hierarchy of forgiveness that placed church leaders alongside the Godhead. Just as sins against God require God's forgiveness, and sins against Jesus require Jesus's forgiveness, sins against Brigham Young require Brigham's forgiveness — "the Father" cannot forgive you without it. "That is the law of Deseret, gentlemen."

---

## Key Quotes

### The Hook

> "And when you sin against brother Brigham, will the Father forgive you? No: you have got to ask forgiveness of brother Brigham. And when you sin against me, you have got to seek forgiveness of me, before you get it from the Father."
> — JoD 1:357

### Supporting Quote 1

> "As brother Brigham said here, if you sin against God, you have got to satisfy Him; and if you sin against Jesus Christ, you have got to make confession to Jesus, and He and the Father can forgive you; and if you sin against the Holy Ghost, you have got to satisfy the Holy Ghost, for neither the Father nor the Son can forgive that sin."
> — JoD 1:357

### Supporting Quote 2

> "Is not that good law? That is the law of Deseret, gentlemen."
> — JoD 1:357

### Supporting Quote 3

> "We are the Twelve Apostles, the High Priests, the Seventies, the Elders, the Priests, the Teachers, the Deacons, the Bishops... We cannot be separated; we are one."
> — JoD 1:357 (same sermon, from Erastus Snow)

### Supporting Quote 4

> "It is God who rules and leads; it is God who controls the destinies of all men."
> — JoD 1:357 (Erastus Snow)

## Notes

This teaching places church leaders in the same category as God, Jesus, and the Holy Ghost regarding forgiveness authority. The Father Himself cannot forgive sins against Brigham — only Brigham can.

This represents perhaps the most extreme claim of prophetic authority in the JoD — not just speaking for God, but holding veto power over God's forgiveness.

The "law of Deseret" phrasing ties it to the theocratic State of Deseret mindset that saw church and state as one.
