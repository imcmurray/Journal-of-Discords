## Core Concept

Brigham Young taught that some sins are so serious that Christ's blood cannot atone for them—only the shedding of the sinner's own blood can bring forgiveness. This wasn't metaphor: in 1851, George A. Smith used this doctrine to successfully defend a murderer in Utah court. The chilling framing: killing someone is "loving our neighbour as ourselves" because it saves their eternal soul.

---

## Key Quotes

### The Hook

> "If he wants salvation and it is necessary to spill his blood on the earth in order that he may be saved, spill it. This is loving our neighbour as ourselves."
> — JoD 4:220

### Supporting Quote 1

> "There are sins that men commit for which they cannot receive forgiveness in this world, or in that which is to come, and if they had their eyes open to see their true condition, they would be perfectly willing to have their blood spilt upon the ground, that the smoke thereof might ascend to heaven as an offering for their sins."
> — JoD 4:53

### Supporting Quote 2

> "The principle, the only one that beats and throbs through the heart of the entire inhabitants of this Territory, is simply this: The man who seduces his neighbor's wife must die, and her nearest relative must kill him!"
> — JoD 1:97

### Supporting Quote 3

> "I have known a great many men who have left this Church for whom there is no chance whatever for exaltation, but if their blood had been spilled, it would have been better for them."
> — JoD 4:220

### Supporting Quote 4

> "I have had men come to me and offer their lives to atone for their sins."
> — JoD 4:53

## Structural Ideas

### Option A: Murder Ballad Structure
- Verse 1: The crime—seduction, betrayal
- Verse 2: The doctrine—blood must be spilled
- Chorus: "This is loving our neighbour"
- Verse 3: The trial—George A. Smith's defense
- Bridge: The verdict—NOT GUILTY
- Final Chorus: The doctrine echoes

### Option B: Sermon Structure
- Verse 1: Brigham at the pulpit, explaining sin
- Verse 2: Sins beyond Christ's blood
- Chorus: The willing sacrifice
- Verse 3: "I have had men come to me"
- Bridge: What love requires
- Final Chorus: "Spill it"

## Apologetic Responses to Address

1. "Never official doctrine" — Taught by prophets, used in courts
2. "Just rhetoric" — Egan was acquitted using this argument
3. "Metaphorical" — "Spill his blood on the earth" is literal
4. "Product of the times" — They claimed it was eternal divine law
