## Core Concept

Brigham Young declared that the Kingdom of God must control "everything that pertains to men—their feelings, their faith, their affections, their desires, and every act of their lives." He presented a stark binary: either prophets control all temporal matters, or "our faith and labor are vain." This song captures the totalitarian vision of early Mormon theocracy, where no aspect of life was beyond church control.

---

## Key Quotes

### The Hook

> "...everything that pertains to men—their feelings, their faith, their affections, their desires, and every act of their lives—belong, that they may be ruled by it spiritually and temporally."
> — JoD 10:328

### Supporting Quote 1

> "Is it our duty to preach to this people and plead with them, until we can govern and control them in all temporal affairs as much as in spiritual affairs. I answer, it is the absolute and imperative duty of the Elders of Israel to try and control themselves and their families and their brethren, until they can hold control over all things in righteousness."
> — JoD 10:336

### Supporting Quote 2

> "We have declared that God has spoken from the heavens, when in such case He has not spoken. Our faith and labor are vain, and we are still in our sins, or else it is our duty to lead this people in every act of their lives, as much in their temporal as in their spiritual affairs."
> — JoD 10:336

### Supporting Quote 3

> "'In spiritual things you are my leader; I take you for my counsel in spiritual affairs; but if you dictate me in my temporal concerns, you touch a string that does not belong to you...'"
> — JoD 10:336 (Brigham quoting objectors before dismissing them)

### Supporting Quote 4

> "Those who will do this, and will shave the Saints to do a good business for the merchant who employs them, I curse in the name of Jesus Christ, and they shall be cursed."
> — JoD 10:338

## Challenges

1. The quotes are long — need to compress while preserving meaning
2. Making the totalitarian vision clear without modern commentary
3. Balancing Brigham's voice with the objectors he quotes

## Apologetic Responses to Address

1. **"This was about building Zion"** — He explicitly demands control over feelings, affections, desires — far beyond practical matters
2. **"Members always had agency"** — Brigham presents binary: total control or false prophets
3. **"This isn't taught today"** — The shift reveals how dramatically early theocratic claims were abandoned
4. **"He meant righteous influence"** — "Govern and control" and "hold control over all things" is not gentle influence

## Notes

The binary Brigham presents is remarkable: either prophets control everything temporal and spiritual, or "God has not spoken" and "our faith and labor are vain." There's no middle ground in his vision.

The curse he pronounces on those who "shave the Saints" shows the stakes — actual prophetic cursing for commercial behavior he disapproves of.

Connection to modern church: The LDS Church today emphasizes personal agency and conscience. This represents a dramatic retreat from Brigham's stated vision.
