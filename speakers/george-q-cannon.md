## Overview

George Q. Cannon was one of the most influential leaders in 19th-century Mormonism, serving as First Counselor to three church presidents. He was a polygamist, went into hiding during federal prosecution, strongly defended plural marriage, and later supported the 1890 Manifesto. He was also a prolific writer, editor, and territorial delegate to Congress.

**Historical Significance:**
- Served in First Presidency for 21 years (1880-1901)
- Defended polygamy vigorously until 1890 Manifesto
- Went underground to avoid federal prosecution (1885-1888)
- Supported Manifesto as member of First Presidency
- Utah Territory's delegate to U.S. Congress (1872-1882)
- Influential in both church doctrine and politics

**Speaking Style:**
- Systematic and doctrinal, similar to John Taylor
- Confident prophetic declarations
- Apocalyptic expectations
- Strong persecution narratives
- Absolute certainty in divine protection
- Emphasis on gathering and temple work

**Relationship to Polygamy:**
- Practicing polygamist with multiple wives
- Vocal defender during federal pressure (1882-1890)
- Preached church would never abandon polygamy
- Later supported Manifesto as First Counselor
- Represents the leadership's shift from defiance to accommodation

## Key Findings

### Failed Prophecies

#### Finding 1: "Never Given to Another People"
**Source:** JoD 25:271 (August 31, 1884)
**Full Analysis:** `volumes/vol-25/findings/1884-08-31-never-another-people.md`

**The Central Prophecy:**
> "God has given unto us the assurance that this Church, this work that He has established, shall never be given into the hands of another people, but that it shall stand forever, and it shall go forward accomplishing His designs, until it shall fill the whole earth."

**Context:**
- Sunday afternoon discourse, Tabernacle, Salt Lake City
- Six years before 1890 Manifesto
- Height of federal anti-polygamy persecution
- Cannon speaking as First Counselor in First Presidency
- Published in Journal of Discourses as official teaching

**What "The Work" Meant in 1884:**
In context, "this work" clearly included polygamy:
- Same sermon discusses "great sacrifices" required of Saints in 1884
- The only major test/persecution in 1884 was polygamy
- Edmunds Act (1882) was criminalizing plural marriage
- Cannon and other leaders were going underground
- The "work" being attacked was plural marriage

**Why It Matters:**
- **Absolute promise:** "Shall NEVER be given" — no conditions, no exceptions
- **Six years later:** Manifesto fundamentally altered "this work"
- **The ironic question:** Which is "another people"?
  - Fundamentalists (FLDS, etc.) who practice 1884 doctrine?
  - Mainstream LDS who abandoned 1884 doctrine?
- **Failed divine protection:** Cannon promised God would preserve the work; He didn't
- **Constitutional prophecy also failed:** Predicted government collapse "fast approaching" (140+ years later, still waiting)

**Additional Failed Prophecies from Same Sermon:**

**Civil Strife Coming Soon:**
> "For the day will come, and it is not far distant, when in our own nation, there will be civil strife, there will be domestic broils, there will be a withdrawal of peace, and men will yet have to come to the Latter-day Saints for that peace and that freedom from civil strife that cannot be found elsewhere."
- "Not far distant" in 1884
- 140+ years later, still hasn't happened on predicted scale

**Constitutional Government to Fall:**
> "We have been taught to believe that the time will come when constitutional government will be overthrown upon this land, and that it will be the province of the Latter-day Saints to uphold those principles which God inspired the founders of this government to embody in the Constitution; and it seems to be fast approaching."
- "Fast approaching" in 1884
- Constitutional government still functioning
- Federal government forced church to change, not vice versa

**God Will Fight Their Battles:**
> "Men may be slain, as they have been; people may be driven, as they have been; efforts of the most herculean character may be made to extirpate this work from the earth, but we have the promise of our God that it shall stand and that it shall not be overthrown."
- Physical violence okay, but doctrine won't change
- Six years later, doctrine DID fundamentally change
- "Herculean efforts" succeeded (Edmunds-Tucker Act, federal pressure)

**The Devastating Irony:**
Cannon was part of the First Presidency that issued the 1890 Manifesto, directly contradicting his 1884 prophecy. He went from "we have the promise of our God" to supporting the very change he said was impossible.

**Song Potential:** ★★★★★ (CRITICAL TIER — Perfect irony, impossible apologetics)

### The "Another People" Question

**The Built-In Irony:**
Both fundamentalists and mainstream LDS can claim the OTHER is "another people":

**Fundamentalist Claim:**
- "We kept the 1884 doctrine (polygamy, Adam-God, blood atonement)"
- "Mainstream LDS abandoned the work and gave it to another people (themselves, by changing)"
- "We're the continuation; they're the apostates"

**Mainstream LDS Claim:**
- "We follow living prophets and continuing revelation"
- "Fundamentalists are apostates who broke away"
- "We're the continuation; they're another people"

**The Impossible Situation:**
- If mainstream is right, Cannon prophesied falsely (church DID change fundamentally)
- If fundamentalists are right, mainstream gave work to "another people" (themselves)
- Either way, the 1884 promise failed

**This is PERFECT for a song** — the unresolvable irony is built into the material.

## Self-Contradictions

### Internal to 1884 Sermon
Minor tensions between pacifism and divine battle promises, but no major logical contradictions.

### Across Cannon's Career
**MASSIVE contradiction:**

**1884 (as First Counselor):**
- "This work shall never be given into the hands of another people"
- "We have the promise of our God that it shall stand and not be overthrown"
- "God will fight their battles"

**1890 (as First Counselor):**
- Supported Manifesto ending polygamy
- Part of leadership that did exactly what he said was impossible
- Gave "the work" (1884 version) to "another people" (fundamentalists who continued it)

**The Apologetic Dilemma:**
- If Manifesto was revelation, Cannon's 1884 prophecy was false
- If Manifesto was surrender, Cannon was right but church failed
- If both were inspired, God contradicted Himself
- No escape from the contradiction

## Apologetic Challenges

### Challenge 1: "The Church Didn't Stop, Just Stopped Practicing Polygamy"
**Why it fails:** In 1884 context, "the work" centrally included polygamy (the entire sermon defends it as revealed truth requiring sacrifice).

### Challenge 2: "The Manifesto Was Revelation"
**Why it fails:** Creates crisis — did God prophesy church would never change, then change it? Cannon said opposition wouldn't overwhelm them; it did.

### Challenge 3: "Mainstream LDS Is Still the Same Church"
**Why it fails:** By what measure? Doctrine changed (polygamy required → forbidden), temple ordinances changed, leadership changed (polygamist → monogamous).

### Challenge 4: "Constitutional Prophecies Could Still Come True"
**Why it fails:** "Not far distant" (1884) and "fast approaching" (1884) = 140+ years is failed timeline, even if events eventually happen.
