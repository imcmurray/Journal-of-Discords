## Overview

John Taylor was the third president of the LDS Church, serving from 1880 until his death in 1887. He was with Joseph Smith at Carthage Jail and was wounded during the attack that killed Joseph and Hyrum Smith. Taylor was known as a staunch defender of polygamy and spent the last years of his life in hiding from federal authorities enforcing anti-polygamy laws.

**Historical Significance:**
- Present at Joseph Smith's martyrdom (1844), wounded in the attack
- Served as Apostle under Joseph Smith and Brigham Young
- Presided during intense federal prosecution of polygamy (Edmunds Act 1882)
- Died in hiding rather than surrender on polygamy charges
- Defended polygamy with absolute certainty up to his death

**Speaking Style:**
- Systematic and theological, more structured than Brigham Young
- Heavy emphasis on dispensations, priesthood authority, and temple work
- Apocalyptic rhetoric against "Christian ministers" and federal government
- Combines doctrinal exposition with fierce denunciation of enemies
- Absolute certainty in divine sanction: "I tell you that in the name of the Lord"

**Relationship to Polygamy:**
Taylor was perhaps the most doctrinally committed defender of polygamy among LDS presidents. While Brigham Young practiced it extensively, and Wilford Woodruff ended it officially, Taylor presided during its most intense persecution and never wavered. He died as a fugitive rather than compromise.

## Key Findings

### ⚠️ THE MOST EXTREME RACIAL STATEMENT IN JoD ⚠️

#### Finding 1: "The Devil's Representation on Earth"
**Source:** JoD 22:304 (August 28, 1881)
**Full Analysis:** `volumes/vol-22/findings/1882-10-devil-representation.md`

**The Teaching:**
> "And after the flood we are told that the curse that had been pronounced upon Cain was continued through Ham's wife, as he had married a wife of that seed. And why did it pass through the flood? Because it was necessary that the devil should have a representation upon the earth as well as God."

**Context:**
- Provo Stake Conference, August 28, 1881
- Taylor speaking as Church President (ordained October 1880)
- Official discourse on priesthood authority, dispensations, and temple work
- The racial theology embedded in explanation of Noah's flood
- Published in JoD, the church's semi-official record

**Why This Is THE Most Extreme Statement:**

1. **Cosmic satanic designation**: Not merely "cursed" or "less valiant" but explicitly designated as "the devil's representation upon the earth"

2. **Divine intentionality**: Taylor explicitly states God DELIBERATELY preserved the curse through Ham's wife so "the devil should have a representation upon the earth as well as God"

3. **Systematic theodicy**: This is not offhand prejudice but careful theological explanation of why Black people exist—to represent Satan in opposition to God's work

4. **Presidential authority**: Spoken by Church President at Conference, presented as revealed truth ("we are told that"), not personal speculation

5. **The word "necessary"**: Taylor says it was "necessary" that the curse pass through the flood—indicating divine design, not human error

6. **Published and official**: This was official enough to include in the Journal of Discourses

**What Makes It Worse Than Brigham Young's Statements:**

Brigham Young taught:
- Black skin as "mark of Cain" (JoD 7:290)
- "Death on the spot" for interracial marriage (JoD 10:110)
- Black people "deprived of intelligence" (JoD 7:290)

But Taylor goes further by providing **cosmic purpose**: Black people don't just bear a curse—they exist specifically to serve as Satan's representatives in opposition to God.

**Modern Church Position:**

2013 "Race and the Priesthood" essay:
> "Today, the Church disavows the theories advanced in the past that black skin is a sign of divine disfavor or curse, or that it reflects unrighteous actions in a premortal life; that mixed-race marriages are a sin; or that blacks or people of any other race or ethnicity are inferior in any way to anyone else. Church leaders today unequivocally condemn all racism, past and present, in any form."

2020 Church statement:
> "We unequivocally condemn racism, including any and all past racism by individuals both inside and outside the Church."

**The Devastating Problem:**
- Essay uses passive voice: "theories advanced in the past"
- Never names John Taylor
- Never quotes this specific teaching
- Never explains how a Church President could teach that God designed Black people as "the devil's representation"
- Never apologizes to Black members
- "Unequivocally condemn all racism" yet won't unequivocally name and repudiate this racist teaching by name

**Internal Contradictions in the Same Sermon:**

Immediately before the "devil's representation" passage, Taylor condemns Protestant ministers:
> "They are murderers and murderesses of their infants, and the stench of their infamy ascends into the nostrils of Jehovah; and you that want them, take them, and you that do will go along with them, and go to perdition with them; and I tell you that in the name of the Lord."

Then he explains why God designed Black people to represent the devil.

**The irony:** Taylor claims absolute moral authority ("I tell you that in the name of the Lord") while teaching objectively immoral doctrine.

### Polygamy Teachings

#### Finding 2: "We Cannot Tone It Down"
**Source:** JoD 25:309 (October 6-7, 1884)
**Full Analysis:** `volumes/vol-25/findings/1884-10-06-cannot-tone-down.md`

**The Teaching:**
> "God has given us a revelation in regard to celestial marriage. I did not make it. He has told us certain things pertaining to this matter, and they would like us to tone that principle down and change it and make it applicable to the views of the day. This we cannot do; nor can we interfere with any of the commands of God to meet the persuasions or behests of men. I cannot do it, and will not do it."

**Context:**
- General Conference, October 6-7, 1884
- Six years before 1890 Manifesto
- Congregation sustained with loud "Amen"
- Taylor speaking as Church President with absolute certainty

**Why It Matters:**
- **Absolute language:** "We CANNOT do" (theological impossibility, not just preference)
- **Divine command:** "Commands of God" = unchangeable by humans
- **Congregational ratification:** Thousands said "Amen" to never compromise
- **Six years later:** Church DID tone it down (Manifesto)
- **Taylor's fidelity:** He died in hiding (1887) keeping his word
- **Institutional betrayal:** His successor Woodruff issued Manifesto (1890)

**Additional Promises from Same Sermon:**
- "Living we will proclaim them, and dying we will be true to them"
- "Men who will not abide my law shall not preside over my Priesthood" (D&C 132)
- "If God is with us, we ask no odds of the world"
- Mocked those who "slip round a corner... like Nicodemus" — then went underground 4 months later

**Timeline:**
- October 6-7, 1884: "We cannot tone it down"
- February 1, 1885: Taylor goes underground
- July 25, 1887: Taylor dies in hiding
- September 24, 1890: Manifesto issued (5 years, 11 months, 18 days later)

**Song Potential:** ★★★★★ (PLATINUM TIER — Perfect timeline, tragic hero, broken promise)

**Known Context:**
- Taylor died in hiding rather than renounce polygamy
- Presided during Edmunds Act (1882) which criminalized polygamy
- Refused to compromise or issue manifesto (unlike successor Woodruff)
- His death in hiding (1887) preceded the 1890 Manifesto by 3 years

### Authority Claims

#### Pattern: "In the Name of the Lord"

Throughout the August 28, 1881 sermon (JoD 22:297-312), Taylor repeatedly claims ultimate divine authority:

> "I tell you that in the name of the Lord" (JoD 22:303)

> "God expects me to talk plainly. I have not come here to daub you with untempered mortar, but I tell you the truth." (JoD 22:312)

> "In the name of Israel's God we will try and carry it out." (JoD 22:312)

**Problem:** These absolute authority claims frame the "devil's representation" teaching. If Taylor was speaking "in the name of the Lord" when he condemned Christians as "murderers and murderesses," was he also speaking "in the name of the Lord" when he said God designed Black people as Satan's representatives?

**Apologetic Dilemma:**
- If YES: The prophet speaking for God taught horrific racism
- If NO: How do we know when he's speaking for God vs. as a man?

## Apologetic Challenges

### Challenge 1: "Speaking as a Man, Not as a Prophet"

**Why It Fails:**
- Taylor was literally Church President speaking at Conference
- Sermon is about priesthood authority and following prophetic leadership
- He claims to speak "in the name of the Lord"
- The teaching is presented as revealed knowledge ("we are told that")
- Published in JoD, the official/semi-official record
- No disclaimer that this was personal opinion

**Rebuttal:** If Church Presidents speaking at Conference about doctrinal matters in the name of the Lord can be this catastrophically wrong, the entire prophetic model collapses.

---

### Challenge 2: "Product of His Time"

**Why It Fails:**
- Taylor claims divine revelation, not cultural opinion
- He says it was "necessary" that God preserve the curse—this is theology, not mere prejudice
- Abolitionists and anti-racists existed in 1881
- The issue isn't prejudice but claiming GOD designed Black people as "devil's representation"
- Even if cultural racism was common, making God the author of racism is qualitatively different

**Rebuttal:** Many of Taylor's Christian opponents whom he condemned would have rejected his racial theology. If he can hold them morally accountable, we can hold him accountable.

---

### Challenge 3: "The Church Has Disavowed This"

**Why It Fails:**
- Disavowal without explanation creates more problems than it solves
- Essay never names Taylor or quotes this teaching
- No explanation for how prophets could be so wrong
- No apology to Black members
- Passive voice "theories advanced in the past" obscures prophetic responsibility

**Rebuttal:** If John Taylor, as Church President, taught that Black people are "the devil's representation upon the earth," and this was wrong, then:
1. How was he sustained as prophet, seer, and revelator?
2. What other prophetic teachings might be equally wrong?
3. Why should members trust current prophets if past prophets were this wrong?

The essay's silence is damning. Disavowal without accountability is not repentance.

---

### Challenge 4: "Context Matters—Read the Whole Sermon"

**Why It Fails:**
- Full context makes it WORSE, not better
- Taylor explicitly states this was divine design: "Because it was necessary"
- The theological framework reinforces the racism (pre-mortal spirits, corrupt bodies, divine justice)
- The juxtaposition with condemnation of "murderers and murderesses" is devastating
- Taylor claims absolute authority throughout: "I tell you that in the name of the Lord"

**Rebuttal:** Context does not absolve this teaching. Context condemns it more fully.
