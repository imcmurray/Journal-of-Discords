## Sermon Overview

### Main Topic/Purpose
Defense of the LDS Church against persecution with emphasis on God's promise that "this Church, this work that He has established, shall never be given into the hands of another people, but that it shall stand forever." Delivered during intense federal anti-polygamy persecution, six years before the 1890 Manifesto.

### Key Themes
- Persecution as sign of true religion
- God's promise that church will never be given to another people
- Church will stand forever and fill the whole earth
- Pacifism and non-resistance commanded
- Constitutional government to fail; Saints to preserve it
- Civil strife and domestic broils prophesied
- Temple-building people should avoid warfare
- Assassination of Saints justified by enemies

### Audience
General membership in Salt Lake City, with awareness of national scrutiny and persecution. Speaking to members experiencing federal raids on polygamists and legislative assault on church authority.

### Historical Context
- **Six years before 1890 Manifesto:** Polygamy still official doctrine and practice
- **Edmunds Act (1882):** Federal law criminalizing polygamy, creating "cohabs" offense
- **Edmunds-Tucker Act pending (1887):** Would disincorporate church, confiscate property
- **Underground period:** Polygamists in hiding from federal marshals
- **First Presidency member:** Cannon himself practicing polygamy, would later go underground
- **"Twin relics" rhetoric:** Polygamy final battleground after slavery abolished
- **Assassination climate:** Mentions recent assassinations of Saints, justified by newspapers
- **Utah statehood denied:** Polygamy as primary barrier
- **Joseph Smith prophecy (1833):** References D&C 98 on persecution and vengeance

## Key Quotations

### Quote 1: The Central Prophecy — Never Given to Another People

> "God has given unto us the assurance that this Church, this work that He has established, shall never be given into the hands of another people, but that it shall stand forever, and it shall go forward accomplishing His designs, until it shall fill the whole earth."
> — JoD 25:271

**Context:** After discussing historical persecution, Cannon offers "consolation" about God's promises regarding the church's future. This is framed as divine assurance amid federal assault on polygamy.

**Significance:**
- **Absolute promise:** "Shall NEVER be given" — no conditions, no exceptions
- **Same people continuity:** Implies doctrinal consistency with current leadership
- **Stand forever:** Suggests no fundamental changes to church structure/doctrine
- **Fill the whole earth:** Triumphalist expansion prophecy
- **Timing:** Six years before Manifesto fundamentally altered "this work"
- **"The work":** In context, clearly includes polygamy as central practice

**Category:** FAILED-PROPH + IRONIC

---

### Quote 2: What "The Work" Meant in 1884

> "The espousal and propagation of the truth has cost precious blood, and it is not being established in the earth now any more than at any previous time, without great sacrifices on the part of those who are its devotees. God will test us as a people. He will prove us, He will give us the most ample opportunity of showing our faith in Him, and our confidence in the truth that He has revealed."
> — JoD 25:270

**Context:** Discussing the cost of "the Gospel of Jesus Christ" and what Saints were being tested on in 1884. The "test" was clearly maintaining polygamy against federal pressure.

**Significance:**
- **Current sacrifice:** "It is not being established... now... without great sacrifices"
- **What sacrifice in 1884?** Polygamy was the only major test/persecution
- **Divine test:** God testing their faithfulness to revealed truth
- **Six years later:** Church surrendered the very doctrine being "tested"
- **Irony:** The "test" they were told to endure was abandoned

**Category:** IRONIC + FAILED-PROPH

---

### Quote 3: Men May Be Slain But Work Will Stand

> "Men may be slain, as they have been; people may be driven, as they have been; efforts of the most herculean character may be made to extirpate this work from the earth, but we have the promise of our God that it shall stand and that it shall not be overthrown."
> — JoD 25:271

**Context:** Immediately before Quote #1, acknowledging violent persecution but promising divine protection of "this work."

**Significance:**
- **Physical violence OK, doctrinal change impossible:** Implies doctrine won't change even if people die
- **"This work" again:** The work being attacked (polygamy) promised divine protection
- **Six years later:** The work WAS fundamentally altered without violent overthrow
- **Changed by law not violence:** Herculean federal efforts DID succeed

**Category:** FAILED-PROPH

### Quote 4: Looking at Affairs Naturally, It Would Seem Presumptuous

> "Looking at affairs naturally, however, it would seem as though it was presumptuous in a people like us to entertain such hopes. How often have we been told that in a very little while the opposition to this work would be of such a character that it would completely overwhelm it, and that it was useless for us to attempt to stem the tide of opposition or outlive the storm of persecution that has been raised against us."
> — JoD 25:271

**Context:** Acknowledging that from natural perspective, their situation looked hopeless. Immediately follows promise that God would preserve the work.

**Significance:**
- **Admits natural impossibility:** From human view, defeat was inevitable
- **Relies on supernatural intervention:** Only God could save them
- **Six years later:** God did NOT intervene; Manifesto surrendered
- **Opposition DID overwhelm:** The very outcome he said wouldn't happen
- **Failed divine intervention:** God's promised preservation didn't occur

**Category:** FAILED-PROPH

### Quote 5: Temple-Building People Should Not Shed Blood

> "We are a temple-building people. God has given unto us a mission of this kind, to build temples in which we shall perform the ordinances of life and salvation, and it seems to be meet in His providence that we should refrain from everything that would unfit us for the discharge of this high and holy calling."
> — JoD 25:274

**Context:** Explaining why Saints must be peaceful and avoid revenge, citing David unable to build temple because he was "a man of war."

**Significance:**
- **Temple ordinances in 1884:** Included polygamous sealings as essential
- **Six years later:** Those very ordinances fundamentally changed
- **Irony:** Temples still built, but without the polygamy that was central in 1884
- **"Ordinances of life and salvation":** In 1884 included plural marriage

**Category:** IRONIC

### Quote 6: Constitutional Government Will Be Overthrown

> "We have been taught to believe that the time will come when constitutional government will be overthrown upon this land, and that it will be the province of the Latter-day Saints to uphold those principles which God inspired the founders of this government to embody in the Constitution; and it seems to be fast approaching."
> — JoD 25:276

**Context:** Discussing how assassination being justified shows constitutional principles failing. Predicts Saints will save Constitution.

**Significance:**
- **White Horse Prophecy theme:** Saints to save Constitution
- **Fast approaching in 1884:** Imminent expectation
- **140+ years later:** Constitutional government still functioning
- **Role reversal:** Federal government forced church to change, not vice versa
- **Failed timeline:** "Fast approaching" never arrived

**Category:** FAILED-PROPH

### Quote 7: Civil Strife and Domestic Broils Coming to America

> "For the day will come, and it is not far distant, when in our own nation, there will be civil strife, there will be domestic broils, there will be a withdrawal of peace, and men will yet have to come to the Latter-day Saints for that peace and that freedom from civil strife that cannot be found elsewhere. God revealed this and predicted it, upwards of fifty years ago, and it will, just as sure as He predicted it, be fulfilled to the letter."
> — JoD 25:276

**Context:** Referencing Joseph Smith prophecies from 1830s about nation's future. Promises imminent fulfillment.

**Significance:**
- **Not far distant in 1884:** 140+ years later, still waiting
- **Men coming to Saints for peace:** Never happened on promised scale
- **Fulfilled to the letter:** Emphatic certainty of fulfillment
- **Joseph Smith prophecy:** Stakes reputation on 50-year-old prediction
- **Civil War already happened:** But this implies something beyond 1860s war

**Category:** FAILED-PROPH

### Quote 8: When Assassination Can Be Justified, Constitutional Principles Fail

> "When assassination can be justified, assassination of men peaceably worshipping their God, offending no one, committing no violation of law or of good order; when they can be shot down cruelly and inhumanly, and their murderers be justified for the deed, it seems as though the time when constitutional principles would fail, is near at hand."
> — JoD 25:276

**Context:** Discussing recent murders of Saints and newspaper justifications of assassination.

**Significance:**
- **Historical documentation:** Confirms anti-Mormon violence in 1884
- **Moral high ground:** Saints "offending no one, committing no violation of law"
- **Reality:** Saints WERE violating Edmunds Act (polygamy was illegal)
- **Persecution complex:** Framing legal prosecution as religious persecution
- **Constitutional failure prediction:** Based on assassination climate

**Category:** HISTORICAL CONTEXT (supports understanding of persecution claims)

**Internal to this sermon?**
Minor tension:
- Preaches pacifism while predicting God will "fight their battles"
- Says Saints must endure persecution while predicting persecution will end with Saints triumphant
- But these are temporal, not logical contradictions

**Across speaker's other sermons?**
Need to cross-reference, but George Q. Cannon was consistent defender of polygamy through 1880s. Went into hiding to avoid prosecution. Later supported Manifesto as First Presidency member (1890).

**Across institution?**
MASSIVE contradictions:
- **1890 Manifesto:** "This work" WAS given to "another people" (fundamentalists continued, mainstream abandoned)
- **Modern teaching:** Manifesto was inspired revelation, not defeat
- **D&C 132:** Polygamy still canonical but "not currently practiced"

## Apologetic Anticipation

### Defense #1: "The church didn't stop; it just stopped practicing polygamy"

**Why it fails:**
- Cannon said "THIS WORK... shall never be given to another people"
- In 1884 context, "the work" centrally included polygamy (see entire sermon defending it)
- Brigham Young: "The only men who become Gods are those who enter into polygamy" (JoD 11:269)
- Dropping core salvific doctrine = different work
- D&C 132 (1843): "This is a new and everlasting covenant; and if ye abide not that covenant, then are ye damned"
- If polygamy was "the work," and church stopped, the work WAS given to others (FLDS, etc.)

**Source rebuttal:**
The entire sermon is defending polygamy as "the truth that He has revealed" that requires "great sacrifices." Quote #2 explicitly discusses 1884 persecution (polygamy raids) as the test they must endure.

---

### Defense #2: "The Manifesto was revelation, so God changed the work, not men"

**Why it fails:**
- Creates theological crisis: Did God prophesy the church would never change, then change it?
- Cannon said it would "stand forever" and "fill the whole earth" (implying triumphant expansion, not contraction)
- Quote #3: "Efforts... may be made to extirpate this work from the earth, but... it shall stand and not be overthrown"
- Federal pressure (Edmunds-Tucker Act) WAS an effort to extirpate it
- If Manifesto was God changing His mind, then Cannon's prophecy was false
- If Manifesto was revelation, why did it take multiple manifestos (1890, 1904) and continued plural marriages until ~1910?

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #4: "Looking at affairs naturally, it would seem presumptuous... How often have we been told that... opposition would completely overwhelm it, and that it was useless to attempt to stem the tide" — Cannon explicitly says the opposition WON'T overwhelm them. But it did.

---

### Defense #3: "The mainstream LDS church is still the same church, fundamentalists are apostates"

**Why it fails:**
- By what measure is it "the same"?
  - **Doctrine changed:** Polygamy from required to forbidden
  - **Temple ordinances changed:** Plural sealings to monogamous only
  - **Leadership changed:** From polygamist First Presidency to monogamous
  - **Practice changed:** From defying federal law to complying
- Fundamentalists claim doctrinal continuity with 1884 church
- Modern LDS claims organizational continuity but doctrinal evolution
- Question: Which continuity matters for Cannon's prophecy?
- If doctrine is irrelevant to "the work," what is the work?

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #1: "This Church, this WORK" — he defines church by its work/doctrine, not just organization.

---

### Defense #4: "Other prophecies about the church have come true"

**Why it fails:**
- Whataboutism doesn't address this specific failed prophecy
- Cannon stakes his credibility: "Looking at affairs naturally, it would seem presumptuous... but we have the promise of our God"
- Six years later, the presumption was validated; the promise failed
- Failed prophecy isn't negated by successful ones
- This isn't a minor detail but the CENTRAL consolation of the sermon

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #7: "It will, just as sure as He predicted it, be fulfilled to the letter." Cannon demands precise fulfillment, not approximate success.

---

### Defense #5: "The constitutional and civil strife prophecies could still come true"

**Why it fails:**
- Quote #6: "It seems to be fast approaching" (in 1884)
- Quote #7: "The day will come, and it is not far distant" (in 1884)
- 140+ years is NOT "not far distant"
- Specific predictions about assassination climate as sign of imminent collapse
- Failed timeline predictions are still failed prophecies
- Moving goalposts indefinitely isn't fulfillment

**Source rebuttal:**
"Just as sure as He predicted it, be fulfilled to the letter" — demands specific, timely fulfillment, not "eventually maybe."

---

### Defense #6: "Cannon was speaking as a man, not as prophet"

**Why it fails:**
- Cannon was First Counselor in First Presidency (highest leadership)
- Delivered from pulpit in Tabernacle on Sunday
- Published in Journal of Discourses as official sermon
- Claims divine revelation: "We have the promise of our God"
- References specific revelations (D&C 98, Joseph Smith prophecies)
- If First Presidency can be catastrophically wrong about church's future from pulpit, what can we trust?

**Source rebuttal:**
"God has given unto us the assurance" — explicitly claims divine source.

---

### Defense #7: "The work hasn't been given to others; it's still being done by the LDS church"

**Why it fails:**
- Fundamentalists (FLDS, AUB, Apostolic United Brethren, etc.) practice 1884 doctrine
- They claim they're continuing "this work" while mainstream abandoned it
- Modern LDS excommunicates anyone practicing 1884 polygamy doctrine
- By 1884 standards, modern LDS has apostatized
- By modern standards, 1884 doctrine was false
- Either way, "the work" as defined in 1884 is NOT being done by modern mainstream LDS

**Source rebuttal:**
The entire sermon defends polygamy as revealed truth requiring sacrifice. That specific work was abandoned.

---

### Defense #8: "We don't understand the full context of 1884"

**Why it fails:**
- Context makes it WORSE, not better
- 1884 context: Intense federal persecution, polygamists underground, church fighting to preserve doctrine
- Cannon offers divine assurance as consolation for persecution
- Six years later: Church surrendered the very doctrine being "protected"
- Understanding context shows this was the CENTRAL issue, not peripheral

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #2: "God will test us... He will give us the most ample opportunity of showing our faith in Him, and our confidence in the truth that He has revealed" — the test was polygamy; they failed the test by abandoning it (or succeeded by showing wisdom to change, depending on perspective — but either way, prophecy failed).

### Challenges

**Musical:**
- Balancing historical detail with emotional impact
- Making 1880s context accessible to modern listeners
- Avoiding "inside baseball" Mormon terminology without oversimplifying
- Maintaining sympathy for persecuted Saints while critiquing failed prophecy

**Conceptual:**
- The "other people" irony is complex; needs clear explanation
- Fundamentalist/mainstream split requires nuance
- Persecution was real; need to acknowledge suffering while showing prophecy failure
- Multiple failed prophecies in one sermon (focus on central one or include all?)

**Lyrical:**
- "Given to another people" is awkward phrasing (archaic language)
- Need to make theological concepts (exaltation, temple ordinances) understandable
- Timeline jumps (1884 → 1890 → 2024) require clear signposting
- Avoiding "gotcha" tone; letting material speak for itself

**Technical:**
- Hook is long: "This work shall never be given to another people" (10 syllables, awkward rhythm)
- Need to decide: sympathetic to Saints or critical of prophecy? (Can be both, but hard)
- Visual aids needed: Timeline graphic, fundamentalist groups, Manifesto text
- Source mapping will be dense (entire sermon is context)

**Solutions:**

1. **Shorten hook:** "Never given to another people" (6 syllables, cleaner)
2. **Use Cannon's voice:** First-person perspective makes it personal, not preachy
3. **Structure as tragedy:** Cannon genuinely believed, was wrong; sympathize with failure
4. **Visual timeline:** On-screen dates help anchor narrative
5. **Focus on central irony:** Who is "another people"? Everything else supports this
6. **Include fundamentalist voice:** Let them explain why THEY kept the work
7. **No need to resolve:** Song doesn't have to pick a side; can end on the unresolved question
8. **Trust the irony:** It's built into the material; don't oversell
