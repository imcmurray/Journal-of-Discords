## Sermon Overview

### Main Topic/Purpose
Church President John Taylor's defiant defense of polygamy (celestial marriage) as an unchangeable divine command. The sermon's central declaration: "This we cannot do; nor can we interfere with any of the commands of God to meet the persuasions or behests of men." Delivered at General Conference exactly **5 years, 11 months, and 18 days** before the 1890 Manifesto.

### Key Themes
- **Absolute refusal to compromise on polygamy:** "We cannot tone that principle down"
- **Divine commands cannot be changed:** "We cannot interfere with any of the commands of God"
- **Willingness to die for the principle:** "Living we will proclaim them, and dying we will be true to them"
- **Congregational ratification:** Members sustained with "Amen" the commitment to never surrender
- **Priesthood authority:** Only men who "abide my law shall preside over my Priesthood"
- **Defense against federal prosecution:** Detailed critique of Edmunds Act enforcement
- **Moral superiority claims:** Criminal statistics showing Mormons vs. Gentiles
- **Abortion condemnation:** Extensive quotes on abortion in Eastern states
- **Temple purity:** Warning against admitting adulterers to temples
- **Satan's opposition:** Standard persecution-as-proof-of-truth narrative

### Audience
General Conference attendees including priesthood leadership, general membership, and likely federal observers. This was a public, formal declaration of institutional policy by the Church President speaking from the pulpit.

### Historical Context

**Political/Legal Situation (October 1884):**
- **Edmunds Act (1882):** Federal law criminalizing polygamy and "unlawful cohabitation"
- **Edmunds-Tucker Act pending (1887):** Would disincorporate church, confiscate property, disenfranchise polygamous women
- **Underground period:** John Taylor himself would go into hiding in February 1885 (4 months after this sermon)
- **Taylor's death in hiding:** President Taylor died July 25, 1887 in exile at Thomas Roueche's home in Kaysville, Utah — never recanting polygamy
- **Federal pressure intensifying:** US Commissioners, prosecutors, judges actively prosecuting polygamists
- **"Test oath" controversy:** Oath required for voting/office excluded polygamists
- **Jury packing:** Grand juries systematically excluded Mormons, included prostitutes/adulterers per Taylor's complaint

**Church Leadership (1884):**
- **John Taylor:** Church President (1880-1887), practicing polygamist with multiple wives
- **George Q. Cannon:** First Counselor (spoke at same conference, JoD 25:268-275)
- **Joseph F. Smith:** Second Counselor (also spoke at conference)
- **All three:** Practicing polygamists who would go underground to avoid prosecution
- **Wilford Woodruff:** President of Quorum of Twelve, future church president who would issue 1890 Manifesto

**Timeline to Manifesto:**
- **October 6-7, 1884:** This sermon delivered
- **February 1, 1885:** Taylor goes underground (4 months later)
- **July 25, 1887:** Taylor dies in hiding (2 years, 9 months later)
- **April 7, 1889:** Wilford Woodruff becomes church president
- **September 24, 1890:** Manifesto issued (5 years, 11 months, 18 days after this sermon)

**Key Quote from Sermon:**
> "God has given us a revelation in regard to celestial marriage. I did not make it. He has told us certain things pertaining to this matter, and they would like us to tone that principle down and change it and make it applicable to the views of the day. **This we cannot do; nor can we interfere with any of the commands of God to meet the persuasions or behests of men. I cannot do it, and will not do it.**"

The congregation responded with a loud "Amen" — formally sustaining this position.

**The Irony:**
- Taylor: "We will stand by the principles of eternal truth; living we will proclaim them, and dying we will be true to them"
- Reality: Taylor DID die true to them (1887), but his successor Wilford Woodruff issued the Manifesto three years later
- The congregation that said "Amen" to "we cannot tone it down" accepted the Manifesto when it came
- Modern church teaches the Manifesto was inspired revelation, not surrender — contradicting Taylor's "cannot interfere with any of the commands of God"

## Key Quotations

### Quote 1: The Central Declaration — We Cannot Tone It Down

> "God has given us a revelation in regard to celestial marriage. I did not make it. He has told us certain things pertaining to this matter, and they would like us to tone that principle down and change it and make it applicable to the views of the day. This we cannot do; nor can we interfere with any of the commands of God to meet the persuasions or behests of men. I cannot do it, and will not do it."
> — JoD 25:309

**Context:** Taylor discussing external pressure to modify polygamy doctrine. He's received "advice from outsiders, from newspapers, etc., and sometimes from some of our brethren" to compromise.

**Significance:**
- **Absolute language:** "We CANNOT do" (not "won't" but "cannot" — theological impossibility)
- **Divine command:** "Commands of God" = unchangeable by humans
- **Presidential authority:** Church president declaring institutional position
- **Personal commitment:** "I cannot do it, and will not do it"
- **Six years later:** Church DID tone it down (Manifesto)
- **Modern irony:** If it was truly "command of God," Manifesto contradicts this; if it wasn't, Taylor was wrong about its divine origin

**Category:** FAILED-PROPH + IRONIC

### Quote 2: Living We Will Proclaim, Dying We Will Be True

> "We will stand by the principles of eternal truth; living we will proclaim them, and dying we will be true to them, and after death will live again in their enjoyment in the eternal worlds. That is my feeling; so I don't feel very trembly in the knees, and I do not think you do, generally."
> — JoD 25:309

**Context:** Immediately after refusing to "tone it down." Taylor expressing his personal resolve and assuming congregation agrees.

**Significance:**
- **Dying true:** Taylor DID die in hiding (1887) without recanting
- **Eternal principle:** Claims polygamy will be practiced "after death... in the eternal worlds"
- **No trembling:** Confident declaration of unwavering commitment
- **Six years later:** Church leadership (Woodruff) DID tremble, issued Manifesto
- **Irony:** Taylor kept his word (died in hiding 1887), but his successors didn't
- **Modern teaching:** Church still teaches polygamy in afterlife (men sealed to multiple wives), but not on earth

**Category:** IRONIC + SELF-CONTRA (across institution)

### Quote 3: Congregational Ratification — All Who Sanction It Say Amen

> "If God has introduced something for our glory and exaltation, we are not going to have that kicked over by any improper influence, either inside or outside of the Church of the living God. We will stand by the principles of eternal truth; living we will proclaim them, and dying we will be true to them, and after death will live again in their enjoyment in the eternal worlds. That is my feeling; so I don't feel very trembly in the knees, and I do not think you do, generally... and all who sanction it say Amen—[the vast congregation responded with a loud 'Amen.']"
> — JoD 25:309

**Context:** Taylor invoking congregational sustaining vote on refusing to compromise polygamy.

**Significance:**
- **Formal ratification:** Congregation formally sustained "never tone it down" position
- **General Conference setting:** Official church vote recorded in published record
- **"Vast congregation":** Thousands present, witnessed ratification
- **Six years later:** SAME congregation (or their children) sustained Manifesto
- **Opposite vote:** 1890 conference sustained abandoning what they'd sworn to uphold in 1884
- **Which was inspired?** If 1884 was, 1890 contradicts God; if 1890 was, 1884 was rebellion
- **Irony:** "We are not going to have that kicked over" → It was kicked over six years later

**Category:** IRONIC + SELF-CONTRA

**Context:** Referencing D&C 132 revelation on plural marriage. Taylor insisting only polygamists can lead the church.

- **D&C 132 reference:** "It is not meet that men who will not abide my law shall preside over my Priesthood"

- **1884 requirement:** Church leaders MUST be polygamists
- **1890 onwards:** Church leaders commanded to STOP polygamy
- **Modern church:** Led by monogamists since ~1910
- **Doctrinal contradiction:** D&C 132 still canonical, but "my law" redefined
- **Taylor's confidence:** "They CANNOT do it" (but they did)

**Category:** SELF-CONTRA + DISAVOWED

## Analysis

### Primary Category
**FAILED-PROPH** — Taylor prophesied (in effect) that the church would never compromise on polygamy ("we cannot do; nor can we interfere with any of the commands of God"), but six years later the church issued the Manifesto doing exactly that.

### Secondary Categories
- **IRONIC** — Nearly every declarative statement has ironic reversal
- **SELF-CONTRA** — Taylor's own behavior (going underground) contradicts his mockery of "creeping around"
- **DISAVOWED** — Modern church explicitly disavows the doctrinal position Taylor defended
- **MORAL-PROBLEM** — Congregational "Amen" created moral crisis: either 1884 vote was rebellion or 1890 surrender was apostasy

### Self-Contradiction Check

**Internal to this sermon?**

YES — Major self-contradiction:

1. **Mocking "creeping around":**
   - Taylor mocks those who "slip round a corner, or creep out in some way"
   - Compares them to Nicodemus who came to Jesus "by night"
   - Says "there is not much to such folks"

2. **Four months later:**
   - Taylor himself went underground (February 1, 1885)
   - Spent final 2.5 years in hiding
   - Died in exile (July 25, 1887)

3. **The contradiction:**
   - Condemned hiding practice while publicly defending it
   - Then hid his practice while privately maintaining it
   - The very behavior he mocked became his own

**Across speaker's other sermons?**

John Taylor was extremely consistent in defending polygamy across his tenure:
- JoD 21:9 (1879): "We shall not compromise on polygamy"
- JoD 24:192 (1883): Similar defenses of plural marriage
- JoD 25:84-96 (1884, same conference): "Object of Assembling, etc." - more polygamy defense
- JoD 25:261-267 (1884, same conference): "Why We Gather, etc." - references "this revelation" on marriage

Taylor was MORE consistent than most — he NEVER compromised, died in hiding defending it. The contradiction is between Taylor's 1884 statements and the church's 1890 decision under Woodruff.

**Across institution?**

MASSIVE contradictions:

1. **1884 Taylor:** "We cannot tone it down... nor can we interfere with any of the commands of God"
   **1890 Woodruff:** Manifesto suspends polygamy
   **Modern church:** "The Manifesto was inspired revelation"

2. **1884 Taylor:** "Men who will not abide my law shall [not] preside over my Priesthood"
   **Modern church:** All general authorities are monogamists (practicing polygamy = excommunication)

3. **1884 Congregation:** Sustained with "Amen" the commitment to never compromise
   **1890 Congregation:** Sustained the Manifesto compromising the principle
   **Question:** Which vote was inspired?

4. **1884 Taylor:** Polygamists are spiritually superior, monogamists need to "find some polygamists" for counsel
   **Modern church:** Polygamists are criminals/apostates, monogamous couples achieve same exaltation

5. **D&C 132 (1843, still canonical):** "New and everlasting covenant... if ye abide not that covenant, then are ye damned"
   **Modern church:** "We don't practice that anymore" (but it's still in scripture)

## Apologetic Anticipation

### Defense #1: "The Manifesto was revelation, so God changed His mind, not the church"

**Why it fails:**
- Taylor explicitly said "we cannot interfere with any of the commands of God to meet the persuasions or behests of men"
- If God commanded polygamy, then commanded cessation, the 1884 statement was false (God CAN change commands)
- If Manifesto was revelation, Taylor was wrong that they "cannot tone it down"
- Creates theological problem: Was 1884 Taylor speaking for God or not?
- Manifesto framed as response to government pressure (statehood, confiscation), which is exactly "persuasions or behests of men" that Taylor said they wouldn't accommodate

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #1: "This we cannot do; nor can we interfere with any of the commands of God to meet the persuasions or behests of men."

The Manifesto was literally interfering with a command of God (if polygamy was such) to meet persuasions and behests of men (federal government, desire for statehood). Taylor said this was impossible. It happened.

---

### Defense #2: "Taylor died before the Manifesto; he kept his word; only later leadership changed"

**Why it fails:**
- **Congregation sustained it:** Taylor invoked congregational "Amen" for "we will never tone it down"
- **Institutional continuity:** Taylor spoke for the church, not just himself
- **Same congregation (mostly):** Many who said "Amen" in 1884 said "Amen" to Manifesto in 1890
- **Priesthood succession:** Taylor said "men who will not abide my law shall not preside" — Woodruff (his successor) stopped abiding the law
- **Taylor anticipated this:** He was arguing against those who wanted to compromise, predicting church wouldn't/couldn't
- **If institutional:** Church broke promise; if personal: Taylor misrepresented institutional position

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #3: "All who sanction it say Amen—[the vast congregation responded with a loud 'Amen.']"

This wasn't just Taylor's personal commitment. The church body formally ratified it at General Conference. That same church body (or immediate descendants) reversed it six years later.

---

### Defense #3: "He said they 'cannot' as a moral position, not a prediction"

**Why it fails:**
- **Sermon context:** Taylor was addressing people who wanted them to change ("they would like us to tone that principle down")
- **Implicit prediction:** "We will not" functions as prediction when solemnly declared from pulpit
- **Dying promise:** "Living we will proclaim them, and dying we will be true to them" — promises future behavior
- **"After death" claim:** "And after death will live again in their enjoyment in the eternal worlds" — predicts eternal practice
- **Congregational vote:** Asking "Amen" creates institutional commitment, not just moral stance
- **Challenge to God:** "If God is with us, we ask no odds of the world" — implicitly claims God will preserve them

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #2: "Living we will proclaim them, and dying we will be true to them, and after death will live again in their enjoyment in the eternal worlds."

These are future-tense promises. They predict continued fidelity and eternal practice. The church broke fidelity (from 1884 perspective), though individuals (like Taylor) kept it.

---

### Defense #4: "The church still believes in plural marriage eternally, just not on earth"

**Why it fails:**
- **Taylor's context:** Defense was about earthly practice under federal prosecution
- **"Tone it down" means:** Stop practicing it openly, which is exactly what Manifesto did
- **Modern practice:** Complete reversal — earthly practice = excommunication
- **Temple ordinances:** 1884 temples performed plural sealings; modern temples don't (except widower remarriage)
- **"Living we will proclaim":** Taylor promised living proclamation, not just afterlife belief
- **FLDS problem:** Those who practice eternal belief on earth (like 1884 church) are excommunicated

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #5: "If you want to get along smoothly, you had better find among your various neighbors... some of these polygamists and ask a little counsel at their hands."

Taylor was talking about earthly practice and earthly spiritual authority. Modern church: finding polygamists means finding apostates.

---

### Defense #5: "Taylor was speaking as a man, not as prophet"

**Why it fails:**
- **Church President:** Highest authority in church
- **General Conference:** Official setting for church policy
- **Published in JoD:** Official record of sermon
- **Invoked God:** "God has given us a revelation... He has told us certain things"
- **Congregational sustaining:** Asked for "Amen" vote, creating formal church action
- **If speaking as man:** Then Church President at General Conference can be catastrophically wrong about core doctrine
- **Undermines all authority:** If this can be wrong, what can we trust?

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #4: "We have also been told that 'it is not mete that men who will not abide my law shall preside over my Priesthood'... if we are here... to do the will of our Father who sent us, and He has told us what to do, we will do it, in the name of Israel's God—and all who sanction it say Amen."

Taylor explicitly claims divine authorization ("He has told us what to do") and invokes God's name ("in the name of Israel's God"). This is formal prophetic declaration.

---

### Defense #6: "Circumstances changed; revelation is progressive"

**Why it fails:**
- **Taylor anticipated this:** He addressed those who wanted to "make it applicable to the views of the day" — explicitly rejected accommodation to changing circumstances
- **"Cannot interfere" language:** Not "won't unless circumstances change" but "cannot interfere with any of the commands of God"
- **"Commands of God" vs. "views of the day":** Taylor framed it as divine vs. human, unchangeable vs. changeable
- **Progressive revelation defense:** If God's commands change based on government pressure, they're not really commands
- **Modern problem:** What other "commands" might change based on circumstances? (LGBTQ+ issues?)

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #1: "They would like us to tone that principle down and change it and make it applicable to the views of the day. This we cannot do."

Taylor explicitly rejected the "make it applicable to the day" argument. That's exactly what apologetic claims happened.

---

### Defense #7: "The Manifesto was strategic, not doctrinal; practice continued secretly"

**Why it fails:**
- **Proves the point:** If Manifesto was lying to government, church DID "tone it down" in public
- **Taylor condemned this:** He mocked those who "want to slip round a corner, or creep out in some way... like Nicodemus"
- **Exactly what happened:** Post-Manifesto "new plural marriages" were secret, underground
- **Second Manifesto (1904):** Needed because secret practice continued
- **Validates Taylor's fear:** The "creeping" he condemned became official strategy
- **Modern teaching:** Church claims Manifesto was inspired revelation, not strategic deception

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #9: "People want to slip round a corner, or creep out in some way. There is something very creepy about it... There was a man in former times we are told, came to Jesus by night. His name was Nicodemus... I have known some people who would want to be baptized in the evening, or get into some corner that they might not be seen. Well, there is not much to such folks."

If post-Manifesto strategy was secret continuation, church became the "creepy" Nicodemus Taylor mocked.

---

### Defense #8: "Taylor and Woodruff both acted under inspiration for their time"

**Why it fails:**
- **Logical contradiction:** Taylor said they "cannot tone it down" — if he was inspired, Woodruff contradicted inspiration
- **Creates theological chaos:** If both were inspired with opposite commands, how do members know which prophet to follow?
- **"Commands of God" language:** Taylor said they cannot interfere with commands of God — if Woodruff was inspired to change, it wasn't a command, Taylor was wrong
- **Congregational vote:** 1884 congregation voted "Amen" to never change; 1890 congregation voted to change; one vote was wrong
- **Woodruff's own claim:** "The Lord will never permit me... to lead you astray" — if Taylor was right, Woodruff led them astray; if Woodruff was right, Taylor was wrong

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #1: "We cannot interfere with any of the commands of God to meet the persuasions or behests of men."

This is absolute language. No "unless a future prophet receives contrary inspiration." If it's a command of God (as Taylor claimed), future prophets can't interfere. If they can interfere, Taylor's statement was false.

---

### Defense #9: "We can't judge 19th century context by modern standards"

**Why it fails:**
- **This is internal contradiction:** Not judging by modern standards, but by Taylor's own 1884 words vs. church's 1890 action
- **Same historical moment:** Only six years apart, same generation, many same people
- **Taylor set the standard:** He declared "we cannot tone it down," we're holding him to his own standard
- **Modern church judges it:** By teaching Manifesto was inspired, church judges 1884 position as changeable
- **Historical context makes it worse:** 1884 was height of confidence; 1890 was surrender; context validates failure, doesn't excuse it

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #2: "Living we will proclaim them, and dying we will be true to them."

Taylor himself set the standard: lifelong/eternal fidelity. Church (institutionally) broke that standard within six years. This is his own metric.

---

### Defense #10: "God tests people differently in different eras"

**Why it fails:**
- **Test failed (by Taylor's standard):** If 1884 was a test of fidelity to polygamy, church failed by issuing Manifesto
- **Test succeeded (by modern standard):** If 1890 was test of following living prophet, church succeeded — but then 1884 Taylor was wrong
- **Can't have both:** Either polygamy was eternal principle (Taylor right, church wrong to change) or it was temporary (Taylor wrong, church right to change)
- **Taylor's language:** "Eternal truth," "commands of God," "after death will live again in their enjoyment" — not "temporary test"
- **Modern teaching problem:** If this was temporary test, what else might be? (Priesthood ban? Current positions?)

**Source rebuttal:**
Quote #3: "If God has introduced something for our glory and exaltation, we are not going to have that kicked over."

Taylor framed polygamy as essential for "glory and exaltation," not a temporary test. Modern church teaches monogamous couples achieve full exaltation. One of these positions is wrong.
