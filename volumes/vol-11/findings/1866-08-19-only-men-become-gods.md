## Sermon Overview

### Main Topic/Purpose
This sermon has three primary segments:
1. Praise for Delegate Hooper's service in Washington
2. Extended defense and doctrinal exposition of polygamy
3. Discussion of salvation, kingdoms of glory, and racial teachings

The bulk of the sermon (60%+) focuses on defending polygamy against "outside world" criticism and teaching that plural marriage is required for the highest degree of exaltation.

### Key Themes
- Polygamy as eternal requirement for exaltation
- Critique of Christian sexual hypocrisy
- Defense against federal anti-polygamy pressure
- Kingdoms of glory and degrees of salvation
- Polygamy as patriarchal pattern

### Audience
General membership gathered at the Bowery (outdoor tabernacle), including many women. Brigham specifically addresses "the ladies" multiple times, acknowledging their struggles with polygamy while insisting it's God's word.

### Historical Context
**1866** — Six years after the Republican platform called polygamy and slavery the "twin relics of barbarism." With slavery abolished (1865), polygamy was the next federal target. The Morrill Anti-Bigamy Act (1862) had been passed but not yet enforced. Brigham was preparing the Saints for sustained federal pressure.

This is also post-Civil War. Brigham references slavery's abolition and expresses concern for formerly enslaved people.

## Key Quotations

### Quote 1: THE ONLY MEN Who Become Gods

> "The only men who become Gods, even the Sons of God, are those who enter into polygamy. Others attain unto a glory and may even be permitted to come into the presence of the Father and the Son; but they cannot reign as kings in glory, because they had blessings offered unto them, and they refused to accept them."
> — JoD 11:269

**Context:** This appears in the middle of a long defense of polygamy. Brigham has just told the Elders and members that "it is in the hearts of many of them to wish that the doctrine of polygamy was not taught and practiced by us." He then delivers this absolute pronouncement.

**Significance:** This is categorical. Not "polygamy is better" or "polygamy brings greater blessings"—but ONLY polygamists become Gods. Monogamists, even faithful temple-sealed couples, "cannot reign as kings in glory."

**Category:** PRIMARY: CONTRA-MODERN / SECONDARY: DISAVOWED, SELF-CONTRA

**Cross-References:**
- JoD 25:309 (John Taylor's "never abandon" statement)
- D&C 132 (still in canon)
- Modern temple marriage (monogamous, yet promises exaltation)

---

### Quote 2: Polygamy in Your Faith or Come Short of Glory

> "You who wish that there were no such thing in existence, if you have in your hearts to say: 'We will pass along in the Church without obeying or submitting to it in our faith or believing this order, because, for aught that we know, this community may be broken up yet, and we may have lucrative offices offered to us; we will not, therefore, be polygamists lest we should fail in obtaining some earthly honor, character and office, etc,'—the man that has that in his heart, and will continue to persist in pursuing that policy, will come short of dwelling in the presence of the Father and the Son, in celestial glory."
> — JoD 11:269

**Context:** Immediately before the "only men" statement. Brigham acknowledges some Saints are hoping polygamy will go away so they can gain political offices and respectability.

**Significance:** Even believing in polygamy as an eternal principle is required. Not just practicing it—believing it. Those who hope it will be abandoned "will come short" of celestial glory.

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN, SELF-CONTRA

**Irony:** This exactly describes the modern LDS church—passing along without polygamy to gain respectability and avoid federal pressure. By Brigham's definition, they "come short of dwelling in the presence of the Father and the Son."

### Quote 3: Polygamists at Least in Your Faith

> "I wish to say to you, and all the world, that if you desire with all your hearts to obtain the blessings which Abraham obtained, you will be polygamists at least in your faith, or you will come short of enjoying the salvation and the glory which Abraham has obtained. This is as true as that God lives."
> — JoD 11:269

**Context:** Following the condemnation of those who wish polygamy didn't exist.

**Significance:** Invokes the authority of God's existence itself. "As true as that God lives"—this is definitive prophetic authority language. No hedging, no "in my opinion," no "I believe."

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN, SELF-CONTRA

**Cross-References:** Modern church does not require belief in polygamy as eternal law.

### Quote 4: They Cannot Be Crowned as Gods

> "John Wesley, and other great ecclesiastical reformers, could not attain to the same glory, by their own acts, while in the flesh that they would have done had the fullness of the Holy Priesthood been upon the earth in their day, and they had possessed all the glory and power and keys of it, and lived faithful to its requirements all their days. They cannot be crowned as Gods, even the Sons of God."
> — JoD 11:270

**Context:** Discussion of kingdoms of glory and who can attain godhood.

**Significance:** Even faithful, righteous people who lived before or without access to the "fullness of the Holy Priesthood" (which includes polygamy) cannot become Gods.

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN

**Implication:** By this logic, modern monogamous Latter-day Saints also cannot become Gods, since they don't have access to the "requirements" Brigham is describing.

### Quote 5: I Know It Is From God

> "I heard the revelation on polygamy, and I believed it with all my heart, and I know it is from God—I know that he revealed it from heaven; I know that it is true, and understand the bearings of it and why it is."
> — JoD 11:270

**Context:** Near the end of the polygamy section, responding to the question of whether they'll be admitted to statehood.

**Significance:** Strongest possible testimony language. Not "I believe" but "I KNOW." Direct revelation claim. He understands "the bearings of it and why it is"—claiming complete comprehension of the eternal principle.

**Category:** SELF-CONTRA (the church that claims him as prophet abandoned what he "knew" was from God)

### Quote 6: We Shall Never Be Admitted (Without Polygamy)

> "Do you think that we shall ever be admitted as a State into the Union without denying the principle of polygamy? If we are not admitted until then, we shall never be admitted."
> — JoD 11:270

**Context:** Answering the question about statehood.

**Significance:** Prophetic prediction. Brigham states the church will never deny polygamy to gain statehood.

**Category:** FAILED-PROPH

**What Actually Happened:**
- 1890 Manifesto suspended polygamy
- 1896 Utah granted statehood
- Brigham's absolute "never" was reversed in 24 years

### Quote 7: Marriage Is a Civil Contract (Libertarian Defense)

> "Marriage is a civil contract. You might as well make a law to say how many children a man shall have, as to make a law to say how many wives he shall have. It would be as sensible to make a law to say how many horses or oxen he shall possess, or how many cows his wife shall milk."
> — JoD 11:271

**Context:** Arguing that anti-polygamy laws are unconstitutional.

**Significance:** Treats wives as property comparable to livestock. The comparison is explicit: wives = horses = oxen = cows.

**Category:** MORAL-PROBLEM

**Modern Problem:** This reveals the property-view of women underlying polygamy theology.

The Government of the United States do not intend that that prophecy shall be fulfilled, and the Lord Almighty means that it shall. Do you not think that the Lord will conquer?"
> — JoD 11:271

**Context:** Discussing surplus women in Lowell, Massachusetts.

**Significance:** Prophecy that the Lord will fulfill Isaiah 4:1 through polygamy, in direct conflict with the U.S. government.

**Category:** FAILED-PROPH, CONTRA-MODERN

**What Actually Happened:** The U.S. government won. The church abandoned polygamy.

**Significance:** Eternal, not temporal. This is about exaltation in the resurrection, not just earthly family structure.

## Apologetic Anticipation

### Defense #1: "He Was Speaking as a Man"

**The Defense:**
"Brigham was expressing his personal opinion, not speaking as a prophet. The church doesn't claim prophetic infallibility."

**Why It Fails:**
1. **He explicitly claims divine knowledge:** "I KNOW it is from God—I know that he revealed it from heaven"
2. **He invokes God's existence as authority:** "This is as true as that God lives"
3. **He's teaching doctrine from the pulpit:** This is a formal sermon published in the official Journal of Discourses
4. **He claims to understand the principle:** "I understand the bearings of it and why it is"
5. **He's addressing all Saints:** "I wish to say to you, and all the world"

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
The entire sermon is saturated with prophetic authority markers. If this isn't prophetic teaching, then NOTHING Brigham said can be trusted as prophetic teaching. The "speaking as a man" defense would destroy all prophetic authority.

---

### Defense #2: "Polygamy Is Still Doctrine, Just Not Practiced"

**The Defense:**
"Eternal polygamy is still LDS doctrine—men can be sealed to multiple wives. It's just not practiced on earth right now."

**Why It Fails:**
1. **Brigham requires EARTHLY entry:** "Those who ENTER INTO polygamy" (present tense, mortal action)
2. **Modern monogamists attain godhood:** Current teaching is that monogamous temple marriage leads to exaltation
3. **The contradiction remains:** If ONLY polygamists become Gods, monogamists cannot—even if sealed
4. **"At least in your faith" requirement:** Brigham requires belief, modern church doesn't
5. **The church actively excommunicates polygamists:** Contradicts the "still doctrine" claim

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
Brigham condemned those who "wish that there were no such thing in existence" and hope to "pass along in the Church without obeying or submitting to it." This describes the modern church exactly. If the defense is "it's still doctrine," then why don't modern Saints believe it's required for exaltation?

---

### Defense #3: "Continuing Revelation Superseded It"

**The Defense:**
"The 1890 Manifesto was revelation that superseded the earlier requirement. God can change his requirements."

**Why It Fails:**
1. **The Manifesto never claims to be revelation:** It's a public statement of policy, not "thus saith the Lord"
2. **It doesn't say polygamy is wrong:** Just that the church is "not teaching" it
3. **D&C 132 is still canon:** The revelation commanding polygamy remains scripture
4. **Undermines prophetic trust:** If "as true as God lives" can be reversed, nothing is trustworthy
5. **Brigham said "I know":** Not "God currently requires" but "I KNOW it is from God"

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
Brigham: "I know it is from God—I know that he revealed it from heaven; I know that it is true, and understand the bearings of it and why it is."

Either:
- Brigham was wrong about what he "knew"
- God changed an "eternal principle"
- The modern church is wrong

All three options are problems for prophetic authority.

---

### Defense #4: "It Was Conditional Based on Circumstances"

**The Defense:**
"Polygamy was required under certain historical circumstances but not as a universal, eternal law."

**Why It Fails:**
1. **Brigham makes it absolute:** "ONLY men who enter into polygamy"—no conditions, no exceptions
2. **He ties it to Abraham:** "The blessings which Abraham obtained"—presented as eternal pattern
3. **"For the resurrection" not "for this time":** Brigham explicitly says it's eternal, not temporal
4. **"As true as God lives":** No hedging, no conditional language
5. **John Taylor's logic:** "Do away with that = do away with religion"—presented as foundational, not conditional

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
"What is this order for? It is for the resurrection; it is not for this world. I would not go across this bowery for polygamy, if it only pertained to this world."

Brigham is explicitly teaching this is ETERNAL, not circumstantial. The "conditional" defense contradicts the source.

---

### Defense #5: "You're Taking It Out of Context"

**The Defense:**
"You're cherry-picking quotes without understanding the full sermon context."

**Why It Fails:**
1. **The full context makes it worse:** The entire middle section is about polygamy as eternal requirement
2. **Multiple reinforcing quotes:** Not one statement but repeated assertions throughout
3. **The audience confirms it:** Brigham acknowledges Saints struggle with this teaching, showing they understood it as requirement
4. **Published in official record:** This was canonized in JoD as authoritative teaching
5. **Consistent with D&C 132:** The revelation itself says "if ye abide not that covenant, then are ye damned"

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
Read the full sermon. The context is:
- Defense against government pressure
- Teaching that polygamy is required for exaltation
- Condemning those who hope it will go away
- Prophesying the church will never abandon it
- Invoking God's existence as guarantee of truth

The full context makes the problem worse, not better.

---

### Defense #6: "The Government Forced Them to Stop"

**The Defense:**
"The church had to abandon polygamy due to federal prosecution—it wasn't a theological change."

**Why It Fails:**
1. **Brigham prophesied they'd never yield:** "If we are not admitted until then, we shall never be admitted"
2. **This admits defeat by secular power:** God's "eternal law" overridden by Congress?
3. **Contradicts "The Lord will conquer":** Brigham asked "Do you not think that the Lord will conquer?"—the Lord didn't
4. **Makes it pragmatic, not revelatory:** If it was just political surrender, it's not "continuing revelation"
5. **Undermines prophetic protection promises:** Where was the divine deliverance?

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
"The Government of the United States do not intend that that prophecy shall be fulfilled, and the Lord Almighty means that it shall. Do you not think that the Lord will conquer?"

The Lord did not conquer. The government won. Brigham's confidence was misplaced.

---

### Defense #7: "We Don't Understand the Mysteries Fully"

**The Defense:**
"The nature of exaltation and eternal marriage are mysteries we don't fully comprehend. We have to trust that God knows best."

**Why It Fails:**
1. **Brigham claimed to understand fully:** "I understand the bearings of it and why it is"
2. **The prophets understood it well enough to require it:** Can't claim mystery when they were categorical
3. **"ONLY" is not mysterious:** The word "only" is exclusionary and clear
4. **"As true as God lives" leaves no room for mystery:** Brigham anchored it to God's existence
5. **This defense is an admission of failure:** "We don't understand" = we can't defend it

**Source-Based Rebuttal:**
If Brigham "understood the bearings of it and why it is," then either:
- He was wrong (undermines prophetic authority)
- It's still true (undermines modern church)
- It changed (undermines eternal principles)

The "mystery" defense doesn't solve any of these problems.

### Challenges

**Challenge 1: Sensitivity to Modern Practicing Mormons**
- Many listeners will be monogamous temple-married Mormons
- The song directly tells them their exaltation is invalid per Brigham
- Must balance truth-telling with awareness of pain

**Mitigation:** Let Brigham's words do the condemning. Don't add mockery—the contrast speaks for itself. The song can be firm but not cruel.

**Challenge 2: Explaining the Reversal Without Losing Momentum**
- Need to mention 1890 Manifesto
- Need to explain the "never be admitted" → statehood sequence
- Risk: Too much exposition kills the song

**Mitigation:** Use minimal, clear language. "1890, gave it up" is enough. Trust the audience to get it.

**Challenge 3: D&C 132 Complexity**
- The revelation is still canon but reinterpreted
- Explaining this takes time
- Risk: Getting bogged down in nuance

**Mitigation:** One line: "The scripture stays, the practice goes"—that's enough to flag the absurdity.

**Challenge 4: Multiple Strong Hooks**
- "Only men who become Gods"
- "As true as God lives"
- "We shall never be admitted"
- "The Lord will conquer"
- Risk: Trying to use all of them dilutes focus

**Mitigation:** Pick ONE as the primary hook (recommend "The only men who become Gods"). Use others as supporting lines.

**Challenge 5: Avoiding Cheap Shots**
- Easy to mock polygamy itself
- Easy to make jokes about multiple wives
- Risk: Undermining the serious doctrinal point

**Mitigation:** Stay focused on the REQUIREMENT and REVERSAL. This isn't about polygamy being weird—it's about prophets being categorically wrong about salvation.
