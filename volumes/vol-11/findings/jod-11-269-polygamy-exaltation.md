## Sermon Overview

### Main Topic/Purpose
Defense of polygamy as essential religious doctrine in response to federal anti-polygamy legislation. Brigham explicitly states that polygamy is REQUIRED for the highest level of exaltation (becoming a God).

### Key Themes
- Polygamy as requirement for godhood/exaltation
- Defense against "twin relics of barbarism" rhetoric (slavery and polygamy)
- Criticism of Christian sexual hypocrisy
- Racial curse doctrine (seed of Cain)
- Physical paternity of Jesus by God the Father
- Salvation tiers and kingdoms of glory

### Audience
General membership, Great Salt Lake City residents, with awareness that sermon would be published nationally (hence defensive tone regarding federal legislation)

### Historical Context
- **Post-Civil War era:** Slavery abolished 1865, polygamy now sole remaining "twin relic"
- **Political pressure:** Republican party platform opposed both slavery and polygamy
- **Federal legislation:** Anti-polygamy laws being debated in Congress
- **Utah statehood:** Polygamy seen as barrier to statehood admission
- **Delegate Hooper:** Just returned from representing Utah Territory in Washington D.C.
- **Persecution complex:** Saints feeling under siege, defensive about practice

## Key Quotations

### Quote 1: The ONLY Men Who Become Gods Are Polygamists

> "The only men who become Gods, even the Sons of God, are those who enter into polygamy. Others attain unto a glory and may even be permitted to come into the presence of the Father and the Son; but they cannot reign as kings in glory, because they had blessings offered unto them, and they refused to accept them."
> — JoD 11:269

**Context:** Mid-sermon, after criticizing Christian sexual hypocrisy and defending polygamy as revealed doctrine. Brigham is addressing internal doubts among members who wish polygamy "was not taught and practiced by us."

**Significance:**
- **Categorical exclusion:** "The ONLY men" leaves no exceptions
- **Three-tier system:** Gods (polygamists) / Lesser glory (monogamists) / Lower kingdoms (others)
- **Choice framing:** Non-polygamists "refused to accept" blessings
- **Eternal consequences:** Cannot "reign as kings in glory"
- **Direct modern contradiction:** Current LDS church teaches monogamous couples can achieve full exaltation

**Category:** DISAVOWED + CONTRA-MODERN

---

### Quote 2: Faith in Polygamy Required

> "I wish to say to you, and all the world, that if you desire with all your hearts to obtain the blessings which Abraham obtained, you will be polygamists at least in your faith, or you will come short of enjoying the salvation and the glory which Abraham has obtained."
> — JoD 11:269

**Context:** Immediately preceding Quote #1, addressing members who hope polygamy might go away. Brigham insists even belief/acceptance is mandatory.

**Significance:**
- **Minimum requirement:** "At least in your faith"
- **Abramic covenant:** Ties to still-taught doctrine
- **Impossible position:** Modern members can't "believe" in discontinued practice
- **Faith vs. practice:** Even private belief required

**Category:** DISAVOWED + CONTRA-MODERN

---

### Quote 3: Polygamy for Resurrection, Not This World

> "What is this order for? It is for the resurrection; it is not for this world. I would not go across this bowery for polygamy, if it only pertained to this world. It is for the resurrection."
> — JoD 11:269

**Context:** Later in sermon, explaining eternal purpose of polygamy practice. Admits no earthly value.

**Significance:**
- **Reveals motivation:** Entirely about eternal exaltation
- **Contradicts apologetics:** Not about caring for widows, helping women, temporal benefits
- **Resurrection theology:** Polygamy seals apply in afterlife
- **Eternal families:** Plural wives eternal, not temporal convenience

**Category:** DISAVOWED (contradicts modern apologetic explanations)

### Quote 4: Mary Had Two Husbands (God and Joseph)

> "This matter was a little changed in the case of the Savior of the world, the Son of the living God. The man Joseph, the husband of Mary, did not, that we know of, have more than one wife, but Mary the wife of Joseph had another husband. On this account infidels have called the Savior a bastard. This is merely a human opinion upon one of the inscrutable doings of the Almighty. That very babe that was cradled in the manger, was begotten, not by Joseph, the husband of Mary, but by another Being. Do you inquire by whom? He was begotten by God our heavenly Father."
> — JoD 11:268

**Context:** Defending polygamy by citing ancient examples. Brigham presents Jesus as product of celestial plural marriage.

**Significance:**
- **Physical paternity:** God literally fathered Jesus physically
- **Polyandry defense:** Mary had two husbands simultaneously
- **Acknowledges controversy:** "Infidels have called the Savior a bastard"
- **Modern avoidance:** Church no longer teaches this explicitly
- **Christological implications:** Jesus as literal offspring of sexual union

**Category:** DISAVOWED + MORAL-PROBLEM

---

### Quote 5: Cursed With a Skin of Blackness (Seed of Cain)

> "The Lamanites or Indians are just as much the children of our Father and God as we are. So also are the Africans. But we are also the children of adoption through obedience to the Gospel of his Son. Why are so many of the inhabitants of the earth cursed with a sin of blackness? It comes in consequence of their fathers rejecting the power of the Holy Priesthood, and the law of God. They will go down to death. And when all the rest of the children have received their blessings in the Holy Priesthood, then that curse will be removed from the seed of Cain, and they will then come up and possess the priesthood, and receive all the blessings which we now are entitled to."
> — JoD 11:272

**Context:** End of sermon, explaining kingdoms of glory and who receives what blessings. Addresses why some don't have priesthood.

**Significance:**
- **Racial curse:** Blackness as punishment for ancestors' choices
- **Seed of Cain:** Attributes to rejecting priesthood
- **Last in line:** Receive blessings only after "all the rest"
- **2013 disavowal:** Gospel Topics essay explicitly rejects this reasoning

**Category:** DISAVOWED (officially disavowed 2013)

---

### Quote 6: Seven Women Shall Take Hold of One Man (Failed Prophecy)

> "Two thousand good, God-fearing men should go there, and take to themselves seven wives a-piece. It is written in the Bible, 'and in that day seven women shall take hold of one man, saying, we will eat our own bread, and wear our own apparel; only let us be called by thy name, to take away our reproach.' The Government of the United States do not intend that that prophecy shall be fulfilled, and the Lord Almighty means that it shall. Do you not think that the Lord will conquer? I think he will, and we are helping him."
> — JoD 11:270

**Context:** Discussing Lowell, Massachusetts textile mills with 14,000 more women than men. Brigham presents this as setup for polygamy prophecy fulfillment.

**Significance:**
- **Isaiah 4:1 interpretation:** Seven women per man as polygamy prophecy
- **God vs. US Government:** Framed as cosmic battle
- **Active role:** "We are helping him" = Mormons implementing prophecy
- **Failed prophecy:** Did NOT come to pass; polygamy discontinued, not expanded
- **Confidence vs. reality:** "The Lord will conquer" but government won

**Category:** FAILED-PROPH

---

### Quote 7: John Wesley Cannot Become a God

> "John Wesley, and other great ecclesiastical reformers, could not attain to the same glory, by their own acts, while in the flesh that they would have done had the fullness of the Holy Priesthood been upon the earth in their day, and they had possessed all the glory and power and keys of it, and lived faithful to its requirements all their days. They cannot be crowned as Gods, even the Sons of God."
> — JoD 11:271

**Context:** Explaining salvation tiers and who ends up where. Discussing intermediate kingdoms between celestial glory and perdition.

**Significance:**
- **Protestant reformers excluded:** Even great men can't achieve godhood
- **Priesthood requirement:** No priesthood = no exaltation
- **Timing matters:** Born in wrong era = eternal limitation
- **Mormon exclusivity:** Only LDS priesthood holders eligible for godhood

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN (modern church emphasizes broader salvation)

## Apologetic Anticipation

### Defense #1: "Brigham was speaking as a man, not as a prophet"

**Why it fails:**
- This is a formal sermon delivered from pulpit, reported by official scribe G.D. Watt
- Published in Journal of Discourses as official church position
- Presented as revelation: "The Lord gave a revelation through Joseph Smith"
- If prophet can be this catastrophically wrong about EXALTATION REQUIREMENTS, what else?

**Source rebuttal:**
"I heard the revelation on polygamy, and I believed it with all my heart, and I know it is from God—I know that he revealed it from heaven" (JoD 11:270)

### Defense #2: "Polygamy WAS required then, but revelation changed it"

**Why it fails:**
- If exaltation requirements can change, they're arbitrary
- Brigham said "The ONLY men" — no conditions, no time limits
- 1890 Manifesto stopped practice but didn't revoke D&C 132
- No new revelation explicitly changing exaltation requirements
- Creates theological crisis: was God wrong before, or now?

**Source rebuttal:**
"If it is wrong for a man to have more than one wife at a time, the Lord will reveal it by and by, and he will put it away that it will not be known in the Church." (JoD 11:268) — No such revelation given; practice just discontinued under political pressure.

### Defense #3: "This quote is taken out of context"

**Why it fails:**
- Full sermon makes it WORSE, not better
- Context is defending polygamy against federal pressure
- Brigham addresses internal doubts and insists on belief even if not practicing
- Categorical language: "The ONLY men" is unambiguous
- Reading full sermon shows this is central thesis, not aside

**Source rebuttal:**
Provide full paragraph showing this is the main point of the doctrinal section, not a stray comment.

### Defense #4: "Journal of Discourses isn't official scripture"

**Why it fails:**
- Was official publication of sermons for decades
- If we can't trust published prophet sermons, what CAN we trust?
- Brigham Young was prophet/president when this was delivered and published
- No retraction or correction ever issued
- Sets dangerous precedent: any inconvenient teaching can be dismissed

**Source rebuttal:**
"I wish to say to you, and all the world..." — Brigham explicitly addressing broad audience, not casual remark.

### Defense #5: "Polygamy was needed then to care for widows/build up kingdom"

**Why it fails:**
Brigham himself says it's NOT for this world: "I would not go across this bowery for polygamy, if it only pertained to this world. It is for the resurrection." (JoD 11:269)

**Source rebuttal:**
"What is this order for? It is for the resurrection; it is not for this world." — Brigham explicitly rejects temporal justifications.

### Defense #6: "We don't understand the full context of 1860s Utah"

**Why it fails:**
- Context makes it MORE problematic (federal pressure, defensiveness)
- Understanding context doesn't change categorical claim about exaltation
- Theological claims should transcend temporal circumstances
- Brigham presents this as eternal principle, not situational advice

**Source rebuttal:**
"This is as true as that God lives." (JoD 11:269) — Presented as eternal truth, not time-bound counsel.
