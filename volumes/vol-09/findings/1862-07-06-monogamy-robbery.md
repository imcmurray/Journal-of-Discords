## Sermon Overview

### Main Topic/Purpose
A wide-ranging sermon defending the LDS faith against mainstream Christianity, with particular emphasis on plural marriage as the only "popular religion" in heaven. Brigham argues that Latter-day Saints experience joy while "the wicked" experience sorrow, and that the Mormon way leads to comfort while traditional Christianity leads to suffering.

### Key Themes
- Superiority of Mormon religion over sectarian Christianity
- Plural marriage as Abrahamic and heavenly doctrine
- Monogamy as a corrupt Roman invention
- Saints' comfort vs. sinners' suffering
- Defense against federal anti-polygamy legislation

### Audience
General membership at the Bowery (regular Sunday congregation)

### Historical Context
**CRITICAL TIMING:** This sermon was delivered in **July 1862**, the exact same month that the U.S. Congress passed the **Morrill Anti-Bigamy Act** (signed July 8, 1862) — the first federal law criminalizing polygamy in U.S. territories. This sermon is a direct defiant response to federal legislation.

The Morrill Act:
- Made bigamy in territories a federal crime
- Punishable by 5 years in prison and $500 fine
- Limited church holdings to $50,000
- Was specifically targeted at Utah Mormons
- Signed by Lincoln just 2 days after this sermon

This sermon represents Brigham's theological counterpunch to federal authority.

## Key Quotations

### Quote 1: Monogamy Established by Robbers

> "Monogamy, or restrictions by law to one wife, is no part of the economy of Heaven among men. Such s system was commenced by the founders of the Roman empire. That empire was founded on the banks of the Tiber by wandering brigands. When these robbers founded the city of Rome, it was evident to them that their success in attaining a balance of power with their neighbours, depended upon introducing females into their body politic, so they stole them from the Sabines, who were near neighbours. The scarcity of women gave existence to laws restricting one wife to one man. Rome became the mistress of the world, and introduced this order of monogamy wherever her sway was acknowledged. Thus this monogamic order of marriage, so esteemed by modern Christians as a holy sacrament and divine institution, is nothing but a system established by a set of robbers."
> — JoD 9:322-323

**Context:** Immediately follows defense of Abraham's polygamy and immediately precedes discussion of the Morrill Anti-Bigamy Act. This is Brigham's historical/theological justification for rejecting monogamy.

**Significance:**
- Calls monogamy "nothing but a system established by a set of robbers"
- Denies monogamy is "part of the economy of Heaven"
- Describes it as a Roman invention, not divine institution
- Calls it corrupt, criminal in origin, and unholy
- Directly contradicts modern church teaching

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN (primary), IRONIC

## Apologetic Anticipation

### Defense #1: "Speaking About That Time Period"

**What they'll say:**
"Brigham was speaking about the historical origins of Roman monogamy laws, not condemning all monogamy for all time. He was providing historical context for why the church practiced differently."

**Why it fails:**
The text is explicit and absolute:
- "Monogamy... is **no part of the economy of Heaven among men**" (not "was no part in that era")
- "Thus this monogamic order... is **nothing but** a system established by robbers" (categorical rejection)
- "[Polygamy] is **the only popular religion** there [in heaven]" (exclusive claim)
- Context is defending polygamy against federal law, not merely explaining history

**Source rebuttal:**
> "Monogamy, or restrictions by law to one wife, is no part of the economy of Heaven among men."

The phrase "economy of Heaven among men" is universal, not time-bound. Brigham is making a theological claim about God's eternal order, not a historical observation.

---

### Defense #2: "Product of His Time / Cultural Context"

**What they'll say:**
"Brigham was defending against persecution and federal legislation. This was a defensive response to the Morrill Act, not a doctrinal declaration. We need to understand the cultural and political pressure he was under."

**Why it fails:**
1. The "product of his time" defense contradicts prophetic authority
2. If political pressure excuses false doctrine, then the 1890 Manifesto (ending polygamy due to federal pressure) was also just politics
3. Brigham explicitly frames this as theology, not politics: "the economy of Heaven," "the only popular religion there," "the religion of Abraham"
4. He claims divine revelation: "the Lord introduced it to his servants in a revelation"

**Source rebuttal:**
> "Why do we believe in and practise polygamy? **Because the Lord introduced it to his servants in a revelation given to Joseph Smith**, and the Lord's servants have always practiced it."

This is theological, revelatory authority, not cultural opinion.

---

### Defense #3: "He Was Talking About Coercive Monogamy Laws"

**What they'll say:**
"Brigham was condemning laws that force monogamy, not monogamy itself. He was defending religious freedom, not attacking monogamous marriage. The modern church doesn't force polygamy on anyone; people choose monogamy freely."

**Why it fails:**
The sermon goes far beyond condemning coercion:
- Calls monogamy "no part of the economy of Heaven" (theological claim)
- Says polygamy is "the only popular religion" in heaven (salvation claim)
- States monogamy is "nothing but" a robbers' system (categorical rejection)
- Ties polygamy to being "Abraham's seed and heirs according to promise" (exaltation requirement)

**Source rebuttal:**
> "And is that religion [polygamy] popular in heaven? It is **the only popular religion there**, for this is the religion of Abraham, and, **unless we do the works of Abraham, we are not Abraham's seed and heirs according to promise**."

This is soteriological (salvation-related), not merely about religious freedom.

---

### Defense #4: "Continuing Revelation Changed the Practice"

**What they'll say:**
"The church believes in continuing revelation. The 1890 Manifesto was new revelation superseding earlier practice. God can change commandments based on circumstances, just as He did with the Law of Moses."

**Why it fails:**
1. The Manifesto doesn't claim to be revelation (it's a press statement)
2. Brigham's language is absolute and eternal: "economy of Heaven," "the only popular religion there"
3. D&C 132 remains canonized scripture
4. If polygamy was "the only popular religion" in heaven, how can heaven change its mind?
5. This defense admits prophets were wrong about eternal principles
6. The Law of Moses analogy fails because Christ fulfilled the law; what fulfilled polygamy?

**Source rebuttal:**
> "Monogamy, or restrictions by law to one wife, is **no part of the economy of Heaven among men**."

"Economy of Heaven" implies eternal divine order, not temporal commandment subject to change.

---

### Defense #5: "Celestial Polygamy Still Exists"

**What they'll say:**
"The church still practices celestial polygamy. Men can be sealed to multiple wives if widowed. The eternal principle remains; only the temporal practice changed."

**Why it fails:**
1. Brigham said "unless we **do** the works of Abraham" (present practice, not future sealing)
2. Modern members don't practice polygamy, yet church teaches they can be exalted
3. This defense actually proves the contradiction: if the eternal principle requires practice ("do the works"), then modern members are excluded
4. Women can't be sealed to multiple men, so this defense is patriarchal and inconsistent

**Source rebuttal:**
> "Unless we **do** the works of Abraham, we are not Abraham's seed and heirs according to promise."

"Do" is active, practical, present-tense. Not "are sealed in principle to" but "do."

---

### Defense #6: "He Was Using Hyperbolic Rhetoric"

**What they'll say:**
"Brigham was known for colorful, forceful language. This was rhetorical emphasis, not literal doctrine. He was making a point about religious freedom and persecution."

**Why it fails:**
1. If prophetic sermons can be dismissed as "hyperbole," all prophetic authority is undermined
2. The sermon is systematic, theological, and scripturally grounded (cites Paul, Abraham)
3. He invokes revelation (D&C 132), making this doctrinal, not rhetorical
4. The entire sermon is structured as theological argument, not emotional outburst
5. Which parts are hyperbole and which are binding? Who decides?

**Source rebuttal:**
The sermon's structure is methodical and doctrinal:
- Historical claim (Rome's founding)
- Theological claim (Heaven's economy)
- Scriptural support (Abraham, Paul)
- Revelatory authority (D&C 132)
- Salvation requirement (heirs according to promise)

This is doctrine, not hyperbole.

---

### Defense #7: "We Don't Understand All the Mysteries"

**What they'll say:**
"We don't fully understand the eternal nature of marriage. Brigham was speaking from his understanding, but the full truth may be more complex than either polygamy or monogamy as we understand them."

**Why it fails:**
1. Brigham claimed certainty: "the only popular religion there," "is nothing but"
2. If prophets don't understand what they're teaching as doctrine, trust is destroyed
3. This defense is an admission of the problem without solving it
4. Members were required to practice polygamy based on this "partial understanding"
5. Modern members are required to practice monogamy — if we "don't understand," how can either be required?

**Source rebuttal:**
> "Why do we believe in and practise polygamy? Because **the Lord introduced it to his servants in a revelation given to Joseph Smith**."

Brigham grounds this in divine revelation, not personal speculation.
