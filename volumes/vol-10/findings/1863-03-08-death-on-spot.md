## Sermon Overview

### Main Topic/Purpose
A wide-ranging sermon defending the Saints' loyalty to the Constitution despite persecution, recounting the Mormon Battalion experience, and addressing accusations of disloyalty. The sermon culminates in declarations about race relations, slavery, abolition, and God's law regarding interracial marriage.

### Key Themes
- Defense against accusations of disloyalty/secessionism
- Mormon Battalion as proof of patriotism
- Outside pressure and persecution narrative
- Criticism of both abolitionists ("black-hearted Republicans") and pro-slavery advocates
- "The law of God" regarding racial mixing
- Death penalty for interracial marriage
- Eternal nature of racial separation

### Audience
General membership in Salt Lake City, with awareness that the sermon would be published nationally in Journal of Discourses. Mixed audience of men, women, and children.

### Historical Context
- **Civil War ongoing:** March 1863, Union struggling, Emancipation Proclamation issued Jan 1, 1863
- **Slavery question:** Central national issue, Brigham positioning LDS church as neutral
- **Utah Territory status:** Federal troops stationed in SLC (referenced in sermon)
- **Reconstruction debates:** Questions about Black citizenship and rights emerging
- **Mormon persecution memory:** Fresh memory of Missouri/Illinois expulsions, Mountain Meadows Massacre accusations
- **1857 Utah War:** Six years past but still referenced as proof of government persecution
- **Federal pressure on polygamy:** Anti-polygamy legislation pending in Congress

## Key Quotations

### Quote 1: "The Law of God" — Death Penalty for Interracial Marriage

> "Shall I tell you the law of God in regard to the African race? If the white man who belongs to the chosen seed mixes his blood with the seed of Cain, the penalty, under the law of God, is death on the spot. This will always be so."
> — JoD 10:110

**Context:** End of sermon, after criticizing both abolitionists and pro-slavery advocates. Brigham pivots to declaring God's eternal law regarding race mixing. This comes immediately after calling abolitionists "rank, rabid" and "black-hearted Republicans" who "have set the whole national fabric on fire."

**Significance:**
- **Divine authority claim:** "The law of God" — not opinion, not cultural preference, but divine commandment
- **Capital punishment:** "Death on the spot" — immediate execution
- **Theological framing:** "Chosen seed" vs. "seed of Cain" — racial hierarchy as cosmic/eternal
- **Permanence claim:** "This will always be so" — presented as unchangeable, eternal law
- **No qualifications:** No exceptions, no conditions, categorical statement
- **Direct modern contradiction:** Church now explicitly disavows this teaching and condemns racism

**Category:** MORAL-PROBLEM (primary), DISAVOWED (secondary), CONTRA-MODERN (secondary)

---

### Quote 2: Seed of Cain and Transgression

> "The nations of the earth have transgressed every law that God has given, they have changed the ordinances and broken every covenant made with the fathers, and they are like a hungry man that dreameth that he eateth, and he awaketh and behold he is empty."
> — JoD 10:110

**Context:** Immediately following the "death on the spot" declaration. Brigham links racial mixing to broader covenant-breaking.

**Significance:**
- **Nations condemned:** Interracial mixing presented as universal transgression
- **Covenant violation:** Racial separation as covenant requirement
- **Empty promises:** Those who violate will find their hopes void
- **Theological weight:** Connects race doctrine to covenant theology

**Category:** DISAVOWED, MORAL-PROBLEM

### Quote 3: Criticism of Abolitionists

> "The rank, rabid abolitionists, whom I call black-hearted Republicans, have set the whole national fabric on fire. Do you know this, Democrats? They have kindled the fire that is raging now from the north to the south, and from the south to the north."
> — JoD 10:110

**Context:** Transitional paragraph before the "law of God" declaration. Brigham blames Civil War on abolitionists.

**Significance:**
- **Civil War blame:** Abolitionists, not slaveholders, caused the war
- **Racial epithet:** "Black-hearted" Republicans — links blackness to evil
- **Political positioning:** Distancing from both sides while condemning abolition more harshly
- **Irony:** Immediately precedes death penalty declaration for interracial marriage
- **Modern embarrassment:** Church now condemns racism, claims to support civil rights

**Category:** MORAL-PROBLEM, CONTRA-MODERN

### Quote 4: "Neither Abolitionist Nor Pro-Slavery"

> "I am no abolitionist, neither am I a proslavery man; I hate some of their principles and especially some of their conduct, as I do the gates of hell. The Southerners make the negroes, and the Northerners worship them; this is all the difference between slaveholders and abolitionists. I would like the President of the United States and all the world to hear this."
> — JoD 10:110

**Context:** Setting up his racial theology as distinct from both North and South positions.

**Significance:**
- **False equivalence:** Slavery = "making negroes," abolition = "worshipping them"
- **Both-sides claim:** Positions himself as reasonable middle ground
- **Public declaration:** "All the world to hear this" — intentionally provocative, public stance
- **Moral clarity claim:** Implies his position is morally superior to both
- **Reality check:** Follows with death penalty for interracial marriage — far more extreme than either side

**Category:** MORAL-PROBLEM, SELF-CONTRA (claims neutrality then takes extreme position)

### Quote 5: If Congress Can Regulate Marriage, It Can Prohibit Abuse

> "If the Government of the United States, in Congress assembled, had the right to pass an anti-polygamy bill, they had also the right to pass a law that slaves should not be abused as they have been; they had also a right to make a law that negroes should be used like human beings, and not worse than dumb brutes. For their abuse of that race, the whites will be cursed, unless they repent."
> — JoD 10:110

**Context:** After "death on the spot" declaration, critiquing Congressional authority over marriage while defending limited government regulation of slavery.

**Significance:**
- **Polygamy defense:** If government regulates marriage, it overreaches
- **Slavery admission:** Acknowledges abuse of enslaved people
- **Divine curse:** Whites will be cursed for abuse (but not for enslavement itself)
- **Irony:** Defends government restraint on marriage law while proposing death penalty for interracial marriage
- **Self-contradiction:** "Used like human beings" vs. kept as property

**Category:** SELF-CONTRA, MORAL-PROBLEM

### Quote 6: Pro-Slavery Men Were Persecutors

> "I am neither an abolitionist nor a pro-slavery man. If I could have been influenced by private injury to choose one side in preference to the other, I should certainly be against the pro-slavery side of the question, for it was pro-slavery men that pointed the bayonet at me and my brethren in Missouri, and said, 'Damn you we will kill you.'"
> — JoD 10:110

**Context:** Explaining his claimed neutrality through personal persecution narrative.

**Significance:**
- **Missouri persecution:** Invokes mob violence to establish credibility
- **Anti-South lean:** Despite neutrality claim, personal grievance against South
- **Gospel exception:** "Only in the Gospel" — would save them spiritually but not support politically
- **Missing logic:** Personal injury from pro-slavery advocates doesn't lead to opposing slavery
- **Maintained neutrality:** Even after persecution, refuses to condemn slavery itself

**Category:** SELF-CONTRA (claims neutrality but holds grudge against one side)

## Apologetic Anticipation

### Defense #1: "Brigham was speaking as a man, not as a prophet"

**Why it fails:**
1. **Explicit divine authority claim:** "Shall I tell you THE LAW OF GOD" — not opinion, not speculation, explicitly claimed as God's law
2. **Published officially:** Appeared in Journal of Discourses, official sermon publication
3. **From the pulpit:** Delivered as Sunday sermon in Tabernacle, prophet's official teaching venue
4. **No qualifiers:** Brigham didn't say "I think" or "in my opinion," he said "the law of God"
5. **Slippery slope:** If this isn't prophetic teaching, what is? If "law of God" claims can be wrong, nothing is reliable

**Source rebuttal:**
The exact words are "Shall I tell you THE LAW OF GOD in regard to the African race?" This is not ambiguous. He explicitly frames it as divine law, not personal opinion. When prophets claim to speak for God and are catastrophically wrong, the "speaking as a man" defense destroys prophetic authority entirely.

---

### Defense #2: "Product of his time / cultural context"

**Why it fails:**
1. **This defense proves the point:** If prophets merely reflect cultural racism rather than transcending it, they're not prophets
2. **Other Christians opposed slavery:** Quakers, some evangelicals, abolitionists existed in same era
3. **More extreme than culture:** Even in 1863, death penalty for interracial marriage was extreme (not standard position)
4. **Divine law claim:** "Law of God" elevates beyond cultural opinion
5. **"Always be so" claim:** Brigham presented this as eternal, not time-bound
6. **Prophet's job:** Should be to correct culture's errors, not baptize them as God's will

**Source rebuttal:**
"This will always be so" — Brigham explicitly claimed this was eternal, unchanging law, not temporary cultural accommodation. If God's eternal law changes with culture, it's not God's law.

---

### Defense #3: "This was never enforced / nobody was actually killed"

**Why it fails:**
1. **Irrelevant to truth claim:** Unenforced doesn't mean untaught or unclaimed as divine law
2. **Still caused harm:** Doctrine affected real people, prevented relationships, caused guilt/shame
3. **Why not enforced?:** If it's "law of God," why didn't prophet enforce it? Either he didn't believe it or lacked power
4. **Blood atonement parallel:** Blood atonement also rarely enforced but still problematic doctrine
5. **Changed behavior:** People made life decisions based on this teaching
6. **The claim itself is the problem:** Teaching death penalty for interracial marriage is morally wrong regardless of enforcement

**Source rebuttal:**
The fact that "the law of God" was not followed demonstrates either (a) it wasn't actually God's law, or (b) the Saints were in rebellion against God's law. Either way, the teaching is false. And the teaching itself — declaring God wants interracial couples killed — is morally indefensible.

---

### Defense #4: "The Church has since disavowed this teaching"

**Why it fails:**
1. **Admission of error:** Disavowal proves prophets taught false doctrine as "law of God"
2. **Took 150 years:** Why did it take until 2013 to disavow? How many people were harmed?
3. **Called "theories":** What Brigham called "law of God," church now calls "theories" — minimizing language
4. **No apology:** Essay disavows but doesn't apologize to those harmed
5. **Revelation question:** If this was revelation (as Brigham implied), how does revelation get overturned?
6. **Trust problem:** If church was this wrong then, how do we know current teachings won't be disavowed?

**Source rebuttal:**
The 2013 disavowal actually strengthens the critique. It's an official admission that prophets taught racist doctrine as divine law for over a century. It proves prophets can be catastrophically wrong about moral issues while claiming divine authority. Every defense of prophetic authority is weakened by this admission.

---

### Defense #5: "We don't believe in prophetic infallibility"

**Why it fails:**
1. **Moving goalposts:** After 150 years of teaching this, "we never claimed infallibility" rings hollow
2. **"Law of God" claim:** This wasn't presented as fallible opinion but as divine law
3. **Scope matters:** Being wrong about minor details differs from being wrong about who deserves death penalty
4. **Moral vs. factual error:** This isn't a factual mistake but a moral catastrophe
5. **Trust implications:** If prophets can be this wrong about God's will on race, what else?
6. **Sustained as prophets:** Members sustain prophets as "prophets, seers, and revelators" — what does that mean if they teach this?

**Source rebuttal:**
"Shall I tell you the law of God?" and "This will always be so" are not the words of a fallible man offering opinion. These are definitive declarations of divine will. If this can be wrong, the entire basis for following prophetic counsel collapses.

---

### Defense #6: "Focusing on past mistakes ignores current good"

**Why it fails:**
1. **False dichotomy:** Can acknowledge both past wrongs and current efforts
2. **Accountability matters:** Historical wrongs deserve examination regardless of present
3. **Pattern recognition:** Past errors help identify potential present errors
4. **Affected real people:** Those harmed by this doctrine deserve recognition
5. **Institutional honesty:** Confronting past is necessary for institutional integrity
6. **Not "mistakes":** These were authoritative teachings, not accidental errors

**Source rebuttal:**
"The remedy can never be applied, unless the disease is known." — Mormon scripture itself (D&C 19:7-12) emphasizes knowing the truth. Examining what prophets taught as "the law of God" is essential for understanding prophetic reliability. This isn't about dwelling on the past; it's about learning from documented prophetic failures.

---

### Defense #7: "Out of context / quote mining"

**Why it fails:**
1. **Context makes it worse:** Full sermon shows Brigham's racial views in broader framework
2. **Not isolated:** Surrounded by other racial teachings and Civil War blame on abolitionists
3. **Not softened:** No qualifiers, no "some have said," presented as definitive divine law
4. **Reinforced elsewhere:** Consistent with Brigham's racial teachings in other sermons
5. **Complete quote provided:** "Shall I tell you the law of God in regard to the African race? If the white man who belongs to the chosen seed mixes his blood with the seed of Cain, the penalty, under the law of God, is death on the spot. This will always be so." — Complete, in context, devastating

He claims neutrality on slavery while declaring death penalty for interracial marriage. The surrounding content makes it worse.
