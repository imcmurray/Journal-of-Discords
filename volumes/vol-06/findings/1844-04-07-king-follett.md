## Sermon Overview

### Main Topic/Purpose
This sermon, ostensibly delivered as a funeral address for King Follett (who died in a well accident), became Joseph Smith's most comprehensive public teaching on the nature of God and human potential for godhood. Delivered just two months before Joseph's assassination, it represents his most mature theological exposition.

### Key Themes
- God was once a mortal man who progressed to godhood
- Humans can become gods through the same progression
- The eternal nature of intelligence/spirit (co-eternal with God)
- Multiple Gods in a grand council
- Salvation for the dead through proxy ordinances
- The unpardonable sin against the Holy Ghost
- Critique of traditional Christianity's understanding of creation and God's nature

### Audience
General conference gathering of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, including many who had experienced the loss of loved ones. The sermon addressed both theological questions and provided comfort to mourners.

### Historical Context
April 1844 was a critical moment in LDS history. Joseph Smith was:
- Running for U.S. President
- Facing intense opposition in Nauvoo and surrounding areas
- Practicing polygamy secretly (though publicly denying it)
- Just two months away from his death at Carthage Jail (June 27, 1844)

This sermon represents Joseph's most radical public departure from traditional Christian theology. The sermon was reportedly delivered to between 8,000-20,000 people and was recorded by four scribes: Willard Richards, Wilford Woodruff, Thomas Bullock, and William Clayton.

## Key Quotations

### Quote 1: God Was Once a Man

> "God himself was once as we are now, and is an exalted Man, and sits enthroned in yonder heavens. That is the great secret. If the vail was rent to-day, and the great God who holds this world in its orbit, and who upholds all worlds and all things by his power, was to make himself visible—I say, if you were to see him to-day, you would see him like a man in form—like yourselves, in all the person, image, and very form as a man; for Adam was created in the very fashion, image, and likeness of God, and received instruction from, and walked, talked, and conversed with him, as one man talks and communes with another."
> — JoD 6:3

**Context:** This is Joseph's central revelation about the nature of God. He presents this as "the great secret" that traditional Christianity has lost. He's building toward his main point by first establishing God's corporeal, anthropomorphic nature.

**Significance:** This fundamentally redefines the nature of deity in ways that separate Mormonism from all traditional Christianity. It makes God a being who achieved divinity rather than an eternal, uncreated Creator.

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN / DISAVOWED (de-emphasized)

---

### Quote 2: How God Came to Be God

> "In order to understand the subject of the dead, for the consolation of those who mourn for the loss of their friends, it is necessary that we should understand the character and being of God, and how he came to be so; for I am going to tell you how God came to be God. We have imagined and supposed that God was God from all eternity, I will refute that idea, and will take away and do away the vail, so that you may see."
> — JoD 6:3

**Context:** Joseph explicitly promises to explain God's origin—a concept that contradicts the doctrine of an eternal, self-existent God central to Christianity, Judaism, and Islam.

**Significance:** This statement sets up the most controversial aspect of Mormon theology: that God himself underwent a process of development and exaltation.

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN / DISAVOWED

---

### Quote 3: God the Father Died and Rose

> "It is the first principle of the Gospel to know for a certainty the character of God and to know that we may converse with him as one man converses with another, and that he was once a man like us; yea, that God himself the Father of us all, dwelt on an earth, the same as Jesus Christ himself did; and I will show it from the Bible. I wish I was in a suitable place to tell it, and that I had the trump of an archangel, so that I could tell the story in such a manner that persecution would cease for ever. What did Jesus say? (Mark it, Elder Rigdon.) The Scriptures inform us that Jesus said, 'As the Father hath power in himself, even so hath the Son power'—to do what? Why, what the Father did. The answer is obvious—in a manner, to lay down his body and take it up again. Jesus, what are you going to do? To lay down my life, as my Father did, and take it up again."
> — JoD 6:3-4

**Context:** Joseph argues that just as Jesus died and was resurrected, so too did God the Father undergo mortality and resurrection on another world. This is his biblical proof-text.

**Significance:** This is the most explicit statement of the doctrine that God the Father was once mortal, died, and was resurrected—a teaching that modern LDS leadership has distanced itself from.

**Category:** DISAVOWED / CONTRA-MODERN

---

### Quote 4: You Have Got to Learn to Be Gods Yourselves

> "Here, then, is eternal life—to know the only wise and true God; and you have got to learn how to be Gods yourselves, and to be kings and priests to God, the same as all Gods have done before you—namely, by going from one small degree to another, and from a small capacity to a great one—from grace to grace, from exaltation to exaltation, until you attain to the resurrection of the dead and are able to dwell in everlasting burnings and to sit in glory, as do those who sit enthroned in everlasting power."
> — JoD 6:4

**Context:** This is the practical application of the doctrine—humans can and should aspire to godhood through progression.

**Significance:** This codifies the doctrine of human apotheosis and eternal progression. Note the phrase "all Gods have done before you" (plural), suggesting multiple Gods who have undergone this process.

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN (still taught but de-emphasized)

---

### Quote 5: God's Exaltation Through His Children

> "What did Jesus do? Why, I do the things I saw my Father do when worlds come rolling into existence. My Father worked out his kingdom with fear and trembling, and I must do the same; and when I get my kingdom, I shall present it to my Father, so that he may obtain kingdom upon kingdom, and it will exalt him in glory. He will then take a higher exaltation, and I will take his place, and thereby become exalted myself. So that Jesus treads in the track of his Father, and inherits what God did before; and God is thus glorified and exalted in the salvation and exaltation of all his children."
> — JoD 6:4

**Context:** Joseph is explaining the mechanism of divine progression—Gods continue to advance through the exaltation of their spirit children.

**Significance:** This presents an ever-progressing, non-omnipotent conception of deity that contradicts traditional theism. God's glory increases as his children become gods themselves.

**Category:** DISAVOWED / CONTRA-MODERN

---

### Quote 6: The Head God and the Council of Gods

> "I will go back to the beginning, before the world was, to show what kind of a being God is. What sort of a being was God in the beginning? Open your ears and hear, all ye ends of the earth; for I am going to prove it to you by the Bible, and to tell you the designs of God in relation to the human race, and why he interferes with the affairs of man... In the beginning, the head of the Gods called a council of the Gods; and they came together and concocted a plan to create the world and people it."
> — JoD 6:3, 5

**Context:** Joseph reinterprets Genesis using his understanding of Hebrew to argue for polytheism (or at minimum, henotheism—one head God among many Gods).

**Significance:** This explicitly teaches plurality of Gods, moving Mormonism into polytheism despite later apologetic attempts to maintain monotheism.

**Category:** DISAVOWED / CONTRA-MODERN

---

### Quote 7: God Did Not Create Ex Nihilo

> "You ask the learned doctors why they say the world was made out of nothing; and they will answer, 'Don't the Bible say he created the world?' And they infer, from the word create, that it must have been made out of nothing. Now, the word create came from the word baurau, which does not mean to create out of nothing; it means to organize—the same as a man would organize materials and build a ship. Hence we infer that God had materials to organize the world out of chaos—chaotic matter, which is element, and in which dwells all the glory. Element had an existence from the time He had. The pure principles of element are principles which can never be destroyed: they may be organized and reorganized, but not destroyed. They had no beginning, and can have no end."
> — JoD 6:5

**Context:** Joseph refutes creation ex nihilo (from nothing), arguing instead for organization of pre-existing eternal matter.

**Significance:** This limits God's power—he works with eternal materials rather than creating from nothing. This reduces God from an omnipotent Creator to a cosmic organizer.

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN (still taught in LDS theology)

---

### Quote 8: The Intelligence of Man is Co-Eternal with God

> "The mind or the intelligence which man possesses is coequal with God himself. I know that my testimony is true; hence, when I talk to these mourners, what have they lost? Their relatives and friends are only separated from their bodies for a short season: their spirits which existed with God have left the tabernacle of clay only for a little moment, as it were; and they now exist in a place where they converse together the same as we do on the earth."
> — JoD 6:5-6

**Context:** Joseph consoles mourners by explaining that human intelligence/spirit is eternal and was never created.

**Significance:** This denies God's role as Creator of human souls and makes human intelligence co-eternal with God—another limitation on divine power.

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN (still taught but nuanced)

---

### Quote 9: God Could Not Create Human Intelligence

> "I want to reason more on the spirit of man; for I am dwelling on the body and spirit of man—on the subject of the dead. I take my ring from my finger and liken it unto the mind of man—the immortal part, because it has no beginning. Suppose you cut it in two, then it has a beginning and an end; but join it again, and it continues one eternal round. So with the spirit of man. As the Lord liveth, if it had a beginning it will have an end. All the fools and learned and wise men from the beginning of creation, who say that the spirit of man had a beginning, prove that it must have an end; and if that doctrine is true, then the doctrine of annihilation would be true. But if I am right, I might with boldness proclaim from the housetops that God never had the power to create the spirit of man at all. God himself could not create himself."
> — JoD 6:6

**Context:** Joseph uses logical argumentation to establish that human intelligence is uncreated and eternal.

**Significance:** The phrase "God never had the power to create the spirit of man at all" is a striking limitation on divine omnipotence. This makes God a organizer and instructor of eternally existing intelligences rather than their Creator.

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN

---

### Quote 10: God Institutes Laws for Other Intelligences to Progress

> "Intelligence is eternal and exists upon a self-existent principle. It is a spirit from age to age, and there is no creation about it. All the minds and spirits that God ever sent into the world are susceptible of enlargement. The first principles of man are self-existent with God. God himself, finding he was in the midst of spirits and glory, because he was more intelligent, saw proper to institute laws whereby the rest could have a privilege to advance like himself. The relationship we have with God places us in a situation to advance in knowledge. He has power to institute laws to instruct the weaker intelligences, that they may be exalted with himself, so that they might have one glory upon another, and all that knowledge, power, glory, and intelligence which is requisite in order to save them in the world of spirits."
> — JoD 6:6

**Context:** This explains how eternal intelligences progress—God, being more advanced, establishes laws for others to follow his path.

**Significance:** This presents God not as an omnipotent Creator but as a more advanced intelligence who provides laws and instruction. The phrase "finding he was in the midst of spirits" suggests God did not create these spirits but discovered them.

**Category:** DISAVOWED / CONTRA-MODERN

---

### Quote 11: Joseph's Authority and the German Bible

> "I have an old edition of the New Testament in the Hebrew, Latin, German, and Greek languages. I have been reading the German, and find it to be the most correct translation, and to correspond nearest to the revelations which God has given to me for the last fourteen years... The doctors (I mean doctors of law, not of physic,) say, 'If you preach anything not according to the Bible, we will cry treason.' How can we escape the damnation of hell, except God be with us and reveal to us? Men bind us with chains. The Latin says Jachabod, which means Jacob; the Hebrew says Jacob, the Greek says Jacob, and the German says Jacob... I should not have introduced this testimony, were it not to back up the word Rosh—the head, the father of the Gods."
> — JoD 6:4-5

**Context:** Joseph defends his reinterpretation of Genesis by appealing to his polyglot Bible and linguistic knowledge.

**Significance:** This is Joseph asserting his authority to reinterpret scripture based on direct revelation and linguistic study. The phrase "father of the Gods" (plural) reinforces his polytheistic interpretation.

**Category:** AUTHORITY CLAIMS / CONTRA-MODERN

---

### Quote 12: All Sins Forgiven Except Sin Against Holy Ghost

> "I have a declaration to make as to the provisions which God hath made to suit the conditions of man, made from before the foundation of the world. What has Jesus said? All sins, and all blasphemies, and every transgression, except one, that man can be guilty of, may be forgiven; and there is a salvation for all men, either in this world or the world to come, who have not committed the unpardonable sin, there being a provision, either in this world or the world of spirits."
> — JoD 6:7

**Context:** Joseph explains the doctrine of universal salvation except for sons of perdition.

**Significance:** This establishes the framework for salvation for the dead and post-mortal progression, but also creates the category of the "unpardonable sin" which becomes important in Mormon theology and practice.

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN (still taught but nuanced)

---

### Quote 13: The Unpardonable Sin Defined

> "What must a man do to commit the unpardonable sin? He must receive the Holy Ghost, have the heavens opened unto him, and know God, and then sin against him. After a man has sinned against the Holy Ghost, there is no repentance for him. He has got to say that the sun does not shine while he sees it; he has got to deny Jesus Christ when the heavens have been opened unto him, and to deny the plan of salvation with his eyes open to the truth of it; and from that time he begins to be an enemy. This is the case with many apostates of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints."
> — JoD 6:8

**Context:** Joseph defines the unpardonable sin in the context of apostasy from Mormonism.

**Significance:** This creates a framework where apostates who have had spiritual experiences become "sons of perdition" with no hope of salvation. This doctrine has been used to pressure members and demonize ex-Mormons.

**Category:** MORAL-PROBLEM / CONTRA-MODERN

---

### Quote 14: Apostates Seek Blood

> "When a man begins to be an enemy to this work, he hunts me; he seeks to kill me, and never ceases to thirst for my blood. He gets the spirit of the Devil—the same spirit that they had who crucified the Lord of Life—the same spirit that sins against the, Holy Ghost. You cannot save such persons; you cannot bring them to repentance: they make open war like the Devil, and awful is the consequence."
> — JoD 6:8

**Context:** Joseph describes apostates as bloodthirsty enemies who cannot be saved.

**Significance:** This demonization of apostates creates an us-vs-them mentality and has been used to justify extreme measures against those who leave the church. Given that Joseph would be killed by a mob including former members just two months later, this has ironic resonance.

**Category:** MORAL-PROBLEM / SELF-CONTRA (given Joseph's own use of violence)

---

### Quote 15: Children Remain Children in Eternity

> "A question may be asked—'Will mothers have their children in eternity?' Yes! yes! Mothers, you shall have your children; for they shall have eternal life; for their debt is paid. There is no damnation awaits them, for they are in the spirit. But as the child dies, so shall it rise from the dead, and be for ever living in the learning of God. It will never grow: it will still be the child, in the same precise form as it appeared before it died out of its mother's arms, but possessing all the intelligence of a God. Children dwell in the mansions of glory and exercise power, but appear in the same form as when on earth. Eternity is full of thrones, upon which dwell thousands of children reigning on thrones of glory, with not one cubit added to their stature."
> — JoD 6:9

**Context:** Joseph offers comfort to grieving parents.

**Significance:** This creates a bizarre eternal cosmology where children who die young remain children forever in form while possessing divine intelligence. This doctrine is not emphasized in modern Mormonism and raises troubling questions.

**Category:** BIZARRE / CONTRA-MODERN (not emphasized)

## Analysis

**CONTRA-MODERN** — The teachings in this sermon represent the most comprehensive exposition of doctrines that the modern LDS Church has systematically de-emphasized, soft-pedaled, or distanced itself from without formally repudiating them.

### Self-Contradiction Check

**Internal to this sermon?**
The sermon is internally coherent but contains logical tensions:
- Joseph claims to teach only from the Bible while simultaneously arguing that all Christian doctors are wrong about the Bible
- He says "God himself could not create himself" but also teaches that God progressed to godhood (raising the question of infinite regress)
- He claims to want peace while demonizing apostates as bloodthirsty

**Across speaker's other sermons?**
- Earlier in his ministry, Joseph taught more traditional Christian theology about God
- The polytheism here contrasts with Book of Mormon monotheism
- The doctrine of eternal progression here is more explicit than in earlier revelations

**Across institution?**
- Book of Mormon teaches traditional monotheism (Alma 11:26-29, "there is but one God")
- Modern LDS manuals (Gospel Principles, Preach My Gospel) avoid explicit teaching that God was once mortal
- President Gordon B. Hinckley (1997) told a reporter he didn't know much about the teaching that "as man is, God once was"
- The Encyclopedia of Mormonism (official) distances the church from explicit claims about God's mortality

### Irony Potential

**"I am learned, and know more than all the world put together"** (JoD 6:5)
- Spoken by someone with minimal formal education who made numerous provably false claims about Hebrew, Egyptian, and other languages

**"Meddle not with any man for his religion"** (JoD 6:3)
- Spoken by the founder of a religion that sent missionaries worldwide to convert others and that established theocratic rule in Nauvoo

**"No man is authorized to take away life in consequence of difference of religion"** (JoD 6:3)
- Spoken by someone who would, within months, participate in the destruction of a printing press and die in a shootout
- Ironic given later Mormon practice of blood atonement

**"When a man begins to be an enemy to this work, he hunts me; he seeks to kill me"** (JoD 6:8)
- Two months later, Joseph would be killed by a mob that included former members
- Creates self-fulfilling prophecy and persecution narrative

**"I never did harm any man since I was born into the world"** (JoD 6:10)
- Spoken by someone who ordered the destruction of the Nauvoo Expositor, participated in violence, and commanded a militia

### Modern Church Position

The modern LDS Church has adopted a complex position on the King Follett Discourse:

**What They Still Teach:**
- Humans can become like God (deification/exaltation)
- God has a body of flesh and bone
- Eternal progression is possible
- Proxy work for the dead

**What They De-Emphasize or Avoid:**
- God was once a mortal man on another earth
- God the Father died and was resurrected
- Multiple Gods in council
- God's ongoing progression
- The specific mechanics of apotheosis

**Key Modern Statements:**

**Gordon B. Hinckley (1997)** in an interview with Time Magazine, when asked about "As man is, God once was; as God is, man may become":
> "I don't know that we teach it. I don't know that we emphasize it... I understand the philosophical background behind it, but I don't know a lot about it, and I don't think others know a lot about it."

**Official Statement (2014)** on LDS.org:
> "Do Latter-day Saints believe that they will 'get their own planet'? No. This idea is not taught in Latter-day Saint scripture, nor is it a doctrine of the Church. This misrepresentation of latter-day Saint belief is often perpetuated by those who caricature the faith."

**Gospel Principles Manual (2009)** — Does not mention God's mortality or that he dwelt on another earth

**Encyclopedia of Mormonism (1992)** entry on "God the Father":
> "Joseph Smith's King Follett discourse is not 'official' doctrine."

This represents a systematic soft-disavowal without formal repudiation—the church allows the sermon to remain in publication but distances itself from its most radical claims.

## Apologetic Anticipation

### Defense #1: "This is taken out of context—Joseph was speaking metaphorically"

**Why it fails:**
- Joseph explicitly says he's revealing "the great secret" and telling people "how God came to be God"
- He stakes his prophetic credibility on this teaching: "if I fail to do it, it becomes my duty to renounce all further pretensions to revelations, inspirations, or to be a Prophet" (JoD 6:3)
- The language is unambiguously literal: "God himself the Father of us all, dwelt on an earth, the same as Jesus Christ himself did"
- Four scribes recorded it, suggesting official importance
- Later prophets (Brigham Young, Lorenzo Snow) taught the same doctrine explicitly

**Source rebuttal:**
- Lorenzo Snow formalized this teaching in his famous couplet: "As man now is, God once was; as God now is, man may be"
- Brigham Young taught this doctrine repeatedly (see JoD 7:282-291)
- It was published in official church periodicals and never retracted during the 19th century

### Defense #2: "This was just speculation, not official doctrine"

**Why it fails:**
- Joseph presented this as revealed knowledge from God, not speculation
- He claimed to have superior knowledge through the Holy Ghost: "The Holy Ghost does, anyhow; and he is within me, and comprehends more than all the world" (JoD 6:5)
- He made it a test of his prophetic calling
- It was delivered at General Conference, the church's highest official gathering
- It was published in the official Journal of Discourses
- It shaped LDS theology for over a century

**Source rebuttal:**
- The teaching was codified in church hymns (e.g., "If You Could Hie to Kolob")
- It was taught in official church publications throughout the 19th and early 20th centuries
- Only in recent decades has the church distanced itself from it
- The LDS concept of eternal progression and exaltation directly depends on this theology

### Defense #3: "Modern revelation has clarified or superseded this teaching"

**Why it fails:**
- No official revelation has retracted or modified the King Follett Discourse
- The church has not formally repudiated the sermon
- It remains published in official church literature
- The church still teaches exaltation/deification, just with less specificity about God's origin
- If this was superseded by modern revelation, why not say so explicitly?

**Source rebuttal:**
- Doctrine and Covenants 130:22 (canonized scripture) affirms "The Father has a body of flesh and bones"
- D&C 132 (canonized) teaches eternal progression and becoming gods
- No section of D&C or official declaration contradicts the King Follett Discourse
- The church's avoidance is strategic silence, not doctrinal correction

### Defense #4: "You're misunderstanding what 'God' means in Mormon theology"

**Why it fails:**
- Joseph is explicitly talking about "God himself the Father of us all" (JoD 6:3)
- He's not talking about a general category of gods, but specifically about the being Mormons worship as their Heavenly Father
- The teaching is that THIS God, the one they pray to, was once mortal
- Attempting to redefine terms doesn't change the plain meaning

**Source rebuttal:**
- Joseph says "God himself was once as we are now" and "dwelt on an earth, the same as Jesus Christ himself did"
- He's clearly talking about God the Father, whom he distinguishes from Jesus Christ
- The teaching is about the specific deity Mormons worship, not an abstract philosophical category

### Defense #5: "Critics are trying to make Mormonism look weird—this is just Christian deification doctrine"

**Why it fails:**
- While Eastern Orthodox Christianity teaches theosis (deification), it does NOT teach that God the Father was once mortal
- Eastern Orthodox theosis means participation in divine nature, not becoming a separate god
- No traditional Christian theology teaches multiple Gods in council
- No traditional Christian theology teaches God progressed from mortality
- The King Follett Discourse goes far beyond any Christian teaching on deification

**Source rebuttal:**
- The Council of Nicaea (325 AD) defined God as eternal and uncreated
- Every major Christian creed (Nicene, Apostles', Athanasian) affirms God's eternal existence
- Christian theosis is about union with God, not replacing God or becoming a separate deity
- Joseph explicitly contradicts Christian creeds, calling their teachers "fools" (JoD 6:6)

### Defense #6: "This was never official doctrine, just Joseph's opinion"

**Why it fails:**
- Delivered at General Conference with official scribes recording
- Published in Journal of Discourses, the official sermon collection
- Joseph explicitly stakes his prophetic calling on this teaching
- He claims direct revelation: "God has told me so" (JoD 6:5)
- If this wasn't official doctrine, what was? Everything Joseph said could be dismissed as "just his opinion"

**Source rebuttal:**
- The teaching shaped LDS theology for over a century
- Temple ceremonies incorporate the concept of progression to godhood
- Church presidents after Joseph (Brigham Young, Lorenzo Snow, etc.) taught this as doctrine
- The church never officially repudiated it, only quietly distanced itself

### Defense #7: "President Hinckley said we don't teach this anymore, so it's been corrected"

**Why it fails:**
- Hinckley's 1997 statement ("I don't know that we teach it... I don't know a lot about it") is evasive, not a repudiation
- Saying "we don't emphasize it" is not the same as saying "it was false"
- No official declaration or revelation has corrected the doctrine
- The church still publishes the King Follett Discourse
- If Joseph was wrong about this core teaching, what else was he wrong about?

**Source rebuttal:**
- The doctrine remains in canonized scripture (D&C 130:22, 132)
- Temple ceremonies still teach progression to godhood
- The church has not removed the King Follett Discourse from its archives
- Strategic silence about an embarrassing teaching is not the same as doctrinal correction
- If the church now rejects this teaching, it should formally say so—but that would undermine Joseph's prophetic authority
