## Sermon Overview

### Main Topic/Purpose
Instructions to departing missionaries on maintaining spiritual light, living their religion, and avoiding speculation/materialism while on missions. Brigham emphasizes that elders should focus on bringing home converts ("souls of men") rather than material goods, and should use all surplus funds to emigrate the poor.

### Key Themes
- Holy Ghost as constant companion
- Joseph Smith as test of true belief
- Missionaries must avoid materialism/speculation
- Gathering the poor vs. accumulating wealth
- Saints' obligations to God and each other
- Future glory of the kingdom vs. current trials
- **BIZARRE: Temple endowments given to CREATE devils**

### Audience
Mixed congregation including:
- Newly arrived converts
- Long-time residents
- Departing missionaries (primary audience)
- General membership

### Historical Context
September 1860 was a pivotal time for the Utah territory:
- **Buchanan's Utah War (1857-58)** had just ended two years prior
- Federal tensions remained high over polygamy and theocracy
- Missionaries were being sent out during period of external pressure
- Temple endowments had been given in Nauvoo (1840s) and were being given in the Endowment House in Salt Lake (opened 1855)
- The doctrine of "sons of perdition" was well-established but mysterious
- This sermon reveals Brigham's cosmic theology of necessary opposition

The sermon starts instructional and practical, but toward the end (pages 181-182) Brigham ventures into speculative theology about the future kingdom, plural wives "by the million," and the cosmic necessity of devils.

## Key Quotations

### Quote 1: Endowments Given to Create Devils (PRIMARY FINDING)

> "Do you query why we give endowments to A., B., and C? It is to make devils of those who will deny the faith, for that is also necessary, as a host of devils will be needed."
> — JoD 8:181-182

**Context:** Immediately follows Brigham's statement about apostate "Latter-day Saints" becoming "angels of the Devil" and being consigned to the "deep (did I say bottomless?) pit." He's explaining the cosmic necessity of having devils, and reveals that endowments serve a dual purpose: making saints OR making devils.

**Significance:**
- Temple endowments have a **deliberate dark purpose**: creating devils
- God's plan requires "a host of devils"
- Endowments are given to people knowing some will apostatize and become devils
- Implies predestination or foreknowledge: endowments given to future apostates
- Suggests sacred ordinances serve opposition/testing purposes
- Completely contradicts modern teaching that endowments are only for exaltation
- Raises disturbing questions about divine intent and manipulation

**Category:** BIZARRE (primary), DISAVOWED (secondary)

---

### Quote 2: Apostate Saints Become Angels of the Devil

> "A person, to become an angel of the Devil, has first to be a good Saint, and then deny the Lord who bought him."
> — JoD 8:181

**Context:** Brigham is discussing what will happen to enemies and apostates in the future kingdom. He says enemies will become "soap-boilers and kitchen flunkeys," but apostate saints will go to hell to become devils.

**Significance:**
- Only faithful saints who apostatize can become "angels of the Devil"
- Never-members can't reach this level of damnation
- Temple endowments create the possibility for greater damnation
- The "deep (did I say bottomless?) pit" reference shows uncertainty about hell's nature
- Sets up the next statement about needing devils

**Category:** BIZARRE, DISAVOWED (related to sons of perdition doctrine, now de-emphasized)

---

### Quote 3: Enemies Will Be Servants, Apostates Will Be Devils

> "What will you do with all those who have sought to kill you? Make them soap-boilers and kitchen flunkeys. We are not going to send them into hell fire, for it takes a good Latter-day Saint apostatized to get down into that deep (did I say bottomless?) pit."
> — JoD 8:181

**Context:** Discussing the future millennial kingdom when saints will reign. Brigham contrasts the fate of external enemies (servitude) with internal apostates (damnation).

**Significance:**
- External persecutors will be servants in the kingdom
- Only apostate saints reach the deepest hell
- The parenthetical "(did I say bottomless?)" shows Brigham questioning his own cosmology
- Reveals hierarchy of damnation: never-believers < enemies < apostate saints
- Echoes D&C 76 (vision of degrees of glory) but with different emphasis

**Category:** BIZARRE, CONTRA-MODERN (on severity and certainty about apostates)

---

### Quote 4: Brigham Will Have Wives and Children by the Million

> "Brother Cannon remarked that people wondered how many wives and children I had. He may inform them that I shall have wives and children by the million, and glory, and riches, and power, and dominion, and kingdom after kingdom, and reign triumphantly."
> — JoD 8:181

**Context:** Responding to George Q. Cannon's comment about public curiosity regarding Brigham's plural families. Brigham expands to describe his future exalted state.

**Significance:**
- Celestial polygamy on cosmic scale ("by the million")
- Eternal increase doctrine (having spirit children eternally)
- "Kingdom after kingdom" = becoming a god over multiple worlds
- Extremely bold claim about personal deification
- Context for the "devils needed" statement (large kingdoms need opposition)

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN (eternal polygamy de-emphasized), BIZARRE (scale of claims)

---

### Quote 5: The Charity of God's Kingdoms

> "My charity is that God has provided for his children, the sons and daughters of Adam and Eve, where all who have lived according to the best light they had will have better kingdoms than ever entered into their hearts to conceive."
> — JoD 8:182

**Context:** Responding to Heber C. Kimball's comment about charity. Brigham defines "charity" as God's provision of appropriate kingdoms for all people based on the light they received.

**Significance:**
- Universal salvation into "better kingdoms than ever entered into their hearts"
- Everyone gets something good, even non-Mormons
- But this contradicts the previous statement about needing devils
- If all get good kingdoms, who populates hell?
- Tension between universal mercy and cosmic necessity of devils

**Category:** SELF-CONTRA (with "devils needed" statement), BIZARRE

---

### Quote 6: Joseph Smith as Test of True Belief

> "Whosoever confesseth that Joseph Smith was sent of God to reveal the holy Gospel to the children of men, and lay the foundation for gathering Israel, and building up the kingdom of God on the earth, that spirit is of God; and every spirit that does not confess that God has sent Joseph Smith, and revealed the everlasting Gospel to and through him, is of Antichrist, no matter whether it is found in a pulpit or on a throne."
> — JoD 8:177

**Context:** Early in the sermon, Brigham is defining true belief. He adapts 1 John 4:2 (about confessing Jesus) to make it about Joseph Smith instead.

**Significance:**
- Replaces Jesus with Joseph as the test of divine vs. antichrist spirits
- Absolute requirement: no salvation without accepting Joseph
- Applies to "pulpit or throne" (religious and political leaders)
- Shows Joseph's central role in LDS soteriology
- Modern church emphasizes Christ more, Joseph less in public teaching

**Category:** CONTRA-MODERN (emphasis), AUTHORITY-CLAIMS

---

### Quote 7: Missionaries Must Bring Home Souls, Not Goods

> "I utterly forbid this practice. I forbid your bringing or sending home silk dresses and the like. Send and bring home the poor. I forbid your coming home in your carriages; I forbid your going out preachers and coming home merchants, but come home bringing your sheaves with you the souls of the children of men—spirits clothed in tabernacles."
> — JoD 8:179

**Context:** Major theme of the sermon. Brigham is cracking down on missionaries who used missions to enrich themselves, speculate, and bring home luxury goods instead of converts.

**Significance:**
- Strong prophetic command ("I utterly forbid")
- Missionaries had been profiteering from missions
- "Book Debt" system at Liverpool Office was draining emigration funds
- Brigham's priority: poor saints over material goods
- Reveals corruption/materialism among returning missionaries
- Shows practical ecclesiastical concerns alongside bizarre theology

**Category:** HISTORICAL-CONTEXT (not problematic, shows leadership)

## Analysis

### Primary Category
**BIZARRE** — The statement about giving endowments "to make devils of those who will deny the faith, for that is also necessary, as a host of devils will be needed" is one of the strangest theological claims in the entire Journal of Discourses.

Key bizarre elements:
1. **Dual-purpose ordinances:** Temple endowments serve BOTH to exalt saints AND to create devils
2. **Cosmic necessity:** God's plan requires "a host of devils" to function
3. **Predestination implications:** If endowments are given to people who "will deny the faith," this suggests foreknowledge or predestination
4. **Instrumental use of sacred rites:** The most sacred ordinances are tools for cosmic opposition, not just salvation
5. **No modern equivalent:** The modern church NEVER teaches that temple ordinances serve this purpose

### Secondary Categories

**DISAVOWED** — Modern church teaching:
- Temple endowments are for making covenants that lead to exaltation
- Never mentions creating devils as a purpose
- De-emphasizes sons of perdition doctrine
- Focuses on positive aspects of temple worship
- Would be horrified by this characterization

**SELF-CONTRADICTORY** — Within this sermon:
- Brigham says "a host of devils will be needed" (opposition required)
- But also says "all who lived according to best light they had will have better kingdoms than ever entered their hearts to conceive" (universal positive outcome)
- These cannot both be true: if everyone gets good kingdoms, who are the devils?
- Either not everyone gets good kingdoms, OR devils come from another source, OR Brigham is contradicting himself

**CONTRA-MODERN** — Theological differences:
- Modern church: Temple ordinances are sacred tools for exaltation
- Brigham: Temple ordinances also create devils (necessary opposition)
- Modern church: Sons of perdition rarely discussed, extremely rare
- Brigham: "A host of devils will be needed" (many apostates expected)
- Modern church: Emphasizes Christ over Joseph Smith publicly
- Brigham: Joseph Smith is THE test of true vs. antichrist spirits

### Self-Contradiction Check

**Internal to this sermon?**
Yes, significant contradiction:

1. **On ultimate fate of people:**
   - "A host of devils will be needed" (many will be damned)
   - "All who have lived according to the best light they had will have better kingdoms than ever entered into their hearts to conceive" (everyone gets something good)

2. **On the purpose of endowments:**
   - Given "to make devils of those who will deny the faith" (instrumental, anticipating apostasy)
   - Implied earlier: given to teach all things, bring light, prepare for exaltation (positive)

**Across speaker's other sermons?**
The "devils needed" theme is rare but consistent with Brigham's opposition theology. However, the universal charity statement contradicts his harsher statements elsewhere about apostates and non-believers.

**Across institution?**
Complete contradiction with modern church:

| Brigham Young (1860) | Modern LDS Church |
|---------------------|------------------|
| Endowments create devils | Endowments prepare for exaltation |
| Devils are necessary to God's plan | Opposition exists, but not emphasized as divine creation |
| "Host of devils will be needed" | Sons of perdition extremely rare |
| Endowments given knowing recipients will apostatize | Endowments given with hope/expectation of faithfulness |
| Joseph Smith = test of true belief | Christ = test of true belief (Joseph important but secondary) |

### Irony Potential

**Self-Condemning Phrases:**
1. "To make devils of those who will deny the faith"
   - Every active member who has received endowments knows people who left the church
   - Were their endowments given "to make devils" of them?
   - Disturbing implications for temple recommend interviews

2. "That is also necessary, as a host of devils will be needed"
   - God needs devils
   - Sacred ordinances serve dark purposes
   - Plan of salvation requires mass apostasy

3. "A good Latter-day Saint apostatized"
   - Only the faithful can become devils
   - Temple endowments increase damnation potential
   - Better to never join than to join and leave?

**Standards Set Then Violated:**
- Brigham says endowments create devils
- Modern church says endowments create gods
- Both can't be primary purpose

**Hook Potential:**
- "To make devils of those who will deny the faith" — shocking, disturbing
- "A host of devils will be needed" — cosmic necessity framing
- "A good Latter-day Saint apostatized" — only the faithful can be truly damned

## Apologetic Anticipation

### Defense #1: "He Was Speaking Hyperbolically"

**What they'll say:**
"Brigham was using exaggerated, colorful language to make a point. He didn't literally mean God gives endowments to create devils. He was emphasizing that people have agency and some will unfortunately choose to reject their covenants."

**Why it fails:**
The statement is too specific and theological to be hyperbole:
- "It is to make devils of those who will deny the faith" — active purpose statement
- "For that is also necessary" — theological claim about cosmic necessity
- "As a host of devils will be needed" — functional requirement in God's plan
- Embedded in larger cosmological discussion (kingdoms, opposition, glory)
- No markers of hyperbole (no "as it were," "figuratively speaking," etc.)

**Source rebuttal:**
> "Do you query why we give endowments to A., B., and C? **It is to make devils of those who will deny the faith, for that is also necessary**, as a host of devils will be needed."

The question-answer format suggests this is doctrinal explanation, not rhetorical flourish. "Why do we do X?" "It is to accomplish Y." That's straightforward purpose statement.

---

### Defense #2: "Out of Context"

**What they'll say:**
"This is one sentence pulled out of a long sermon about missionary work. The main topic was missionaries avoiding materialism. This brief cosmological aside doesn't represent the sermon's purpose or Brigham's considered doctrine."

**Why it fails:**
1. The context actually makes it worse, not better
2. The statement comes in a section about future glory and kingdoms
3. It's connected to established doctrine (sons of perdition, outer darkness)
4. The broader passage includes other bizarre claims (wives by the million, enemies as servants)
5. Being a "brief aside" doesn't make false doctrine acceptable
6. Prophets are supposed to be careful about teaching doctrine, especially on sacred ordinances

**Source rebuttal:**
The full context shows this is part of Brigham's cosmic theology:
- Discussing future kingdoms and glory (v. 181)
- Explaining fates of different groups (enemies, apostates, saints)
- Making theological claims about necessity of opposition
- Teaching about endowments' purposes

This isn't a slip of the tongue; it's integrated into his cosmology.

---

### Defense #3: "He Meant It Creates the POSSIBILITY, Not the Intent"

**What they'll say:**
"Brigham wasn't saying God INTENDS to make devils through endowments. He was saying that endowments, by elevating people to higher knowledge and covenants, create the possibility for higher rebellion. It's like saying 'we give people freedom to let them become criminals if they choose.' The purpose is freedom, not criminality."

**Why it fails:**
1. The text says "It is TO MAKE devils" (purpose), not "It creates the possibility"
2. "That is also necessary" — suggests intentional design, not mere side effect
3. "A host of devils will be needed" — functional requirement, not just possibility
4. The parallel to freedom/criminality fails because we don't say "we give freedom TO MAKE criminals"
5. If Brigham meant "possibility," he would have said so
6. This defense admits endowments serve a negative function, which modern church denies

**Source rebuttal:**
> "It is **to make** devils of those who will deny the faith, for **that is also necessary**."

"To make" = purpose
"Also necessary" = dual function alongside making saints
This is intentional design, not accidental byproduct.

---

### Defense #4: "Opposition in All Things"

**What they'll say:**
"This fits LDS doctrine that opposition is necessary. 2 Nephi 2:11 says 'it must needs be, that there is an opposition in all things.' Brigham was teaching that God's plan requires opposition, and devils provide that opposition. This isn't bizarre; it's standard LDS theology."

**Why it fails:**
1. "Opposition in all things" doesn't require CREATING devils through sacred ordinances
2. Opposition can exist without God instrumentally making devils
3. The Book of Mormon teaches Satan and his hosts provide opposition (pre-mortal rebellion)
4. No scripture teaches that temple ordinances serve to create devils
5. This defense validates the bizarre claim instead of explaining it away
6. If accepted, this means every endowment session includes people who will become devils by design

**Source rebuttal:**
The issue isn't opposition theology; it's the MECHANISM. Brigham claims temple endowments are the tool for creating devils. That's not in 2 Nephi 2, D&C 76, or any modern teaching.

---

### Defense #5: "He Was Talking About Foreknowledge, Not Predestination"

**What they'll say:**
"God knows who will deny the faith, but He doesn't cause it. Brigham was saying that God, knowing some endowment recipients will apostatize, still gives them endowments because agency requires the possibility of apostasy. This is about divine foreknowledge, not divine causation."

**Why it fails:**
1. This defense still admits God gives ordinances knowing they'll create devils
2. "It is to make devils" is active causation, not passive foreknowledge
3. If God knows someone will become a devil, why give them higher knowledge/damnation?
4. This raises more problems than it solves (why not withhold endowments from future apostates?)
5. The text says endowments are given "TO MAKE devils," not "despite knowing some will become devils"
6. This defense contradicts free agency (if God knows outcomes, are they predetermined?)

**Source rebuttal:**
> "Do you query why we give endowments to A., B., and C? It is **to make devils** of those who **will** deny the faith."

"Will deny" suggests foreknowledge, but "to make" suggests purpose. Both together create theological nightmare: God knowingly uses sacred ordinances to create devils.

---

### Defense #6: "Brigham Was Speculating, Not Teaching Doctrine"

**What they'll say:**
"Brigham often speculated about theology. Not everything he said was official doctrine. This was likely personal speculation about the mechanics of opposition and apostasy, not a binding teaching about endowments."

**Why it fails:**
1. He's instructing departing missionaries in official capacity
2. The sermon was recorded and published in Journal of Discourses
3. No disclaimer or hedge ("I think," "perhaps," "it seems to me")
4. If prophets can't be trusted when teaching in official settings, what's the point?
5. How do members distinguish "speculation" from "doctrine"?
6. Temple endowments are too sacred to speculate about casually
7. This defense destroys prophetic authority

**Source rebuttal:**
This was an official address, recorded by scribes, published as prophetic teaching. The question-answer format ("Do you query why...?") suggests this was addressing actual questions about endowments. That's teaching doctrine, not speculating.

---

### Defense #7: "The Church Has Grown in Understanding"

**What they'll say:**
"Line upon line, precept upon precept. The early church's understanding of temple ordinances was incomplete. Modern prophets have clarified that endowments are for exaltation, not creating devils. This is continuing revelation, not contradiction."

**Why it fails:**
1. This is 1860, not 1830 — temple doctrine was mature by then
2. Nauvoo temple had been operating 1840s, Endowment House since 1855
3. "Growing in understanding" shouldn't go from "creating devils" to "never mind"
4. This isn't refinement; it's complete reversal
5. Modern church doesn't say "we used to think endowments created devils, but now we know better"
6. If fundamental purpose of most sacred ordinance was misunderstood, what else is wrong?
7. Members living in 1860s received endowments under this theology

**Source rebuttal:**
Brigham wasn't proposing new doctrine; he was explaining established practice. "Do you query why we give endowments?" implies this is the existing rationale. If the prophet who supervised temple ordinances for decades fundamentally misunderstood their purpose, the epistemological crisis is massive.

---

### Defense #8: "He Meant Apostate Leaders, Not All Apostates"

**What they'll say:**
"Brigham was talking about apostate leaders who become 'angels of the devil' by leading others astray. He wasn't saying all apostates become devils or that endowments are given to create all devils. The 'host of devils' refers to prominent apostates who had sacred knowledge."

**Why it fails:**
1. The text doesn't limit this to leaders: "A., B., and C" sounds like regular members
2. "A host of devils will be needed" suggests many, not just a few leaders
3. If only leaders become devils, it's not much of a "host"
4. The broader passage discusses general apostasy, not just leadership
5. This defense still admits endowments create SOME devils, which contradicts modern teaching
6. Where's the textual evidence for this limitation?

**Source rebuttal:**
> "Do you query why we give endowments to A., B., and C?"

A, B, and C are generic placeholders for individual people, not titles for leadership positions. This sounds like regular endowment recipients.

## Song Potential Assessment

### Rating: ★★★★ (HIGH POTENTIAL)

### Justification

This is strong material with significant song potential:

**1. The Hook is Disturbing and Memorable:**
"It is to make devils of those who will deny the faith" is shocking, specific, and deeply troubling. Combined with "a host of devils will be needed," it creates a dark cosmic vision that contradicts everything modern members hear about temples.

**2. Personal Relevance:**
- Every active member knows someone who left the church after receiving endowments
- Every temple-attending member has heard beautiful descriptions of endowments as sacred preparation for exaltation
- This quote flips the script: maybe those endowments served a darker purpose?

**3. Theological Depth:**
This isn't just a failed prophecy or outdated policy; it's a fundamentally different view of:
- Sacred ordinances
- God's plan
- Opposition and agency
- Apostasy and damnation

**4. No Clean Defense:**
Every apologetic response creates new problems:
- "Hyperbole" → Destroys prophetic authority on sacred matters
- "Foreknowledge" → Why give ordinances that increase damnation?
- "Opposition theology" → Validates the bizarre claim
- "Speculation" → Prophets can't be trusted on temple matters?

**5. Institutional Contradiction:**
Modern temple preparation materials emphasize:
- Making sacred covenants
- Receiving instruction for exaltation
- Drawing closer to God
- Preparation for eternal families

NEVER mentioned:
- Creating devils
- Cosmic necessity of apostasy
- Dual purpose of ordinances

**6. Self-Contradiction:**
Within same sermon, Brigham says:
- "Host of devils will be needed" (many damned)
- "All who lived according to best light will have better kingdoms than they conceived" (universal salvation)

This internal tension adds depth to the song.
