## Sermon Overview

### Main Topic/Purpose
A wide-ranging philosophical discourse on the nature of intelligence, human origins, foreordination, salvation, and priesthood authority. Brigham Young attempts to synthesize various Christian doctrines while establishing LDS theological positions on creation, race, and eternal progression.

### Key Themes
- Nature of intelligence and human capacity
- Foreordination and election vs. free will
- Joseph Smith's role in salvation
- Universal salvation with gradations
- Creation and origins of humanity
- Racial theology and priesthood restriction

### Audience
General membership gathered in the Tabernacle for Sunday services

### Historical Context
October 1859 - Two years before the Civil War begins. Utah Territory is under federal scrutiny due to polygamy. The priesthood ban against Black members has been in effect since Joseph Smith's death (1844), though not yet formally codified in policy statements. The LDS church is establishing theological justifications for its racial policies.

## Key Quotations

### Quote 1: Physical Features as Divine Curse

> "You see some classes of the human family that are black, uncouth, un-comely, disagreeable and low in their habits, wild, and seemingly deprived of nearly all the blessings of the intelligence that is generally bestowed upon mankind. The first man that committed the odious crime of killing one of his brethren will be cursed the longest of any one of the children of Adam. Cain slew his brother. Cain might have been killed, and that would have put a termination to that line of human beings. This was not to be, and the Lord put a mark upon him, which is the flat nose and black skin."
> — JoD 7:290-291

**Context:** Near the end of a long sermon on intelligence and human nature, Brigham Young transitions to discussing God's creation of all humanity "of one blood" before singling out Black people as cursed descendants of Cain.

**Significance:** This is one of the most explicit statements identifying specific physical features (flat nose, black skin) as divine curses placed on Cain. It directly associates Blackness with divine punishment for murder and moral inferiority. The language is profoundly dehumanizing, describing Black people as "uncouth, un-comely, disagreeable and low in their habits, wild."

**Category:** MORAL-PROBLEM (primary), DISAVOWED (secondary), FALSIFIED (tertiary)

---

### Quote 2: Priesthood Ban Until "The Last Ones"

> "Trace mankind down to after the flood, and then another curse is pronounced upon the same race—that they should be the 'servant of servants;' and they will be, until that curse is removed; and the Abolitionists cannot help it, nor in the least alter that decree. How long is that race to endure the dreadful curse that is upon them? That curse will remain upon them, and they never can hold the Priesthood or share in it until all the other descendants of Adam have received the promises and enjoyed the blessings of the Priesthood and the keys thereof. Until the last ones of the residue of Adam's children are brought up to that favourable position, the children of Cain cannot receive the first ordinances of the Priesthood. They were the first that were cursed, and they will be the last from whom the curse will be removed."
> — JoD 7:290-291

**Context:** Immediately following the "flat nose and black skin" statement, Brigham Young doubles down by adding the Noah/Ham curse narrative and making a prophetic declaration about when the priesthood ban would be lifted.

**Significance:** This is a clear prophetic statement with testable parameters. Young declares that Black people will be the "last" to receive priesthood blessings - only after "all the other descendants of Adam" receive them first. The 1978 priesthood revelation contradicts this timeline, as millions of non-Black people still have not received priesthood ordinances. Young also dismisses abolitionists as powerless against divine decree.

**Category:** FAILED-PROPH (primary), DISAVOWED (secondary), MORAL-PROBLEM (tertiary)

---

### Quote 3: Joseph Smith's Universal Gatekeeping Authority

> "Joseph Smith holds the keys of this last dispensation, and is now engaged behind the vail in the great work of the last days. I can tell our beloved brother Christians who have slain the Prophets and butchered and otherwise caused the death of thousands of Latter-day Saints, the priests who have thanked God in their prayers and thanksgiving from the pulpit that we have been plundered, driven, and slain, and the deacons under the pulpit, and their brethren and sisters in their closets, who have thanked God, thinking that the Latter-day Saints were wasted away, something that no doubt will mortify them—something that, to say the least, is a matter of deep regret to them—namely, that no man or woman in this dispensation will ever enter into the celestial kingdom of God without the consent of Joseph Smith."
> — JoD 7:289

**Context:** Within a discussion of salvation and the spirit world, Brigham Young makes an extraordinary claim about Joseph Smith's continuing authority.

**Significance:** This theological claim is extreme even by LDS standards. It makes Joseph Smith (not Jesus Christ) the ultimate gatekeeper to celestial glory. While this doctrine appeared occasionally in 19th-century LDS thought, it has been quietly abandoned in modern teaching. The vengeful tone toward critics and the elevation of Smith above Christ presents serious theological problems.

**Category:** DISAVOWED (primary), CONTRA-MODERN (secondary)

---

### Quote 4: Pre-existence and Foreordination of Pharaoh as Wicked

> "He was foreordained in eternity to preside over this last dispensation, as much so as Pharaoh was foreordained to be a wicked man, or as was Jesus to be the Saviour of the world because he was the oldest son in the family."
> — JoD 7:290

**Context:** Discussing foreordination and Joseph Smith's pre-mortal calling.

**Significance:** This creates a severe theological problem: if God foreordained Pharaoh to wickedness, then Pharaoh had no moral agency in his evil actions. This undermines the entire doctrine of free will and accountability. It makes God the author of evil and unjust in punishing Pharaoh for doing what God foreordained him to do. The casual equation of Pharaoh's wickedness with Jesus's mission as savior is theologically jarring.

**Category:** SELF-CONTRA (primary), MORAL-PROBLEM (secondary)

---

### Quote 5: Joseph Smith's Blood Predestination

> "The Lord had his eye upon him, and upon his father, and upon his father's father, and upon their progenitors clear back to Abraham, and from Abraham to the flood, from the flood to Enoch, and, from Enoch to Adam. He has watched that family and that blood as it has circulated from its fountain to the birth of that man."
> — JoD 7:290

**Context:** Explaining Joseph Smith's foreordination through bloodline.

**Significance:** This establishes a racial/bloodline theology that contradicts modern LDS claims about equality and universal opportunity. It suggests salvation is determined by genealogy and blood purity. This same logic was used to justify racial priesthood restrictions - if righteous foreordination flows through blood, then cursed status could too.

**Category:** DISAVOWED (primary), MORAL-PROBLEM (secondary)

## Analysis

### Primary Category
**MORAL-PROBLEM**

This sermon's core issue is moral: it articulates a racist theology that describes Black people in dehumanizing terms and links physical features to divine curses for sin. The ethical problems transcend mere doctrinal error.

### Secondary Categories
- **DISAVOWED** - The 2013 "Race and the Priesthood" Gospel Topics essay explicitly disavows the theories about Black people presented in this sermon
- **FAILED-PROPH** - The timing prophecy about priesthood restriction contradicts the 1978 revelation
- **FALSIFIED** - Genetics disproves the Curse of Cain narrative
- **SELF-CONTRA** - Contradicts the sermon's own statements about free will and God creating all humanity "of one blood"

### Self-Contradiction Check

**Internal to this sermon?**
Yes, profoundly. Brigham Young states: "God has created of one blood all the nations and kingdoms of men that dwell upon all the face of the earth: black, white, copper-coloured, or whatever their colour" (JoD 7:290). This directly contradicts his subsequent argument that Black people are cursed descendants of Cain who are "deprived of nearly all the blessings of the intelligence that is generally bestowed upon mankind."

He also extensively defends free will and independent volition ("Were this not so, I would at once request parents never to correct a child for another disobedient act") while simultaneously teaching that Pharaoh was "foreordained to be a wicked man."

**Across speaker's other sermons?**
Brigham Young was relatively consistent on racial doctrine across his ministry, though the specifics varied. However, this sermon's extreme statements about "flat nose" as a curse mark stands out even among his other racial teachings.

**Across institution?**
Massively contradicts modern LDS teaching:
- 1978 Official Declaration 2 (priesthood to all worthy males)
- 2013 "Race and the Priesthood" essay disavows theories about Black people
- Modern church emphasis on universal spiritual equality
- Current church teaching that physical features are not markers of divine favor/disfavor

### Irony Potential

The sermon is rich with ironic reversals:

1. **"The remedy can never be applied, unless the disease is known"** - Young's own framing from JoD 13 can be turned on the racism he preaches here

2. **One blood, many curses** - "God has created of one blood all the nations" immediately followed by descriptions of some races as "uncouth, un-comely, disagreeable"

3. **Intelligence sermon, unintelligent claims** - A sermon supposedly about the nature of intelligence contains scientifically falsified claims about heredity and race

4. **Gathering truth, spreading error** - Young states "it is our duty and calling...to gather every item of truth and reject every error" while teaching racial pseudoscience

5. **Free will for me, foreordination for thee** - Defends human agency while claiming some were foreordained to wickedness

6. **"All the nations that forget God"** - Young warns of hell for those who forget God, while teaching a doctrine that makes God the author of racist curses

## Apologetic Anticipation

### Defense #1: "He was speaking as a man, not a prophet"
**Why it fails:**
- Delivered from the Tabernacle pulpit as President of the Church
- Reported by official church scribe G.D. Watt
- Published in official church publication (Journal of Discourses)
- Presented as doctrinal teaching, not personal opinion
- Used prophetic authority: "I can tell our beloved brother Christians..."
- Made specific prophetic claims about timing of priesthood restriction

**Source rebuttal:**
If prophets can teach false doctrine from the pulpit for decades without correction, what use is prophetic authority? Young himself taught that the prophet cannot lead the church astray (JoD 9:289). The LDS church sustained these teachings for 119 years (1849-1978).

### Defense #2: "It was a product of his time - everyone was racist then"
**Why it fails:**
- Abolitionist movement was strong in 1859; many Americans opposed slavery
- Even other Christian denominations of the era didn't claim physical features were divine curses
- The specific theological framework (Cain's curse = flat nose + black skin) was uniquely LDS
- Most racism of the era was social/cultural, not claimed as divine revelation
- Young explicitly dismisses abolitionists and claims divine authority for racial hierarchy

**Source rebuttal:**
The defense actually proves the problem: if prophets merely reflect cultural prejudices rather than receiving revelation from God, then prophetic authority is meaningless. Young claimed this was God's decree that "Abolitionists cannot help" - not a personal opinion shaped by his era.

### Defense #3: "The priesthood ban is over now, so this doesn't matter"
**Why it fails:**
- The ban lasted 119 years, affecting millions of Black Latter-day Saints
- The 1978 change came with no apology or explanation
- The church still hasn't fully addressed why God would command racism
- Young's prophetic timeline ("last ones") was proven false
- The theological framework used to justify the ban (curses, unworthiness in pre-mortal life) was used for over a century
- Generational trauma and ongoing effects persist

**Source rebuttal:**
If this sermon represents a century+ of prophetic error, it raises fundamental questions about prophetic reliability. What other current teachings might be cultural prejudice dressed as revelation? The 2013 essay says past leaders were wrong but doesn't explain how they could authoritatively teach false doctrine for so long.

### Defense #4: "The 2013 essay disavowed these teachings - the church has corrected itself"
**Why it fails:**
- The essay disavows theories but calls them "theories," not acknowledging they were taught as doctrine
- No apology was offered to Black members or their ancestors
- The essay is not signed by church leaders and carries less authority than the original teachings
- The essay doesn't explain HOW prophets taught false doctrine as revelation for over a century
- Many members remain unaware of the essay; racist folk doctrine persists
- The church hasn't addressed the failed prophecy aspect

**Source rebuttal:**
The essay's existence proves the criticism's validity. But it creates new problems: if latter-day prophets can authoritatively teach fundamental errors about God's nature and human worth for 119 years, the entire claim to prophetic authority is undermined. True correction would require institutional repentance, not just a quiet essay.

### Defense #5: "You're taking it out of context"
**Why it fails:**
- The full context makes it worse, not better
- Reading the entire sermon shows the racial teaching embedded in a broader theological framework
- The surrounding text includes internal contradictions (one blood vs. cursed bloodlines)
- Context shows this was systematic theology, not an offhand remark
- Young spends significant time on this topic, indicating its importance to his message

**Source rebuttal:**
Here's the full context: a sermon on intelligence that teaches (1) all humans are created from one blood, then (2) some races are "uncouth, un-comely, disagreeable" with "flat nose and black skin" as divine curses, and (3) they must wait until "the last ones" to receive priesthood. The context reveals a self-contradictory, racist theology taught as divine truth.

### Defense #6: "Scientists now say all humans came from Africa, so we're all Black - this vindicates the teaching"
**Why it fails:**
- This is a desperate misreading of both Young's sermon and modern genetics
- Young specifically describes Black people as "uncouth" and cursed - not the ancestors of all humanity
- The sermon teaches Cain's descendants bear curses; genetics shows no such cursed lineage exists
- Modern science contradicts the entire Curse of Cain narrative
- This defense ignores the moral problem: the teaching was used to deny priesthood and demean Black people

**Source rebuttal:**
Modern genetics has falsified the Curse of Cain theory entirely. There is no genetic "mark" linking any people group to Cain, no genetic basis for priesthood worthiness, and no scientific support for the idea that physical features represent divine curses. The sermon's racial theology is scientifically, morally, and doctrinally bankrupt.

## Song Potential Assessment

### Rating: ★★★★★

This is absolutely prime material for the Journal of Discords project.

### Justification

**Strengths:**
1. **Shocking specificity**: "flat nose and black skin" as the mark of Cain is viscerally specific and damning
2. **Multiple angles**: Combines moral failure, false prophecy, disavowal, and self-contradiction
3. **Official disavowal**: The 2013 essay provides clear institutional admission of error
4. **Quotability**: Highly memorable phrases that are devastating in their clarity
5. **Failed prophecy**: The "last ones" timeline is demonstrably false
6. **Self-contradiction**: The sermon contradicts itself internally (one blood vs. cursed races)
7. **Contemporary relevance**: Racial justice remains urgent; this shows religious roots of racism
8. **Impossible to defend**: Apologetic attempts only highlight the problem

**Why it works musically:**
- Strong contrast between lofty claims about intelligence/truth and racist pseudoscience
- Ironic potential in having Young condemn himself with his own words
- The phrase "flat nose and black skin" is rhythmically catchy (unfortunately)
- Multiple movements possible: intelligence → contradictions → racism → failed prophecy → disavowal

### Hook Possibilities

1. **"Flat Nose and Black Skin"** - Direct, memorable, shocking
2. **"The Last Ones"** - Ironic focus on the failed prophecy
3. **"One Blood, Many Curses"** - Highlights the internal contradiction

5. **"Un-comely, Disagreeable"** - Focuses on the dehumanizing language
6. **"Until That Curse Is Removed"** - Prophetic failure angle

### Structural Ideas

**Option A: Chronological Journey**
- Verse 1: Young's lofty claims about gathering all truth
- Verse 2: "One blood" statement setting up false hope
- Verse 3: The racist revelation (flat nose, black skin)
- Verse 4: The "last ones" prophecy

**Option B: Ironic Contrast**
- Chorus: Young's own words about truth/error as recurring refrain

- Build to climax with the racial teaching
- End with the disavowal

**Option C: The Prophet's Voice**
- Sung from Brigham Young's perspective as if teaching the doctrine
- Let his own words condemn without commentary
- Musical irony carries the weight
- Final verse: the 2013 essay speaking back to him

**Option D: Call and Response**
- Call: Young's authoritative declarations
- Response: Modern disavowals and contradictions
- Build tension between prophetic certainty and institutional repudiation

### Challenges

1. **Sensitivity**: This material deals with real harm to real people. The song must honor victims of racist theology while critiquing the teaching. It must punch up at power, not down at those harmed.

2. **Complexity**: The sermon covers many topics. The song must maintain focus while representing the key problems.

3. **Tone**: Must be devastating without being gleeful. The harm was real and ongoing.

4. **Balance**: Need to make the moral problem clear while also documenting the failed prophecy and institutional disavowal.

5. **Length**: There's so much material that condensing into a song format will be difficult.

6. **Apologetic resistance**: This topic triggers strong defensive reactions. The song must be bulletproof.

**Solutions:**
- Focus primary narrative on the "flat nose and black skin" quote and "last ones" prophecy
- Use the "one blood" quote as ironic counterpoint
- Include the 2013 essay disavowal as the climax
- Keep tone somber and serious, not satirical
- Let Young's words do the work; minimize editorial commentary
- Address top 2-3 apologetics in liner notes, not song lyrics
